# Production monitor and incident response

For [AIR-349](https://trello.com/c/VUyampoh) and [ADR 0095](../adr/0095-cloudflare-native-production-monitor.md). The monitor only diagnoses and reports; it never recovers anything. Every activation step below needs the owner's separate approval.

## What it is and is not

A tick on the existing hourly cron that reads the stored status of EPG, Discovery, Ratings and the prediction queue, judges it, keeps `monitor/state.json` and sends actionable alerts to the one channel the owner selected: a generic HTTPS webhook or the Telegram Bot API. It is independent of ChatGPT. It is **not** an availability check: a Worker cannot see its own total outage or a Cloudflare-wide outage. That needs an external dead-man service pinged by `MONITOR_HEARTBEAT_URL`.

## Activation order (each needs an owner GO)

1. Choose the channel and the dead-man service. For the generic webhook set `MONITOR_WEBHOOK_URL`; for Telegram ([ADR 0097](../adr/0097-monitor-telegram-bot-api-transport.md)) set `MONITOR_TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` and `MONITOR_TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID` and then `MONITOR_TRANSPORT=telegram`. Optionally set `MONITOR_HEARTBEAT_URL`. The bot token must be a Worker secret and, like `MONITOR_WEBHOOK_URL`, is never in git, GitHub, Trello or logs. `MONITOR_TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID` is a destination identifier held as a plain Worker variable (it is visible in the Cloudflare Dashboard and is still kept out of logs and public reports; see ADR 0097 decision 3); `GET /api/monitor/status` shows `transport` and `alerting` without them.
2. Set `MONITOR=on`. Read `GET /api/monitor/status`: one healthy snapshot with a fresh `heartbeatAt`. There is no observe-only mode: `MONITOR` is `off` or `on`, and while the selected channel is not configured `on` keeps the snapshot and sends nothing.
3. Send one synthetic message with `POST /api/monitor/test` (only with `MONITOR=on`; `off` and any other value refuse it without a write or a request), confirm the owner received it, and that a second call inside ten minutes is refused.
4. Confirm the dead-man service sees the pings and alerts when they stop (disable the pings briefly only with the owner's approval).

Roll back by setting `MONITOR` to `off` or removing it. Alerts already sent stay sent.

## When an alert arrives

Read `GET /api/monitor/status` first (a pure read), then the domain's own status, and decide. A manual action needs its own action-specific owner approval; the monitor never takes it.

| Issue key | Read | Meaning and next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `epg_stale`, `epg_failed` | MCP `epg_status`, `epg_pipeline_status` | No generation published since the Madrid 17:00 due time. See the deployment runbook |
| (no alert) EPG `health: late` | MCP `epg_status`: `orchestration.seal` (`cursorItems` of `itemTotal`, `updatedAt`) and `orchestration.publication.overdueMs` | The publication is more than 15 min past due and not waiting or cooling down. A cursor that moves between two reads is a slow seal, not a stopped one: read the `epg_phase` exit events (`writeMs / batches` is the D1 round trip) before anything else, and do not step or retry the run by hand. See the [AIR-420 evidence](../evidence/d1-migration/air-420-seal-ledger-round-trips-2026-11-10.md) |
| `epg_authority` | the published EPG authority marker | Held or unreadable: owner |
| `discovery_stalled`, `discovery_executor_stalled`, `discovery_generation_mismatch` | `epg_pipeline_status` with `view: "operational"` (the detailed view can answer `discovery_fence` while Discovery commits); its `queue` block is the classification the Monitor judged | Owed work (`queue.state` `backlog`, `running`, `failed` or `blocked`) without allowed progress. Check the executor mode and the Workflow before any resolver call. A `queue.state: empty` (`drained` or `complete`) is never a stall, whatever `discovery.nextCatchUpAt` the continuation still carries. AIR-687: `queue.usefulProgressAt` / `queue.usefulProgressSince` (the Workflow's `lastUsefulProgressAt` / `usefulProgressTrackedSince`, same programme run only) are the progress clock of a tracked series. A fresh saved error, `discovery_fence` collision or backoff checkpoint, a fresh `checkpointAt` / `lastChunkAt` and a future gate are not progress: three hours (the existing Discovery threshold) without a chunk that really moved the queue (checked incl. the cheap pass, touched, added, assigned or replayed slot, revalidated decision) raise or keep `discovery_stalled` for a zero-work loop (`queue.state: failed` or `queue.fenceCollisions` > 0), even while its gate is in the future; a legitimate deferred wait (cooldown, budget backoff) stays `waiting` inside its gate. An open incident recovers only on useful work newer than its baseline (`metrics.lastProgressAt` of its last stalled observation, else when it was established), a proven empty queue or the EPG pause; the first zero-work checkpoint of a newly deployed Worker only starts tracking and leaves the incident open (`metrics.held: progress_unconfirmed`). A record written before AIR-687 has no such fields and keeps the checkpoint and chunk clocks. `lastProgress.usefulProgress` and `fenceCollisions` in the executor readout say which kind the last chunk was. `fenceCollisions` counts consecutive fence re-evaluations (waits start at 30 s, double and stop at five minutes; there is no hour tier); the continuation's `fenceCause` and `fenceBasis` say what the refusal looked like and what the authoritative state said (`state_confirmed`, `state_unreadable`, `pointer_unconfirmed`, `lease_lost`, `authority_not_ready`, `authority_held`, `authority_loading`; a fail-closed `error` carries `run_superseded` or `other_programme`). A long counted loop is a persistent refusal, not a transient conflict: read `fenceBasis` and the step output before anything else. A Workflow waiting as `generation_unpublished` means the published pointer is missing or not yet published for this run: nothing is written meanwhile; a pointer naming another run ends the series as `stale_generation` |
| `discovery_unconfirmed` | the same operational read: `queue.reason`, `queue.admission` and `unavailable` | Neither work nor its absence is proven for 3 hours of the state's own clock (another generation's continuation, a loading or unreadable authority, a missing or foreign status, partial counts, an all-zero reading of another hop, an unreadable admission gate). Find which fact is missing; do not run or retry Discovery to "prove" it. A Discovery alert whose text says `Инцидент остаётся открытым` is held: the incident is not over, the Monitor only cannot confirm or clear it now |
| User site 502 on `/api/catalog/available` or `/api/enrichment/view` with `route_error` `discovery_fence` | those two GETs plus operational Discovery status | Unexpected: those user reads must not use the ledger generation fence. A 503 with `code` on the same paths is a held, loading or pin-change refusal; it is not an empty collection |
| User site 503 on `/api/programme`, `/api/enrichment/view`, `/api/catalog/available`, `/api/enrichment/footer/status` | Workers Observability: the requests' `outcome`, wall time and version; the `user_read_refused` line of the same request | Outcome `exceededMemory` with the same wall time on several of them: one isolate held them all and ran out of memory (AIR-586, [RCA](../evidence/user-read-oom-rca-2026-11-23.md)). Check whether the page still asks them one after the other and whether `discovery_catalog_read` of `programme`, `view` and `catalog/available` says `projected: true`; measure with `scripts/footer-memory-profile.mjs` (`CATALOG=<n>`, `--paths`, `--sequential`) before any change. Outcome `ok`: the Worker refused; `user_read_refused` names the code (a Discovery user-read refusal, the Film/Person reader, the Watchlist authority, the owner-review read or `notReady`). Neither is a reason to run a writer, a lease reset or a sync |
| `discovery_quarantined` | the Workflow readout | The retry budget is exhausted: owner (`discovery_executor_invalid` was removed with the selector, AIR-325) |
| `ratings_stalled`, `ratings_quarantined`, `ratings_failed_permanent`, `ratings_switch_open` | `ratings_sync_status` (`orchestration`) | See the Ratings integration reference; clearing a quarantine is an owner action |
| `predictions_no_progress` | `prediction_queue_overview`, `enrichment_status` (last successful run) | Eligible predictions and no confirmed progress for 60 minutes, or at least three ownerless starts in two hours after 20 minutes (heartbeats, `already_running`, empty wake-ups and a run that holds the lease at the tick do not count, [ADR 0210](../adr/0210-prediction-no-progress-verdict-survives-live-runs.md)). The message gives the last confirmed progress and the ownerless starts; a tick that cannot read the queue keeps the incident open, and the one recovery says whether progress is confirmed or nothing is eligible. The Worker cannot tell why: check the four ChatGPT Scheduled Tasks, their authorization (an expired MCP authorization produces no request at all) and the model quota. Do not start a run by hand from this alert |
| `outbox` growing in `/api/monitor/status` | `outboxOldestAt`, the channel provider (webhook service or Telegram) | Notifications are queued because the channel fails. They are kept and retried with the same id (at most five attempts per tick, two when the channel is down); fix the channel (owner), nothing is lost |
| `*_resource_limit` | Workers Observability | Repeated Worker 1102 or memory limits |
| `<stream>_unreadable` | the named status | A source could not be read; confirm whether the authority marker is held or loading |

`waiting` and `delayed` never alert: a backlog inside its backoff, an EPG within two hours of its due time and a Ratings cooldown are by design.

Since [ADR 0206](../adr/0206-monitor-four-states-one-reminder-interval.md) (AIR-592) every alert is `degraded`; there is no `needs_owner`. A message marked `critical` (Telegram `🚨 Сбой` or `🔴 Критичный сбой`, webhook `critical: true`) is a proven hard failure (a quarantine, a held authority, a failed poster run, a `blocked` Producer stop) and arrived on the first snapshot; an ordinary one was confirmed first. Both remind at most every 6 hours after the last delivered message, and an ordinary issue that turns critical sends one `ESCALATION`. A `needs_owner` still shown in an old Telegram history or a status read before the first tick after deployment means the same as critical `degraded`. Acknowledgement and snooze do not exist; the owner fixes the cause and the issue recovers on its own.

Reading a Telegram message (content per ADR 0206 decision 9; the exact layout is kept in the reference, ADR 0212). The message is a few short blocks separated by empty lines:
- **Header:** the kind, the subsystem, the problem and its severity.
- **Statistics.**
- **The domain's stall:** `⏱ Без …`, with what it is counted from on the next line (the last confirmed progress, the allowed step, or for the Producer a reference that may be the publication).
- **The monitor's own incident:** `🕒 Инцидент`, with `Открыт` and, on a recovery, `Снят`.
- **The overall state.**
- **A technical footer:** the time and `Ref`.

The stall and the incident differ on purpose: the monitor may notice a stall hours after the last progress. `✅ Проблема снята` means the monitor no longer sees that issue, not that the domain is proven healthy. Its stall lines are the last measured value (`Не меньше: …`, `Последнее наблюдение`), not proof that no progress happened since. A recovery with `Остаются другие проблемы` resolves only that issue: read `GET /api/monitor/status` for the rest. `📨 Отправлено с задержкой` means the channel was down or held; the durations still describe the moment of detection. `Ref` is the id to quote when comparing with the webhook or the stored outbox. Block-by-block table: [monitor reference](../reference/api/monitor.md#telegram-text).

## Cost, retention and privacy

When `on`, one tick is a handful of small R2 reads, one conditional R2 write and at most the alerts and one ping; when `off` it does nothing. The state document is one small object overwritten each tick. Messages and state hold no titles, ratings, credentials or private records.

## Telegram delivery problems

| `lastDeliveryFailure.reason` | Meaning and next step |
| --- | --- |
| `config_invalid` | A token or chat id is missing or malformed (status shows `alerting: false`); nothing was sent and the queue is kept. Owner: set a valid token (secret) or chat id |
| `http_401`, `http_403` | The token is wrong or revoked, or the bot was removed from the chat. Owner: fix the bot or the secret |
| `telegram_not_ok`, `http_400` | Telegram refused the request, for example a wrong chat id. Owner: check the chat binding |
| `rate_limited` | Telegram answered 429; `holdUntil` shows when sending resumes. Nothing is lost |
| `timeout`, `network`, `http_5xx`, `malformed_response` | Telegram or the network is failing; messages are kept and retried (two attempts per tick while it fails) |

A growing `outbox` with a `lastDeliveryFailure` means alerts are not reaching the owner even though the monitor is alive: the dead-man heartbeat will not show this. Telegram cannot deduplicate, so after a Worker crash the owner may see one message twice (same `Ref`). Switch back to the webhook by removing `MONITOR_TRANSPORT`.

## Attributing D1 load to callers from the Worker logs (AIR-579)

Two structured log lines let a read of the Worker logs attribute D1 load to its callers without a per-request counter. Both carry **no** argument, key, film id, title, payload, identity or header, and both are best-effort (they never throw into the work and change no result). Workers Logs attach a line to the invocation that wrote it, so an aggregate **by `$metadata.trigger`** (a route such as `GET /api/enrichment/view`, a cron expression, `POST /mcp`) names the caller.

- `{"event":"mcp_tool_call","tool":"<registered name or unregistered>","outcome":"ok|tool_error|rejected|failed","ms":<n>}`: one line per authorized MCP `tools/call` ([MCP reference](../reference/api/mcp.md#call-log)).
- `{"event":"discovery_catalog_read","rows":<n>,"ms":<n>,"projected":<bool or null>}`: one line per **resolved whole-catalog read** of `discovery_admission` (`readCatalog`, the 12 k-row statement of the RCA); the bounded reads by id or by TMDB id write none. `projected` is `null` when the read was refused as over the cap (`discovery_bounded`), where the caller's options are not looked at. The lines grouped by `$metadata.trigger` give the **observed** whole-catalog reads per caller in a window.

**Limits of this signal (state them with any result).** The line is written only after the D1 read resolved and on a best-effort basis, so a failed D1 read, an interrupted invocation, a failing log sink or dropped or sampled telemetry leaves **no line**: the count of lines is a lower bound of the executions, not an exhaustive count. `rows` is the rows **returned**, not the D1 billed `rows_read`; `ms` is the application wall time awaiting the query, not the SQL engine time. **Reconcile the count with the D1 analytics count of the same statement for exactly the same window and report any gap** as missing attribution; never present the line count alone as the number of reads.

A third line serves the user reads of the page (AIR-586): `{"event":"user_read_refused","path":"<one of the four user GETs>","status":503,"code","reason","error","notReady","retryAfter"}`, one per 503 the Worker answers itself on `/api/programme`, `/api/enrichment/view`, `/api/catalog/available` or `/api/enrichment/footer/status`. Each code field is the body's value only when it has the shape of an internal code, else `null`; `notReady` is the programme's own first-update answer. An isolate that ran out of memory writes no such line (its 503 is Cloudflare's, outcome `exceededMemory`).

Use them read-only through the Cloudflare Workers Observability aggregate (counts and the trigger; never raw events with identity), compare a window with the D1 analytics signature of the same statement, and remember a request is not a statement: one invocation may read the catalog several times, and a cron tick or a Workflow step shows as its own trigger.

## D1 projector grouped write path

> **Historical (AIR-537 slice 3, 19 Nov 2026).** The D1 enrichment projector, its runner and its write arms were removed; this section describes fields and log lines that are no longer produced. Nothing here is an operating check.

This section describes historical projector summaries that can still exist in `projection_run`. Since AIR-690 `GET /api/d1/status` no longer returns them ([D1 status contract](../reference/api/d1-catalog.md#d1-status-contract)); read the table only through an authorized read when the history itself is needed, and never as runtime health. The production projector and its `D1_ENRICHMENT_BULK` selector are retired ([ADR 0155](../adr/0155-retire-enrichment-projector-cron.md), AIR-512), so the live Worker cannot create a new grouped run. In the offline historical harness, a grouped run carries a `bulk` object; a per-entity run does not. The fields are whole numbers, fixed names and one short error sample: no payload, id, key or SQL.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `groups`, `filmGroups`, `personGroups`, `groupSize` | Group cycles of the run, by lane, and the configured group size |
| `applied`, `fallbacks`, `fallbackReasons` | Groups written by their one batch, and groups sent to the per-entity path by reason (`guard_refused`, `prefetch_miss`, `undigestable_read`, `plan_error`, `too_large`, `constraint`, `batch_error`, `digest_unusable`) |
| `guardRefusals`, `constraintFallbacks`, `prefetchMisses` | Groups the in-batch assertions refused, groups a constraint rolled back, reads the collecting pass issued that the trace had not |
| `prefetchReads`, `guards`, `prefetchStatements`, `statements` | Traced reads, assertions in the write batches, statements of the read batches and of the write batches |
| `prefetchMs`, `applyMs` | Time in the read batches and in the write batches |
| `films`, `people`, `perWindowSecond` | Films and Persons the groups wrote and the rate over the seconds since the budget clock started (the window grant, or the run start) |
| `stop`, `errors` | Why the grouped path ended before the run did: `batch_error`, `fallback_rate`, `budget` (no group fits the clock or the statement budget left), or the reason a run was stopped (`lease_lost`, `window_lost`, `authority_moved`, `authority_unavailable`); up to five error samples |

**`bulkBudget`** (present only with the flag on): `wallMs` the write-phase budget this run used (180,000 with a held window and the marker, 60,000 otherwise), `policy` (`l1`, `l1_unavailable`: the flag without a window or marker, or `explicit`: a caller's own value) and `effectiveMs`, the budget after the window's absolute expiry (the smaller of the two, counted from the grant). A run that ends `budget` with `bulk.stop: budget` and `policy: l1` spent its 180 s, or what the window left of it, minus the reserve; the same outcome with `policy: l1_unavailable` means no window was held.

How to read it. The healthy picture is `fallbacks` 0 and `prefetchMisses` 0, `guards` equal to `prefetchReads`, and `statements` around 95 per Film. Stop conditions during the first observed runs after activation (each needs the owner and a decision brief, never a retry loop): a fallback share above 5 % of `groups`; any `constraint` fallback whose error names a unique or foreign key violation that the per-entity path does not also report; a dangling credit (`film_person_credit` rows with no `person`); an outcome `deferred` with `authority_moved` or `authority_unavailable` (the Film/Person marker moved, is absent or left `r2` and `prepared`: the projector must not write, and the marker is the owner's); a D1 error that names a batch or statement size (`batch_error`, check `errors`); a window that ran past its absolute deadline; throughput below half of the offline model. The run itself latches the grouped path off for the rest of the run at a fallback share above 5 % after ten groups, or an unknown batch outcome, and then continues per entity (each of those writes still asserts the marker). Switching the flag off restores the per-entity path on the next run and undoes no row.

## D1 projector run: phase timing (AIR-392)

> **Historical (AIR-537 slice 3, 19 Nov 2026).** The D1 enrichment projector, its runner and its write arms were removed; this section describes fields and log lines that are no longer produced. Nothing here is an operating check.

**Historical (AIR-537 slice 3): the projector runner was removed and the `d1_enrichment_projection` line is no longer emitted; the hourly cron never ran it after [ADR 0155](../adr/0155-retire-enrichment-projector-cron.md). Do not alert on it.** The D1 enrichment projector ([ADR 0056](../adr/0056-d1-enrichment-write-through.md), [ADR 0103](../adr/0103-projector-exclusive-window.md)) logged one `event: d1_enrichment_projection` line per hourly run and keeps the same summary in `projection_run.summary_json` (also `GET /api/d1/status`, `projector.lastRun.summary`). Since [AIR-392](https://trello.com/c/ivMqVpKC) the summary carries three additive measurement groups. They only read the clock around calls the run already makes; no run, fence, budget, retry or write depends on them. They hold fixed names and whole numbers only: no payload, id, key, SQL or parameter. All times are milliseconds on the Worker's `Date.now()`, which advances only across I/O: pure CPU work between two calls shows as 0 ms and lands in the I/O phase that follows it, so a CPU-local share cannot be separated by these fields.

**`timing`: the run in order.** `leaseMs` (taking the projector lease), `windowMs` (ADR 0103 request to grant, only when a window was taken; the existing `window.waitMs` is the part spent waiting for quiet), `diffMs` (load, view and diff, see `phases`), `diffResidualMs` (the named remainder of the diff, see below), `writeGateMs` (the pre-write authority check), `writeMs` (the write loop), `windowReleaseMs`, `leaseReleaseMs` and `runMs` (log line only: they are measured after `summary_json` is stored). `leaseMs`, `windowMs`, `diffMs`, `writeGateMs` and `writeMs` are consecutive and add up to at most `runMs`.

The first write is measured on the budget clock's start (the grant when a window is held, otherwise the run start), at two boundaries, and each is absent when the run never reached it: `preTxnMs` to the moment the first fenced transaction is **entered** (its planning starts), `preWriteMs` to the moment the first batch is **submitted** to D1 (the instant before `binding.batch`, so it is an attempt, kept when that batch fails, not a confirmation of success), and `firstBatchPlanMs` = `preWriteMs - preTxnMs`, the repository's own planning (its reads and statement assembly) before that submission; `firstBatchStatements` is the size of that first batch.

**`phases`: a sequential partition of the diff.** Entries do not overlap, so they add up. Each is `{ms, n, q, rows}`: wall time, calls, D1 adapter queries and rows read.

| Name | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| `store.writersIdle` | The writer-idle checks (two R2 reads each), before and after the reads |
| `store.publishedSnapshot` | The published EPG generation read, before and again after the reads |
| `store.handoffGuard` | The Discovery authority handoff guard |
| `store.readBlock` | The whole block of R2 and discovery reads (they run together, see `detail`) |
| `store.ownerReview` | The owner-review authority and its fenced read |
| `store.etagRecheck` | The repeated etag check of every object read (`rows` = objects) |
| `store.build` | Building the store (CPU) |
| `diff.schemaContract`, `diff.metadataCache`, `diff.predictionAuthority` | The D1 reads of the schema version, the metadata cache and the prediction authority |
| `diff.lifecycle`, `diff.ownerOverlay`, `diff.inputs` | Building the view and the entity inputs (CPU) |
| `diff.keys` | The fresh change keys of every entity of the view, computed once (CPU), when the view has at least 5,000 entities |
| `diff.checkpointCount` | One `COUNT(*)` of `projection_state` (the summary's `stored`) and the backlog check: a view with far more entities than the table has rows reads the table whole |
| `diff.checkpointDigest` | One D1 query that returns, per bucket (kind and a hash of the last four UTF-8 bytes of the id, at most 8,192 buckets), the count and two 32-bit sums of the stored keys' hash part; `rows` = buckets. Present when the buckets can be used |
| `diff.checkpointRows` | The rows of only the buckets that differ from the fresh keys, in statements of up to 90 buckets, at most six at a time; `rows` = rows read |
| `diff.storedRead` | The previous read of every checkpoint (`rows` = checkpoints; since AIR-395 interim paged: a table of up to 6,000 rows is one query, a larger one a first page, a count and the other pages together, six at a time, accepted only when the rows add up to the count with no duplicate, otherwise read whole). It runs for a view below 5,000 entities, when more than 40 % of the buckets differ, when the view has far more entities than the table has rows (a backlog), and when a key is not of the expected shape |
| `diff.plan`, `diff.admittedFirst` | The change keys and the plan (CPU) |
| `diff.membersRead`, `diff.syncProbe`, `diff.storedSyncRead`, `diff.knownFilmsRead`, `diff.aliasesRead` | The remaining D1 reads of the diff, each with its row count |

`diffMs` = the sum of `phases` + `diffResidualMs` exactly (the residual is the untimed glue and CPU, never negative; it is emitted for every run whose diff was attempted, including `failed`, `deferred` and `dry`). `detail` is not part of this equation. `loadMs` (the `store.*` part) and `viewMs` keep their old meaning.

**`detail`: overlapping reads.** `read.watchlist`, `read.programme`, `read.metadata`, `read.titles`, `read.people`, `read.catalog`, `read.resolution`, `read.diary`, `read.retirements`, `read.viewIndex`, `read.verifiedFilms`, `read.ownerMirror` (only under the `r2` owner-review authority): the time of each individual read inside `store.readBlock`. They run concurrently, so their sum exceeds the block's wall time; never add `detail` to `phases`. `read.catalog` and `read.resolution` are routed through the Discovery authority (D1 under authority `d1`).

How to read a run: `preWriteMs` is the overhead before the first write; it is `preTxnMs` (the diff, the write gate and the glue before the first transaction) plus `firstBatchPlanMs`. With a window the wait is in `windowMs`, not in it (the budget clock restarts at the grant). In a slow run find the largest `phases` entry first, and compare it with `diffResidualMs`; if it is `store.readBlock`, the largest `detail` read names the object; if it is a `diff.*Read`, compare its `rows` and `q` with its `ms`; if `firstBatchPlanMs` is large, the repository planning before the first submission is the overhead. Judge several runs, fast and slow, never one. An entry that is missing means the run ended before it (`failed`, `deferred`, `dry`).

**Retained duplicates (AIR-405).** `retainedCount` and `retainedFilms` (at most 20 entries of `id`, `reason` `duplicate_film_retained` and `held`, the prediction-domain tables that hold rows of the Film the link would retire) appear in the run summary only when a Letterboxd Film was skipped on purpose because its catalog twin holds prediction, fingerprint or candidate facts. It is not an error: it does not raise `errorCount`, does not make the run `partial` and is not counted in `writtenFilms`. The Film has no checkpoint, so every run plans it again (a few queries each) and a changed input or the facts going away lets it through. A foreign key error of any other table, `identity_collision` and an EPG history refusal are still errors in `errors`.

**D1 meta per phase (AIR-396).** Every `phases` and `detail` entry whose reads carried a D1 `meta` also holds `sqlUs` (the database's own `sql_duration_ms`, summed, in microseconds), `dbRows` (`rows_read`), `dbN` (reads with a meta) and `replica` (of those, served by a replica, so normally 0). `ms - sqlUs/1000` is the part of the phase that is not D1 SQL execution: queueing, transport, and CPU charged to it because the Worker clock does not advance during CPU. Entries without D1 reads (R2, CPU-only phases) and batches do not carry them. No SQL, parameter or id is recorded.

**Which checkpoint path a run took (AIR-395).** The summary field `checkpointMode` is `buckets` or `full`, and `checkpointBuckets` is `{expected, stored, differing}` (buckets the view has, buckets the table has, buckets that differ) `{expected, missing, skipped:'backlog'}`, or a `skipped` of `unsafe_id` (a fresh id that is not well-formed UTF-16) or `bucket_mismatch` (a digest or row whose bucket the Worker does not recognize: the database and the Worker disagree about the mapping). Both read the whole table, and `bucket_mismatch` is not expected: a run that reports it needs the ids involved found and the mapping checked, not a retry loop. `buckets` means the stored keys were summarized in D1 and only the differing buckets were read; the plan, the writes and the change keys are exactly those of reading every checkpoint. While a backlog is large (the view has far more entities than the table has rows, as on 2026-11-09) a run reads the whole table as before and `diff.storedRead` is present; the buckets are expected to be used once the backlog has drained.


**The boundary line `d1_enrichment_phase`.** A run that reaches the write also logs one earlier line, `event: d1_enrichment_phase`, `stage: diff_complete_write_start`, immediately before the write starts and after the diff, the write gate and the write budget are settled. It carries `projection`, `trigger`, `mode`, `startedAt`, `timing` (`leaseMs`, `windowMs` when a window was taken, `diffMs`, `writeGateMs`), the view and diff counts (`films`, `people`, `stored`, `changedFilms`, `changedPeople`), the size of the plan (`plan.films`, `plan.people`, `plan.sync`, `plan.absent`), the D1 `queries` so far, `bulk` and `bulkBudget`. It has no id, title or payload, reads no clock and sends no query. Use it when the platform ends a run without its summary line (a Workers outcome of `exceededMemory` or a time limit): a boundary line with the run's `startedAt` shows the run completed its diff, so the end came in the write phase or later; no boundary line shows nothing by itself, because a log line can be lost, and is not proof that the end came earlier. A dry run, a run the input guard refuses and a run that fails before the write log no boundary line. The boundary line does not replace the summary line and does not enter `summary_json`.

## EPG Workflow attempt correlation (AIR-462)

Use when an EPG Workflow invocation ends `exceededMemory` or a step disappears, to place the death between two confirmed points. Read-only: Workers Logs of the Worker `iptv` and the Workflow history `iptv-epg/instances/<instanceId>`; never run, retry or step EPG to produce evidence (see [read side effects](../reference/api/read-side-effects.md) and the [log skill](../../.claude/skills/cloudflare-observability-read/SKILL.md)). Vocabulary: [orchestration reliability](../reference/orchestration-reliability.md).

1. From the Workflow history take the `instanceId` and the `act-N` step whose attempt ended on memory, with its start and end time.
2. Filter `epg_act` lines by `instanceId` and `actStep`. Each execution of the callback has its own `attemptTraceId`; an attempt with an `enter` and no `exit` or `error` is the one that did not return.
3. Take the `epg_phase` and `epg_memory_boundary` lines with that `attemptTraceId` in time order. The last one is the last confirmed point; the paired boundary or phase whose `enter` has no matching `exit` or `error` is where the attempt was when logging stopped. Milestones (`*_begin`, `seal_ledger_batches`, `seal_stage_archive_segments`) are one-sided by design and never an unmatched enter or exit: use only the pairs listed in the [pairing rule](../reference/orchestration-reliability.md#epg-correlation-and-memory-boundary-markers-air-462). An `epg_act` `error` means the action rejected (the Workflow then slept `act_failed`); it carries no error text.
4. Interpret with care. The trace id is local, not Cloudflare's. The last line is not the instruction that killed the attempt: a line written just before death can be lost, and a long synchronous `JSON.parse` or `JSON.stringify` shows as a gap after an `enter`. Byte counts are allocation proxies, not heap figures. Logs are kept about 7 days and may be truncated: if an `act-N` has the platform attempt but no `epg_act` line, say so and stop; do not infer a position.
4a. `WorkflowInternalError` (AIR-522): when the attempt ends `Attempt failed due to internal workflows error` and not on memory, compare the callback's `epg_act exit` time with the attempt end in the Workflow history and look for the `epg_act_stored` line of the same `actStep`. That line is a best-effort log written outside `step.do`: a present line shows only that `step.do` returned to this execution (and may repeat on replay), an absent line is inconclusive on its own. The pattern "exit logged, platform failure afterwards, no stored line" is a lead, not proof of what failed inside the platform. Before concluding anything about persistence or repeated work, read back the durable state (control record, `sync.json`) and take the Workflow attempt history as the authority. Record the gap, the instance id and the version id for the owner; no application-side remedy for this boundary is proven, and a Cloudflare support case is an owner decision ([RCA](../evidence/orchestration/air-522-workflow-internal-error-rca-2026-11-17.md)). An attempt with an `enter` and no `exit` is a different case (step 2).
5. Record several natural daily runs as dated evidence with observed, inferred and unknown, exact UTC and Madrid times and the deployed SHA. One run without `exceededMemory` does not close AIR-420 or show a memory fix.

## EPG seal hand-off stuck in `starting` (AIR-520)

Symptom in `epg_status`: `stage=seal`, `phase=starting`, `handoff.successorPending=true`, `owner.liveness=absent`, `published=null`. Since AIR-520 the replayed old instance creates the claimed successor by itself, and the hourly `ensure` remains the fallback; a read-only check is enough:

1. Read `epg_status` twice, at least one cron period apart. `failure.code=create_uncertain` with a `cooldownUntil` means a recorded, bounded retry; no failure and no progress across two ticks is a new defect to report.
2. Compare with the Workflow GET of `epg_<day>_<series>` (read only). Do not create, terminate or retry an instance and do not run EPG steps by hand without a separate operation-specific GO.
3. Evidence and mechanism: [AIR-520](../evidence/orchestration/air-520-orphan-handoff-claim-rca-2026-11-17.md).

## EPG `complete` that still shows an `error` (AIR-525)

Symptom in `epg_status`: `stage=complete` with a non-empty `error` and `priorAttemptError=true`. The error belongs to an earlier attempt of the same `runId` (or to a tail that threw after the run was saved complete); it is not the current state of the run, and it is not a reason to start EPG again. Read only:

1. Today's publication is done when `orchestration.runKey` is today and `orchestration.published` names the run (control `doneAt` set), or, without a control `doneAt`, when `sync.json` has a valid completion receipt (`completedAt`) and the D1 published pointer names the same `runId`; then `orchestration.health` is `healthy`. The same proof is required with or without an `error`. The executor finishes such a control itself on its next action (`lastStep.note=recovered_complete`), after at most one `/api/sync/epg` step on the same run for an owed event or Ratings hand-over, and never with a second `/api/sync/start`.
2. `failure.code=evidence_contradictory` on a complete run means the proof did not hold (no or malformed receipt, the pointer names another or no run, or the run's frozen day is malformed or contradicts its `startedAt`): the domain is quarantined for the owner and no run is started. `failure.code=d1_unavailable` means the authority read failed and the next action checks again after the cooldown.
3. Do not clear the error, reset the run, start a sync or retry an instance by hand without a separate operation-specific GO. Contract: [orchestration reliability](../reference/orchestration-reliability.md) (AIR-525 paragraph); evidence: [AIR-525](../evidence/orchestration/air-525-recovered-complete-error.md).

## EPG-priority pause: `awaiting_complete` and the old-Worker signature (AIR-539, [ADR 0175](../adr/0175-epg-priority-holds-until-run-complete.md))

Read-only checks; none of them authorizes a manual EPG or Discovery run, a lease reset or an override.

- **`awaiting_complete`.** With migration `0026` applied, Discovery is refused as `epg_priority` with detail `awaiting_complete` from the pointer switch until the run is `stage=complete` and certified. It is a soft wait, not a failure (no counter, no 502). Duration = the post-pointer tail. If it exceeds the usual `finalize` time, check the EPG health (`late`, `stalled`, quarantine) first.
- **Certificate owed.** `sync.json` `stage=complete` while `published_epg_authority.finalized_run_id` is not equal to `programme_run_id`: the hourly `ensure` repairs it (`certificate` appears in the `ensure` result when it ran, waited for a live step lease, found a proven debt, `owed: true` with `ageMs` since the durable completion receipt, or could not read its evidence, `unreadable: true`; an idle tick stays quiet. A growing `ageMs` means Discovery is held: repeated `d1_unavailable`/`readback_mismatch` is the signal to look at D1 and the step lease). Repeated `step_lease_held` means a live EPG step holds the lease; the repair retries next tick.
- **Old-Worker signature.** Pointer published, pause row `clear`, `finalized_run_id` different from `programme_run_id`: a Worker without the certificate code lifted the pause at the pointer. Redeploy the current code; the repair certifies the run. Rollback to such a Worker is an owner decision.
- **Busy telemetry.** `seal_busy` phase lines carry `busyKind`, `holderKind` (`epg`, `discovery`, `projector`), `holdExpiry`, `scheduledWaitMs`, `sealPhase`, so an EPG-held lease is told apart from a Discovery-held one. `scheduledWaitMs` is the planned retry delay, not the measured time spent blocked; do not read it as observed contention latency. `seal_certify` carries `outcome` (`certified`, `owed`, `absent`, `skipped`).
- **Events journal during the pause ([AIR-595](https://trello.com/c/dPjrNIbQ)).** An hourly Discovery card «Discovery приостановлен на время обновления программы» (badge «Предупреждение») is a `report_failed` notice with `reason=epg_priority`: the hourly close was refused by the pause, the accumulator is untouched and the next report after the pause carries the deltas (its `from` reaches back to the last closed hour). Workers Logs show `discovery_hour_close_failed` with `reason=epg_priority`. It is not a fault and needs no action, unless the card also says the queue state could not be read (`fault=queue_unreadable`, an independent read failure of that close) or names a failed or stalled Discovery state, which keep their usual meaning. A notice the journal refused waits in `events/discovery-report-faults-v2.json` (pause notices) or `events/discovery-report-faults.json` (the older reasons); both drain on later firings, and a code rollback evicts neither. «Отчёт за час не удалось сформировать» with `reason=write_conflict` outside the pause is a genuine lost compare-and-swap; repeated hours of it are worth a look at the executor record.
- **`scanOutcome`** on the completed `sync.json` (`applied`, `facts_only`, `skipped_incomplete_watchlist`, `skipped_no_observation`) says whether the retention scan was applied; the certificate does not.

## Canonical Producer: `canonical_producer_*` alerts (AIR-572, [ADR 0141](../adr/0141-producer-workflow-chain-protocol.md))

Read `GET /api/monitor/status` first (stream `canonical_producer`, `facts.status`), then the Producer's own stored rows with a bounded read-only query of `canonical_producer_chain` (by `programme_run_id`) and `canonical_producer_run` (by `source_generation`). The monitor only reports; every action below needs its own owner approval.

| Status / key | Meaning | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `held_by_delivery` | The static delivery hold is on. Lifted in code by AIR-572 after the ADR 0203 membership switch, so this appears only if the hold is re-imposed by a code change. | None. Do not treat it as success: no canonical facts are being written. |
| `stalled` / `canonical_producer_no_progress` | No proven run progress (finished units or a moved cursor of the same pass) for more than 2 h; restarts, new passes and generation rollovers with zero finished units do not count. A running Workflow whose instances all error (for example the former `RPC receiver does not implement the method "bind"`, `step_count` 0) shows exactly this: chain `pending` or `active`, takeovers growing, no run row. | Read the Workflow instance errors through the Cloudflare read path. Do not reset a lease or create an instance by hand. A code fix is a PR. |
| `failed` | Recoverable stop (`transient`, `receipt`, `authority`); the hourly reconcile retries by its own backoff. `producer_work_replayed` (transient) means a `work` step ran again for the same epoch (a step retry or a replayed instance): it ran no work and stopped, and the reconcile relaunches the chain into a new epoch. | Wait for the next hourly ticks; escalate if the same code repeats. |
| `blocked` / `canonical_producer_failed` | Identity code or `terminal` / `fail_closed` / `exhausted`. `producer_identity_unresolved` (a Letterboxd lid with neither a trusted claim nor an existing Film), `producer_identity_ambiguous` (an untrusted or competing claim), `producer_identity_redirect_chain`, `producer_identity_bounded` (more than 1000 lids), `producer_identity_unreadable`. | Owner decision. The fix is a trusted claim or an admission through the existing owner paths; the Producer never creates a Film from a spelling and never guesses from title or year. |
| `blocked` with `producer_budget_infeasible` or `producer_chain_epoch_cap` | `producer_budget_infeasible`: an instance made no durable progress because its fixed part plus one admitted operation and the end reserve do not fit the headroom. A large plan only hands over, since the AIR-572 budget fix. `producer_chain_epoch_cap`: the chain of one programme run used all `CHAIN_EPOCH_CAP` instances (re-publications of the same run count on). Both are `fail_closed`: this input is not relaunched, and the next published EPG run starts a new chain. | Owner decision. Read the Workflow instance steps (read only) and the [budget section](../reference/data/producer-driver.md#instance-budget-inside-the-workflow-chain-adr-0141-d5-air-572); do not reset or reopen the chain by hand. |
| `canonical_producer_unreadable` | A bounded D1 read failed. | Check D1 health first; the streak is kept, not reset. |

Recovery is automatic and uses the existing outbox (one recovery message per delivered alert). A held or stopped Producer does not stop EPG publication or any other canonical writer.

The status footer row «Обогащение каталога» (AIR-593) shows the same `facts.status` on the same facts, computed at the moment of the page read from the clock the last Monitor tick stored: a footer `сбой` is `failed` or `blocked`, «нет подтверждённого прогресса» is `stalled`, «приостановлено при поставке» is the hold, «статус не прочитан» is `unreadable`. The footer dates progress by the run row's own `updated_at`, never by the page read, so reloading the page cannot renew the 2 h window. «нет данных · движение не подтверждено: монитор давно не обновлял отметку прогресса» (`unconfirmed`) means the Producer looks like it is still moving but the Monitor has not stored its clock for more than 90 min: check `GET /api/monitor/status` (`heartbeatAt`) and the Monitor before anything else. The counts are Producer units, not films. The footer is a read; nothing it shows starts, resets or retries the Producer.

**Events Log reports (AIR-594, [ADR 0208](../adr/0208-producer-outcome-events-delivery-store.md)).** The «События» screen shows the same outcomes to the owner under «Обогащение каталога» (`type: canonical_producer`): a completed pass, a recorded stop (its code, class and whether it was eligible for the automatic retry at the stop), a `held` report when that retry did not happen, and a pass cut by a newer publication. Nothing is reported until migration `0038` is applied (a separate owner GO); `facts.outcomeDelivery.active` says whether it is. Each outcome is a row of `canonical_producer_outcome`, written in the same D1 batch as the transition and delivered to `events/items/` by the hourly reconcile, so a report appears up to an hour after the outcome, dated by the outcome itself. To check a missing report, read (read-only) `facts.outcomeDelivery` (`pending`, `oldestPendingAt`, `readable`) and, with a bounded query, the row by id in `canonical_producer_outcome` (`state`, `attempts`, `next_attempt_at`). A pending row is retried every hour without limit and never dropped; it is not a Producer failure. The log lines are `canonical_producer_outcomes` and `canonical_producer_held_failed`. Do not insert, update or delete outcome rows, write `events/items/` by hand, or run the Producer or the reconcile to produce a report; a report of an outcome from before activation is never produced (no backfill).
