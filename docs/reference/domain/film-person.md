## Where the model runs (do not confuse with source of truth)

`DOMAIN_CONTRACT_VERSION=1` describes pure lifecycle projections and guarded transitions. `film-person.js` has no HTTP/R2 effects and is not the deployed authoritative Film/Person storage adapter. `film-person-reader.js` constructs views from records already loaded by a caller; `film-person-backfill.js` computes desired lifecycle sidecars and a **dry-run** reconciliation; it does not write production storage. Diagnostics and offline parity are views/measurements, not cutover evidence. Between [ADR 0056](../../adr/0056-d1-enrichment-write-through.md) and AIR-537 slice 3 the Worker bundle carried the reader in its own scope for the D1 enrichment projector only; that projector and the bundle entry were removed, so the reader now serves the offline import, diagnostics and seed scripts. `entitiesOnly` returns the Films and People without sidecars or legacy parity. The reader's per-object lookup indexes return what its linear scans returned.

The canonical D1 authority is **live** at `open` generation 2 ([ADR 0099](../../adr/0099-canonical-film-person-d1-authority.md), [evidence](../../evidence/d1-migration/air-499-film-person-open-2026-11-16.md)): migration `0013` and later Film/Person schema are applied (production schema **23**); `writerRoute` sends canonical writes to the fenced adapters and the enrichment projector that was compare-only for this domain was removed in AIR-537 slice 3; frozen R2 `titles.json` / `people.json` are not a fallback. **Activated call sites at `open`:** `catalogReaderGate` and the freeze readers (programme, watchlist, catalog, admin, footer/export); Discovery `upsertAdmissions`; `metadataSync` (`canonical-metadata-sync-producer`); rated admission/retention (`commitVerified`, `commitBaselineChunk`, `persistScan`); Producer Workflow from hourly `EpgOrchestration.ensure` when `PRODUCER_WORKFLOW` is bound. A first natural Producer / daily EPG pass at `open` is not yet recorded as accepted. Offline baseline/capture tools remain inactive. **Historical:** until that flip, the projector was the only canonical writer and R2 was the authority.

- **Proof (all of it, read by the planner, never supplied by the caller).** Both authorities are `d1`/`open` and neither generation moves during the read. The authoritative Ratings link: the facts link index maps `letterboxd:<lid>` and `tmdb:<n>` to the **same fact key**, whatever its form (`tmdb-<n>`, or `lb-<lid>` when a Diary observation created the record first and the movie alias was added to it later), no other alias carries that key, and the fact record under that key states the same lid and movie, is neither a redirect nor mid-migration, and carries no inferred provenance (AIR-559, [ADR 0104](../../adr/0104-inferred-letterboxd-identity.md): a unique original title and year is not proof of the lid). Two marks are read, both written only by the baseline import for an `inferred` item: the record-level `identity` (a record that first received its lid from that import) and the pair-level `inferredPairs`, an append-only list with one entry `{kind, basis, sourceRef, letterboxd, tmdb}` per exact pair an inferred item attempted or linked on the record. The import writes the entry of its pair on the target record and reads it back **before** it links the alias (and adds it again in the compare-and-swap that fills the record's ids); it skips only a record that already states exactly that pair without an entry, never replaces or removes an entry, and refuses the link when the list is unreadable or full (8 entries). A recorded `identity`, or an entry naming this pair (also among entries of other pairs), is `not_proved` / `ratings_link_inferred`; an unknown kind or an unreadable list or entry fails closed as `ratings_link_unverified`; entries naming only other pairs (attempts whose link failed or conflicted) do not concern this link. **Limits:** a pair the import linked before AIR-559 on a record that already held facts and its lid carries neither mark and is not detected by the planner; and a mark is never lifted by a later independent verification of the same pair (that claim needs a case-specific owner decision). The `ratings_view` projection is corroboration only (it can keep an alias that a case-specific repair removed from the link index): a row of either alias that carries another key is a conflict, a missing row is neither a conflict nor proof. Canonically: exactly one trusted, non-conflicting `tmdb` claim for `<n>`, its Film exists, is not a redirect source, `<n>` is not retired, and `canonical_identity` is null or exactly `tmdb:<n>`; no other Film claims the lid in any form, and the Film has no other `letterboxd` claim, no second `tmdb` claim and no untrusted or conflicting claim of either scheme.
- **Already claimed.** A Film that holds the lid as its one trusted claim is `pass` / `already` only when the rest of the proof still holds: nothing is written, no receipt, no revision. A stored claim whose proof is gone (for example a link that is inferred) is reported `refused` with that reason and is left in place; nothing removes it.
- **Write-time fence.** The proof that lives in D1 is asserted again inside the fenced batch (`guards` of `mutateFilm`): ratings generation (every ratings write bumps it, which covers link documents and a hold), retirement, any claim of the lid on another Film, an untrusted claim of the movie, that the Film still owns exactly one trusted `tmdb` claim for it, its other claims and its identity. A receipt retry re-plans from the current stores (`replanLetterboxdClaim`); a source that no longer proves the claim ends the receipt as a moved source.
- **Readback.** An applied receipt is not claim evidence. A write is reported `written` only when a fresh plan finds the trusted claim stored; an applied receipt whose claim is not stored is `unknown` (`readback_missing_claim`) and restores nothing.
- Batches are bounded to 12 and refuse duplicates; the unit never creates a Film and never matches by title, year or slug.
- **Backfill operator.** The cohort of published lids that still lack a claim is written by the owner-only MCP tool `letterboxd_claim_backfill` (`src/letterboxd-claim-backfill.js`), which derives the cohort on the server from the published run and the authoritative Ratings link index, judges every pair with this planner, and writes through this writer only when the owner's pins match a fresh derivation ([runbook](../../runbooks/letterboxd-claim-backfill.md), [MCP contract](../api/mcp.md)).
- **Producer wiring and the activation gate.** The module is bundled into the Worker with its unit. The in-code constant `LETTERBOXD_CLAIM_ACTIVATED` is `true` from the AIR-559 activation PR on, merged after the owner's explicit GO; while it is `false`, `ensureLetterboxdClaims` does nothing and stores nothing. The gate is the constant `true` (an ordinary reviewed line, not a Cloudflare variable or flag). Two Ratings flows offer it the pairs whose Ratings link they have just made durable. The Diary identity step offers a verified bridge, an existing trusted bridge and a repaired `link_failed` link (since AIR-559 not an id that is merely already linked). The baseline import offers **verified** entries only, after the chunk receipt is committed (also on a replay of a committed chunk); an **inferred** link is never offered: a unique title and year is not proof of the lid, and since [ADR 0192](../../adr/0192-retire-view-index-selector-and-raw-object.md) no unlink can withdraw such a link (a mistaken one is repaired case by case). The planner also refuses a marked inferred pair whichever flow offers it. The Diary step offers **only pairs it verified itself** (a verified page check, an existing trusted bridge, a confirmed `link_failed` repair) and later only their own `claim_pending` retries for the same movie; a lid that the link index alone ties to a movie is not offered, because the index does not say how the pair was made (an unmarked legacy inferred pair would otherwise pass). Each call site runs after the durable steps, behind a `typeof` guard and a `try`, so the rating, the Diary facts, the import receipt and the Event never wait for or fail because of the claim; the outcome is a diagnostic counter (`complete`, `incomplete`, `skipped`) and no Event.
- **Budget.** One call attempts at most 12 pairs (`LETTERBOXD_CLAIM_UNIT_CAP`) out of at most 200 offered (`LETTERBOXD_CLAIM_OFFER_MAX`) with one batched ordering read (lids no trusted claim holds yet go first; the read is never evidence). Every attempted pair, held or not, gets the full read-only proof, so `already` is only ever the proof's decision. The D1 statement count does not grow with the offer; every cut-off pair is `overflow` and the lids that may still lack a claim are returned as `unattempted`. The event of a pair is stable (`link:<lid>:<tmdb>`), never a timestamp.
- **Recovery.** While active, a pair the Diary step verified and linked is **handed off** to the claim without losing its durable intent (AIR-559, review of PR #966): in the same compare-and-swap that removes the link intent it becomes a `claim_pending` entry for that exact pair (attempts 0, due now), written before the claim is requested and removed only after the claim is proved (written or already). A link intent of the step for the same movie (`linking` or `link_failed`) whose alias and record committed before the step was cut off is repaired and handed off the same way; an intent for another movie, or any other prior reason, is cleared and never offered. So a kill or a lost write anywhere between the bridge and the claim leaves either the link intent or the pending claim. An id whose claim is not proved (refused, unknown, deferred, failed, or not attempted for budget or cap) keeps that bounded `claim_pending` entry (with its movie id, attempts and next attempt) in the Diary identity document. **Receipt operation key (AIR-600, card R1 of [ADR 0207](../../adr/0207-letterboxd-admissions-coordinated-in-d1-only.md) decision 3, owner choice 0207-U1 = K2; dormant).** A receipt may carry an `operation_key` that names the **item** an attempt works on (`admission:<kind>:<code>` for an admission, `evidence:<evidence_key>` for the evidence-only operation of [ADR 0213](../../adr/0213-film-identity-evidence-ledger.md)), so a retry finds every earlier attempt of that item whatever its request id, target or digest. `src/receipt-operation-key.js` holds the protocol: read every receipt of the key and its history fingerprint in one statement; wait on a live `pending` attempt; abandon every other unresolved attempt (`superseded_by_retry`, fenced on request id, state, attempt and key, so its original tail can never land); validate each applied attempt against **current** state through the caller's check (with the Film target followed through merge redirects), never on the receipt alone; then reserve a new attempt, or finalize a terminal outcome that reserves nothing, atomically against the observed fingerprint, so the loser of a race writes nothing and replans. A keyed attempt is owned by this protocol alone: the legacy receipt reconciler never selects or replays one. A key holds at most 100 receipts; a reservation that would exceed that is refused before any write (`exhausted`), while recovery and terminal outcomes at the cap still work. It is the shared foundation of the admission coordination (A1) and the catalog enrichment producer (C1); no caller uses it yet, it is not bundled, and while the column is absent every keyed call refuses before any write (`receipt_operation_key_missing`). Schema, indexes and triggers: [D1 schema](../data/d1-schema.md#receipt-operation-key-schema-v39-adr-0207-decision-3-in-the-repository-not-applied).

**Letterboxd admission coordination (AIR-602, card A1 of [ADR 0207](../../adr/0207-letterboxd-admissions-coordinated-in-d1-only.md), with [ADR 0217](../../adr/0217-source-independent-film-admission.md); dormant until the marker `letterboxd_admission_authority` reads `d1`, a separate owner activation).** The marker ([schema 46](../data/d1-schema.md#letterboxd-admission-authority-schema-v46-adr-0207-decision-1-accepted-air-602-in-the-repository-not-applied)) decides who coordinates the admission of a verified Letterboxd Film. - **One key.** `admissionOperationKey` gives `admission:lid:<lid>` to every attempt of any path that knows the lid ([ADR 0217](../../adr/0217-source-independent-film-admission.md) single-key rule); the older kind keys exist only for an item whose lid is not known. - **Reconcile, then reserve** (the AIR-600 protocol): a live attempt waits; every other unresolved attempt is abandoned first, so its tail can never land; an applied attempt is validated against the current admission row (never trusted alone); the pair is then classified: `present` (writes nothing; a genuinely new check records its evidence row through the evidence-only operation), `other_code` (another short code holds the Film), `conflict` (the code is held by another Film), `held` or `redirect` (non-terminal), `source_changed` (the reacquired source names another movie than the writer's durable record, checked before the pair is classified) or `admit`, which reserves a keyed receipt and runs the d1 writer in the same receipted batch.
- **The d1 writer** is `admitRatedFilm` at `d1`: keep-first (an existing row for the same movie and code is a no-op whatever the archive, title or year of the new input), the typed conflicts `other_code_on_film` and `code_on_other_film`, `verified_at` = the admitting batch, the evidence row appended to the same batch, and the marker asserted at the generation read.
- **Recovery and fencing (review of PR #1018).** The coordinator is called **first** and reconciles the key before any source is touched: `reacquire` (the real bounded source check) runs only when the history is clear and the item still needs a check. The writer's own durable intent (a Diary `linking` entry, or the `intent` an earlier not-now outcome carried; a rated row's `intendedTmdb`) is never overwritten by the movie of a new check: an applied attempt that matches the intent finishes the item **without a fetch and without fresh evidence**; a live attempt makes it wait (the intent untouched); a check that names another movie is `source_changed` (needs review); a source that cannot be reacquired is `source_unavailable` (transport keeps its place, a 404 costs one attempt, the sixth makes a rated row `needs_review`). A completion after a genuine check is **proven**: the evidence-only operation runs under its own key `evidence:<evidence_key>` (redirect-aware, a lost response settles from the ledger) and a failed, unavailable or in-flight evidence write is `evidence_unrecorded`: the item is not linked or marked verified, and the evidence to record is kept in the writer's durable record (`pendingEvidence`) for a retry that needs no fetch. An applied attempt that matches the intent settles the item without a fetch only if no evidence is owed: a genuine check sets the boolean obligation `evidenceDue` in the writer's own durable record (the Diary `linking` entry, the rated `admitting` row) BEFORE its first effect and clears it when the evidence is proven recorded, so a check that died before its evidence was written, or an admission that belongs to another route, is never settled from the intent: the source is really reacquired and its evidence recorded (the obligation is a flag, not a proof payload, and the evidence ledger is never read to decide it, ADR 0213 decision 8), and a trusted-bridge shortcut never replaces or bypasses an unresolved intent or evidence obligation. A terminal outcome (`other_code`, `conflict`, `source_changed`, and an answer that costs an attempt such as a 404, the sixth included) is written through `finalize` with the history guard of the history it observed: the Diary entry is written in the **same D1 batch** as that assertion (a refusal replans), and the Watchlist ledger row settles with the same history guard in its own batch (a competing attempt between the two refuses the settlement), while the writers whose state lives outside D1 (the rated candidate document, the inferred chunk receipt) re-check the guard immediately before their own write and treat a moved history as non-terminal; the window between that check and the write is their documented limit.
- **Callers.** The Diary and Watchlist check maps the typed outcome onto its pending reasons (`other_code`, `admission_conflict` and `source_changed` are definite; a live attempt, a held identity and a deferred attempt are not-now); the baseline import plans the pair before the link, admits after it (`refused_after_link` is terminal: no fact, the inferred-marked link stays) and records the outcome per entry; the rated-archive step answers `needs_review` `duplicate_tmdb` / `historical_alias_conflict` or stays `admitting`, ends at the verified admission and the tmdb-only rating, and reads the owned movie ids from D1. The Person-side canonical state writers are prepared the same way, **dormant** ([AIR-374](https://trello.com/c/ha7ais3w) P2b, `src/canonical-person-state.js`, accepted owner decisions 1 and 5 of [ADR 0117](../../adr/0117-film-person-d1-handoff-owner-decisions.md)). `recordPersonState` writes, in ONE fenced batch (marker `d1`/`open` at the read generation plus the Person revision, one revision bump), the `person_dimension` rows (identity and the effective localization) and what the projector's `noteHeldLocalization` does for a Person: the alias and the `localization_conflict` row of a held incoming value. `recordLocalizationConflicts` writes the `localization_conflict` rows of one Film or Person outside a merge; ids are the projector's content ids, so a repeat is a no-op, and nothing here deletes a conflict (the projector clears a Film's conflicts only when it retires the Film, which is the fenced `mergeFilms`). ## Identity and canonical records

- Letterboxd watchlist films retain their `lid` as the existing stored ID. A discovered, verified TMDB movie is usually stored as `tmdb_<tmdbId>`; if the TMDB id is already owned by a watchlist entry, that entry is reused. Neither storage ID nor discovery observation key (`normalizedTitle|year`) should be confused with the canonical TMDB identity (`tmdb:<id>`).
- `localFilmId()` mints `film:<letterboxd lid>`, `film:tmdb:<id>`, or a sorted `film:conflict:<ids>` for conflicting TMDB claims without Letterboxd. A previously assigned `localId` is preserved; it is not recomputed merely because new evidence appears.
- `canonicalFilmId()` is the one rule from a stored spelling back to the canonical id: `tmdb_<n>` → `film:tmdb:<n>`, a Letterboxd `lid` → `film:<lid>`, a `film:` id unchanged, an empty value `null`. It never mints: a result that names no stored Film is left unresolved by its caller. The offline importer loads this function from the domain source rather than keeping a copy.
- `externalIds` carry scheme, value, trusted/conflicting flags and provenance. Two different verified TMDB identities or distinct Letterboxd identities are collisions, **not** automatic merges. `canonicalIdentity` becomes `tmdb:<id>` only after identity is resolved against trusted evidence; an accepted score or search hit alone does not promote it.
- **Identity evidence is not identity** ([ADR 0213](../../adr/0213-film-identity-evidence-ledger.md), Accepted; dormant): the canonical decision stays the trusted, non-conflicting `film_external_id` row. Evidence for it is a separate, append-only ledger of typed propositions (`film_identity_evidence`, schema v41, [D1 schema](../data/d1-schema.md#film-identity-evidence-ledger-schema-v41-adr-0213-accepted-not-applied)): a lid or a short code identifies a Letterboxd entity, a title-and-year search links a lid to a movie only as a guess (`restricts`, never proof), and a page check proves that a Letterboxd entity and a TMDB movie are the same work (`asserts same_work` by `page_check`). Rows that share a source root (the Diary and the Watchlist caller checking one page) are one source, not independent corroboration. The migration is not applied, no writer calls the helper before card A1 of [ADR 0207](../../adr/0207-letterboxd-admissions-coordinated-in-d1-only.md), and nothing reads the ledger to decide anything.
- **Identity evidence writer control** ([ADR 0215](../../adr/0215-identity-evidence-full-model.md), Accepted; card EC, [AIR-637](https://trello.com/c/t35hqplw); dormant, migration 0042 not applied): each new evidence writer has its own durable control row (`off` / `pending` / `on`, generation, coverage start); **no row means `off`**, and a writer inserts only while `pending` or `on`. Coverage is claimed only from a drained `pending -> on` flip, and a rollback to a version without the writer is recorded as a break with the writer set `off`. See the [D1 schema](../data/d1-schema.md#identity-evidence-writer-control-schema-v42-adr-0215-accepted-not-applied) and the [activation runbook](../../runbooks/identity-evidence-writer-activation.md). - **Discovery decision identity evidence** ([ADR 0215](../../adr/0215-identity-evidence-full-model.md) Table 3, Accepted; card E2a, [AIR-617](https://trello.com/c/IfAZDYDj); dormant): the Discovery decision write (`writeDecisionDelta` on the chunk path, `writeDecisions` on the document path) appends one unbound evidence row to its own batch when it records a **new decision hash** that is `verified` with `identitySource` `tmdb_detail` (method `tmdb_detail_check`), `known_epg_alias` or `known_alias_year_runtime` (method `known_alias`, variant in `source_scope`): `candidate_for(discovery_observation:<key>, tmdb_movie|tmdb_tv:<id>)`, `asserts`, route `discovery_scan`, root = the observation key, observation time = the decision's `checkedAt`. ## Film page URL identity (AIR-387)

The film page URL is a presentation of identity, not a new identity. `/film/tmdb_<n>` is the permanent URL of a TMDB movie and `/film/<letterboxd id>` is its alias; both open the same page. A watchlist film uses the TMDB URL only when its record proves a movie (`tmdbLink` resolved to a `/movie/` page of the same number as `tmdbId`) and no other loaded film proves the same number; otherwise its Letterboxd id stays the URL. A catalog-only film's URL is `tmdb_<n>` for its verified `tmdb:<n>` admission. Titles, years, a TMDB redirect on letterboxd.com and a TV id of the same number are never identity. The URL changes no storage id, fact, rating, prediction key, canonical Film/Person record or provenance; see [UI and read models](../../architecture/ui-and-read-models.md#one-permanent-film-url-air-387) and the [catalog API](../api/catalog.md#permanent-catalog-film-api-catalog-film-tmdbid). A canonical Film admitted by the verified rated archive resolves on the same permanent URL even when Discovery never admitted it (AIR-409, a read-only fallback to one key of `letterboxd/admissions/verified-films.json`, only while the authority marker names R2 and bounded by the owner's retirements and D1 contradictions; never in `open`, `held` or an unreadable marker; see the [catalog API](../api/catalog.md#permanent-catalog-film-apicatalogfilmtmdbid)).

## Letterboxd slug alias

A Letterboxd film slug is kept as its own `externalIds` entry: `{scheme: 'letterboxd_slug', value, trusted: false, conflicting, provenance}`. It is separate from the lid (`scheme: letterboxd`), from TMDB ids and from title aliases.

- **Source.** `letterboxdSlugFromUrl` accepts only `https://letterboxd.com/film/<slug>/` and `https://letterboxd.com/<member>/film/<slug>/`: same origin, no query, fragment or credentials, lowercased. A title, a lid, a TMDB URL or Wikidata never yields a slug.
- **Attached only to a Film with a lid:**
  - `current`: the Film's own permalink, when every stored record of the lid that carries one agrees. The records are the programme, the watchlist, `metadata.json` and the record itself. Provenance: `source: letterboxd_film_url`, `records` (which of them carry it), canonical `url`, `observedAt`.
  - `ambiguous`: the same records carry different permalinks, such as a rename seen by one record and not yet by another. Every slug is kept with its `records`, and none is current, because no record is proved fresher.
  - `observed`: a slug from a Letterboxd Diary entry with the same lid (`source: letterboxd_diary`, `entry`), when the caller passes `store.diaryHistory`. An alias stamped by the diary writer keeps its own `firstObservedAt` as `observedAt`. An older alias without one has `observedAt: null` and `observedBetween {from, to}`, the entry's first and latest read; no exact time is claimed. One slug in several entries of the lid: `from` is the earliest lower bound (unknown if any is unknown), `to` the earliest upper bound, and `observedAt` is exact only when the two are equal;
  - `historical`: a slug stored on the same Film by an earlier backfill that the Film no longer carries, so a renamed permalink still names it.

  A TMDB-only Film, and a diary row without a lid, get no slug.
- **Stable.** The backfill keeps each stored slug's stored time: an exact `observedAt`, otherwise its stored `observedBetween`. A replay with the stored sidecars is therefore unchanged, even after later diary reads.
- **Collision.** One slug on two Films marks the entry `conflicting` on both and is reported as `aliasConflicts` by `readLifecycle` and in the backfill report. Nothing is merged, moved or re-rated.
- **Unchanged by the alias:** `id`, `canonicalIdentity`, the lid, TMDB trust, readiness, fingerprint, prediction and the Film count.
- **Storage.** The alias is part of `externalIds`: the R2 lifecycle sidecars and the D1 `film_external_id` rows hold it without a schema change. `trusted: false` keeps it outside the unique index on trusted identities.
- **Inventory.** `scripts/letterboxd-slug-inventory.mjs --snapshot <dir>` counts the aliases a snapshot's stored records would yield. It makes no request and writes nothing; running it on a production export needs owner approval for that export.

## TMDB TV identity and retired movie ids

A miniseries that TMDB keeps as a TV entry is identified by `{scheme: 'tmdb_tv', value, trusted, conflicting, provenance}` ([ADR 0057](../../adr/0057-tmdb-tv-identity-and-retired-movie-ids.md), Accepted). The functions live in `src/domain/tmdb-tv-identity.js`, which is loaded beside this file. The Worker bundle loads it at top level: `parseFilm` keeps the typed link and the lifecycle reader applies the retirements object ([below](#runtime-projection)).

- **Separate namespaces.**
  - `tmdb` (movie) and `tmdb_tv` values are never compared.
  - `tmdbTvCollisions(films)` reports a trusted `tmdb_tv` held by two Films as a collision for owner review, and the same number as a movie id on another Film as a `numericOverlap` only.
  - `linkFilms` keeps refusing a Film whose `tmdb` value differs (`identity_collision`). It never links on a TV value.
- **Letterboxd link.**
  - `letterboxdTmdbLink(html)` returns the single visible `https://www.themoviedb.org/(movie|tv)/<id>` link, typed. Only an anchor's `href` counts, not a URL in text, a `<link>` or another element, with the page's `data-tmdb-type`/`data-tmdb-id` attributes.
  - The status is `resolved` only when exactly one link agrees with exactly one attribute pair.
  - The status is `review` for `link_disagrees_with_attributes`, `multiple_tmdb_links`, `contradictory_attributes` or `attributes_missing`.
  - With no link the status is `unresolved` (`no_tmdb_link`).
  - It never corrects a Film.
- **Retirement.**
  - `tmdbRetirement(entry)` validates an owner-confirmed retirement: `filmId` `film:<lid>`, `lid`, `movieId`, `tvId`, `evidence` and `confirmation` (`by: owner`, `at`, `source`).
  - The evidence must contain:
    - `movieStatus: 404` and `movieCheckedAt`;
    - the Letterboxd permalink in `letterboxdUrl`;
    - `link: tv/<tvId>` and `linkCheckedAt`;
    - `tvResponseId` equal to `tvId`, and `tvCheckedAt`.
  - Each missing or different piece is a named reason.
- **Applying it.**
  - `applyTmdbRetirement(film, retirement, at)` changes only that Film:
    - the `tmdb` entry becomes `trusted: false`, keeping its value; its provenance gets `status: retired`, `reason: tmdb_movie_404`, `replacement`, `evidence`, `confirmation` and `previous`;
    - a trusted `tmdb_tv` entry is added after it, with provenance `source: letterboxd_tmdb_link`;
    - `canonicalIdentity` becomes `tmdb_tv:<id>`;
    - the identity dimension stays `resolved`, with `sourceVersion` `tmdb_tv:<id>` and both ids in its provenance.
  - Everything else is unchanged.
  - The result depends on the Film and the retirement only, never on the time of the run (except the dimension's `updatedAt`), so any writer's correction reads back identically.
  - A Film carrying exactly the result is returned with `changed: false`. Exactly means all of these: the retired `tmdb` entry with its full provenance (including `previous`), the trusted `tmdb_tv` entry with its full provenance, `canonicalIdentity`, and the identity dimension's `sourceVersion` and both ids. Key order is ignored. Any other trace of a correction is `partial_correction`.
  - These states are refused, not repaired: `film_mismatch`, `lid_mismatch`, `identity_not_resolved`, `conflicting_tmdb`, `movie_id_mismatch`, `tv_id_mismatch`, `partial_correction`, `movie_not_trusted_identity`.
- **Storage.** The retirements are one owner-approved R2 object, `identity/tmdb-retirements.json`, applied by the projection ([below](#runtime-projection)). The records keep the movie id Letterboxd shows. D1 holds the untrusted retired `tmdb` row, the trusted `tmdb_tv` row and `canonical_identity` `tmdb_tv:<id>`; the unique index on trusted `(scheme, value)` separates the namespaces without a schema change.
- **Plan.** `scripts/tmdb-tv-identity-plan.mjs` plans exactly the four confirmed Films from the private AIR-286 artifact. It reads either D1 or a snapshot, writes nothing, and states each Film's expected D1 identity; `d1Readback` compares D1 with it ([runbook](../../runbooks/tmdb-tv-identity-correction.md)). D1 is written by the enrichment projector ([ADR 0056](../../adr/0056-d1-enrichment-write-through.md)), not by this tool.

### Runtime projection

- **The page link in the records.** `parseFilm` adds `tmdbLink`, the full `letterboxdTmdbLink(html)` result, to the record it returns. `tmdbId` is unchanged: it is still the first `data-tmdb-id` number, whatever the link or `data-tmdb-type` says.
  - The metadata refresh writes the record to `metadata.json` and to the sync run's watchlist. The completed sync writes the run's films to `watchlist.json`, and the EPG commit carries them into `programme.json`.
  - The field appears on a record only when the regular sync re-parses its page: a stale record (30 days) or a missing synopsis. A record written before has no field and stays valid. A fresh record keeps the field it has.
  - No Film view, readiness rule or change key reads `tmdbLink`, so the field alone changes nothing in D1.
- **The retirements object.** `readLifecycle(store, now, options)` applies `store.tmdbRetirements`, the body of `identity/tmdb-retirements.json`, only when it is not `null`.
  - Absent: the reader calls none of this and the view is exactly what it was. Nothing creates the object; it is written only by an owner-approved run ([runbook](../../runbooks/tmdb-tv-identity-correction.md)).
  - `tmdbRetirementSet(body)` checks the whole object first: `schemaVersion: 1`, a `retirements` array, every entry valid under `tmdbRetirement`, and no two entries naming the same Film, lid, movie id or TV id. One failure fails the object (`valid: false`, reasons such as `entry_0:owner_confirmation` or `entry_1:duplicate_tvId`), and no retirement of it applies.
  - `applyTmdbRetirements` runs after the watchlist and programme Films are built and before catalog records are linked, so a retired movie id, no longer trusted, links no catalog record. A retirement is **held**, and its Film stays as the records build it, when:
    - the Film is not in the view (`film_missing`);
    - a stored record of its lid (programme, watchlist, `metadata.json`) with a `tmdbLink` no longer shows the confirmed divergence, checked per record by `tmdbLinkChange`. That divergence is exactly one link, `tv/<tvId>`, with exactly one attribute pair, `movie`/`<movieId>`. Anything else is held:
      - no link, several links, another link or an unreadable field (`link_changed`);
      - attributes that are missing, several, of another type or another id (`attributes_changed`), including Letterboxd switching only the type, or type and id, to the TV entry;
    - `applyTmdbRetirement` refuses it (`movie_id_mismatch`, `lid_mismatch` and the other reasons above).
  - A record without a `tmdbLink` field (written before the field existed) is no evidence either way. The correction uses the identity dimension's own `updatedAt` as its time, so it is the same on every read.
  - `settleTmdbRetirements` checks the corrections against the whole view once catalog records are linked. A trusted `tmdb_tv` on a second Film (`tmdb_tv_collision`) or a second Film still trusting the retired movie id (`retired_movie_on_other_film`) undoes the correction and holds every Film involved.
  - The view then carries `tmdbRetirements: {valid, reasons, retirements, applied, held: [{filmId, reason}], numericOverlaps}`, with `entitiesOnly` too. It is absent when the object is absent. `numericOverlaps` (a TV id equal to a movie id on another Film) is reported only.
- **Consumers.** The corrected Film keeps its id, lid, slug alias, localization, fingerprint, prediction, history and redirects; `catalogReady` and `displayReady` still hold through the lid. Movie-only consumers read the records' `tmdbId`, which keeps the movie number, and never see `tmdb_tv`: EPG matching and discovery `knownTmdb`, catalog keys `tmdb_<n>`, posters, Wikidata P4947 and the model's TMDB URI (for a corrected Film still the retired movie URL, as before the correction). The retired movie ids answer `404`, so no TMDB movie can be verified under them. `recordsFor` adds catalog records only for a trusted `tmdb` id, so a `tmdb_tv` number never pulls the catalog record of an equal movie id into a Film's credits. The admin Film record (`src/admin/read.js`) builds its identity from the records and still shows the movie id; it reads, and writes nothing.
- **Offline tools.** `scripts/snapshot-store.mjs` loads the object into `store.tmdbRetirements` when a snapshot holds it (`null` otherwise), so the offline import, the projection seed and the lifecycle scripts derive the same corrected view as the Worker.

### Watchlist fact admission

[ADR 0086](../../adr/0086-durable-watchlist-film-retention.md) (Accepted). The reader still builds Films from the current programme list, or from the watchlist list when the programme list is absent, and then from discovered films. It also keeps a Letterboxd lid whose `watchlistAdmissions` value is a boolean. The projector fills that map from `enrichment/view-index.json`: a lid matching `^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,80}$` and not starting with `tmdb_`, and only when `watchlist` is `true` or `false`. A missing index admits nobody. In D1, `film_retention.observed_at` is the start of the current watchlist state: an unchanged daily scan writes no Film row and an exit or a re-add moves it; a single-row scan watermark (`film_retention_scan`, schema 25) is the ordering guard, so a late observation older than the newest admitted scan is stale for every Film. The watermark is the last confirmation; `observed_at` is not, and the same ordering is enforced by database triggers for any writer ([ADR 0173](../../adr/0173-watchlist-retention-observed-at-is-state-start.md), AIR-532). Rows that exist before migration `0025` inherit the old rule (every scan overwrote `observed_at`), so they hold the last pre-activation confirmation, not a recorded state start; no backfill is made, and the oldest-`observed_at` reading of the longest stay is valid only for transitions recorded after the apply. With the schema below 25 the producer rewrites `observed_at` every scan; at schema 25 without the table it fails closed.

The metadata cache is the body of an admitted lid only when the row's id is absent or equals the lid. A row whose id names another film is not that body, and the lid stays a Film. A lid with no usable cache row is still a Film and keeps the Letterboxd id. A `tmdb_<n>` key, a mood facet, a watched flag, a rating, a diary title, a fingerprint, a slug, or a title and year do not mint a Film. `watchlist: false` is a removal of membership. It is not `watched: true` and it does not place the film on the watchlist grid. The grid still reads the watchlist document. Re-adding the same lid returns one Film.

When that lid is on neither current list and has no usable cache, the enrichment writer does not replace an existing Film. It writes watchlist membership only. Credits, metadata, trusted external ids, identity and metadata dimensions, and sync clocks stay as stored. A Film that has no row yet is still inserted.

`reconcileDepartedFilms` classifies rows that are no longer on the current lists. It writes nothing. Join an existing Film by its lid, or by exactly one trusted movie id, and keep that id. Two trusted owners of one movie id, a lid whose trusted movie id disagrees with the Film that holds it, a `tmdb_tv` id together with a movie id, and two lids that share a slug are `conflict`. Two lids that share one unverified movie id are `ambiguous`. A lid with a watchlist boolean and no Film is `resolved` as a watchlist fact the reader already keeps. A lid or trusted movie id with neither, and a row with no id, stay `unresolved`.

### Rated archive admission

[ADR 0087](../../adr/0087-rated-archive-film-admission.md) (Accepted). [ADR 0090](../../adr/0090-rated-identity-redirect-proof-and-retry.md) (Accepted) lets a later identity-step prove the short code by the `boxd.it` hop when the HTML shortlink tag is absent, and returns a stored `shortlink_missing` review to pending only through an owner retry. A verified movie from a confirmed rated ZIP is `letterboxd/admissions/verified-films.json`: schema version 1, films keyed `tmdb_<n>`, each value `{tmdb, sourceCode, archiveSha256, title, year}` and no `watchlist` field. A later explicit continuation may also store synopsis, credits, runtime, genres and a provisional Russian title on that same value, and only when the fetched page still matches that short code, path and movie id. At Film/Person `open` no serving reader reads that object: the catalog reads `film_rated_admission` and the Film's own D1 metadata, so synopsis and credits stored on the entry do not reach the Film (gap G3 of the [admission audit](../../evidence/d1-migration/air-555-verified-films-admission-audit-2026-11-24.md#gap-and-recovery-analysis)). The rest of this paragraph describes the pre-`open` R2 reader, kept for the local stand-in only: the reader adds that key when the current programme, watchlist and discovered films do not already contain the same movie id. `listedOnWatchlist` stays false. A missing object admits nobody. An item that carries `watchlist`, a key that is not `tmdb_` plus its own `tmdb`, or a movie id already on another Film is dropped. When the only occupant of that movie id is this thin archive record and a discovered film of the same id is richer, the discovered film replaces the stub. A programme or watchlist film of that movie id is not replaced. The same verified movie on a later EPG airing stays one Film. The rating is a fact on that movie id and is not membership. Preview and the candidate queue do not admit a Film.

## Lifecycle dimensions and transition rules

`FILM_DIMENSIONS` defines **five independent state dimensions**; availability is a time-bound projection, **not** a sixth status. Every dimension state may hold `status`, `updatedAt`, `source`, `sourceVersion`, `provenance`, `lastError`, `failureReason`, `retryAt`, `attempts` and `nextAction`; localization alone also has `value`. These status envelopes do **not** contain entire fingerprint, metadata, or prediction payloads.

| Dimension | Status vocabulary | Conditions and consequences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | `unresolved / resolving / resolved / ambiguous / rejected` | Keep Letterboxd ID without inventing TMDB; `excluded_nonfilm` creates no Film. Historical/expired or unchanged terminal discovery is `wait_new_evidence`; future retry is `wait_retry`; only **eligible current** discovery can `continue_discovery`. Distinct verified IDs surface conflict. |
| Metadata | `missing / fetching / partial / fresh / stale / failed` | 30-day freshness threshold. Letterboxd metadata and TMDB-only metadata choose different next actions. A synopsis-only update that retains older credits is `partial`; `peopleNamesVersion: 2` means the name attempt finished, not that all names are reviewed. The decision clocks that are not columns of `film_metadata` are projected separately ([below](#metadata-sync-clocks)). |
| Localization | `missing / provisional / reviewed` | Verified dictionary/owner choice cannot be downgraded by Wikidata/cache; provisional transcription is **provisional** even when stored in a reviewed dictionary. Conflicting inputs remain aliases/provenance or `localizationConflicts`. |
| Fingerprint | `missing / generating / current / stale / failed` | Schema v1 and no `invalidatedAt` make the fingerprint current. Profile changes alone do **not** invalidate/recreate fingerprints. The reader and domain accept the fingerprint transition verdict per film (`fingerprintCompatible` on the store, [fingerprint compatibility](fingerprint-compatibility.md#readers-and-diagnostics)): a kept fingerprint of another schema version is current with its own version as provenance; an invalidated one never is. |
| Prediction | `missing / generating / current / stale / failed` | Current means fingerprint current **and** matching profile version/hash, fingerprint hash, schema and prompt version 1. Failures retain retries/backoff; profile changes make prediction stale, with `prediction_only` when fingerprint is still current. Every consumer compares the profile by strict version and hash equality; [ADR-0031](../../adr/0031-selective-prediction-profile-compatibility.md) defines the selective rule that replaces it once connected and activated; its evaluator and the one switch that moves every consumer to it are described in [prediction compatibility](prediction-compatibility.md); the lifecycle reader and domain accept its verdict per film (`predictionCompatible` on the store): a positive verdict keeps a prediction that names the usable fingerprint current over another profile or prediction-prompt version, with its own provenance; a negative one is stale even over an exact match. |

`transition()` rejects edges not permitted by the domain graph (for example, `resolved` identity cannot regress to `unresolved`, and reviewed localization cannot become provisional). `applyAutomaticLocalization()` cannot set `reviewed` and records attempted overrides; only `applyReviewedLocalization()` with `authority: dictionary` or `owner` can promote reviewed content. `observeMetadata()` does not rewrite fingerprint or prediction. `invalidatePredictionForProfile()` preserves fingerprint and chooses the appropriate next action.

## Metadata sync clocks

`metadataSync` still decides freshness from `metadata.json`. The clocks it branches on, and that `film_metadata.updated_at` folds together, are `metadataAt`, `synopsisFetchedAt`, `peopleNamesVersion` and `peopleLocalizedAt`. `peopleVersion` is already `film_metadata.people_version`. Reviewed titles and reviewed names stay on the localization dimension; `titles.json` and `people.json` feed that dimension and are not copied into the clock row.

D1 table `film_sync_state` ([ADR 0070](../../adr/0070-metadatasync-freshness-clocks.md)) stores those four clocks and nothing else. For a Film that already has a row, no sync row means `metadata.json` has no entry for that Film. A NULL clock means the entry exists and the clock is absent. The integer 2 is the only `people_names_version` that means the name attempt finished. The 30-day window is not stored. The enrichment projector writes the row from R2 after schema version 5, including after the Film has left the view while the cache entry remains. A cache key with no Film row does not create one. Until that migration is applied the table is absent and nothing reads it. No sync step and no page reads the row to choose an R2 write. The contract is the [D1 schema](../data/d1-schema.md#metadata-sync-clocks-schema-v5).

- `displayReady`: non-rejected identity, a resolved identity or a Letterboxd external ID, and a usable preferred title (reviewed/provisional, original or EPG). Missing translation, fingerprint or prediction must **not** hide a film.
- `catalogReady`: stable Letterboxd ID or trusted, non-conflicting TMDB ID **and** fresh or partial metadata.
- `predictionReady`: both fingerprint and prediction are `current`. Neither a valid catalog id nor a fresh profile alone suffices.
- `preferredTitle()` uses reviewed/localized value first, otherwise original, otherwise EPG. UI parity must still compare the **actual displayed title** in existing programme/watchlist read paths, not assume it equals the domain projection.

## Availability and published generations

`availabilityProjection()` computes `availableUntil` as the latest known show start plus **72 hours**; `hot` evaluates shows from **72 hours back** through **24 hours ahead** (upper bound exclusive). Shows and `epgLastAiring` are not stable Film attributes and never invalidate a fingerprint or a personal prediction. They also do not decide whether the film may be queued: an unsealed EPG generation does not remove eligibility ([ADR 0154](../../adr/0154-prediction-eligibility-independent-of-epg.md)).

An explicit `shows: []` is known **no availability**. When a published programme run is known, missing `shows` or a mismatched `showsProgrammeRunId` must not produce hot. `epgLastAiring` is a fallback only when no explicit shows, no shows programme run and no published run are known. A new published EPG generation atomically replaces the current show set; it does not append old availability. See [candidate discovery](candidate-discovery.md).

When `published_epg_authority.mode` is `d1` ([ADR 0079](../../adr/0079-d1-authoritative-published-epg.md), Accepted; production marker activated on 3 November 2026), availability is that generation's D1 document plus `published_epg_show`. `film_epg_last_airing` is not a write of this publisher. `programme.json` and `watchlist.json` are the compatibility mirror, not a second authority. An explicit empty show list is still a member and zero show rows. The publisher does not mint a Film for a programme id. `projectFilm` / `observedShows` take `shows` from the completed programme: a member in `films` or a catalog slot in `catalogFilms`, matched by Film id or TMDB id. When the store carries `catalogFilms` (including `[]`), a Film that is on neither list has zero shows; the Film body `shows` and `epgLastAiring` are not used. The R2 sealed programme (no `catalogFilms`) still prefers the member record, then the Film body.

Not a rating or watched fact ([contract](../integrations/letterboxd-diary-history.md), [ADR 0049](../../adr/0049-letterboxd-diary-viewing-history.md)) |

The reader's `*.lifecycle.json` sidecars are **computed views**, not permission to overwrite current `programme.json`, `people.json`, `epg/discovery-latest.json` or `enrichment/prediction-queue.json`. The offline backfill compares `create/update/unchanged/orphan/conflict` without deleting orphaned data. Reviewed downgrades, unapproved identity replacement and prediction-history loss are explicit conflict types; `updatedAt` and redirect observation times alone do not constitute a changed entity. Tests for this scope include `tests/film-person.mjs`, `tests/film-person-reader.mjs`, `tests/film-person-backfill.mjs`, `tests/lifecycle-parity.mjs`, `tests/film-person-localize.mjs`.

- D1 repositories `src/canonical-repository.js`, `src/epg-repository.js` and `src/prediction-repository.js` were developed for migration work but must not be portrayed as authoritative until a separately approved live migration. ## Accepted design (not implemented): admission of a new Letterboxd watchlist id ([ADR 0186](../../adr/0186-letterboxd-sync-and-independent-watchlist-authority.md), Accepted; not implemented)

**Status (21 Nov 2026, [AIR-569](https://trello.com/c/yaXv2oj6)):** the ledger table and its promotion-batch writer are delivered dormant (see [D1 schema](../data/d1-schema.md)); resolution is AIR-570. A removal from the Watchlist is membership-only (owner decision O3): the Film, its show rows and its rating stay.

A new Letterboxd id in a committed Watchlist snapshot is either **admitted** with a verified TMDB identity through the canonical Film writers (compare-and-swap, receipts) or kept **unresolved** in a durable, deduplicated pending-admission ledger (provenance, attempts, backoff, `resolved`, `superseded` or `terminal` state, a hard cap) that every run enumerates whether or not the scan changed anything; a terminal id goes to the owner's identity inventory. Resolution uses the bounded same-run check (film page and TMDB movie agree; at most three ids per step) and never guesses from a title or year. Concurrent writers of one Film produce one Film and idempotent receipts. A watched or rated fact may explain a removal and is never the proven cause. A Film joins the visible watchlist with its showings before the next EPG scan only with a trusted identity and a stored confirmed show row in the current generation. A removal is membership-only: the removed id leaves the Watchlist membership at the next read of the current snapshot, while the canonical Film, its known show rows and its catalog presence stay (catalog-only presentation); removal alone never implies a watched state, a rating or a reason, and a Diary-confirmed rating arrives independently through the facts pipeline.

## Producer target identity (AIR-572)

The EPG Producer forms its Film targets from the sealed published documents (`P:g`), and **a bare Letterboxd lid is never turned into `film:<lid>` by spelling**. Each listed source id is resolved first (`resolveSourceIdentities` in `src/canonical-producer-workflow.js`; bounded, read-only, at most 1000 distinct lids in 200-lid statements):

1. An id with a `film:`, `tmdb_` or `tmdb:` prefix names its Film directly; a one-hop `film_redirect` is followed to the survivor, a longer chain is refused (`producer_identity_redirect_chain`).
2. A bare lid resolves through the one trusted, non-conflicting `film_external_id` claim (scheme `letterboxd`); an existing owner `film:tmdb:<n>` therefore receives the facts, credits, membership and release of that lid.
3. A lid with no claim resolves to an **existing** `film:<lid>` only. Otherwise it is refused `producer_identity_unresolved`: a legitimate new Film is admitted by the admission paths, not created by the Producer, and the Producer never merges by title or year.
4. An untrusted or competing claim is refused `producer_identity_ambiguous`.

Any refusal fails the **whole snapshot closed** by name before any plan or write, stops the chain on the record (`stopped_by` = the code, so the monitor shows `blocked`), and writes nothing. Failing whole, not dropping one id, is deliberate: a dropped id would be mistaken for a departed Film and released. Resolution is plan-time **and** write-time: every resolved target carries proofs (`guards`) that are asserted INSIDE the fenced batch of each Film write to it (`film_facts` through `writeFilmFacts`, `film_credits` through `mutateFilm`, and a receipt replay, which re-plans the same input): for a claim resolution the owner still holds the lid as its one trusted, non-conflicting claim and no other Film claims it; for an own-name resolution no other Film claims the lid; for a followed redirect the redirect row still exists (the writer itself already asserts that no redirect leaves the target). A claim or redirect that changes after the plan fails the whole batch, receipt tail included, and writes nothing (tests inject the change exactly between the plan and the write batch). The guards are not content: the receipt digest is the same with or without them.

**Membership source (resolved by [ADR 0203](../../adr/0203-watchlist-membership-letterboxd-single-writer-projection.md); history below).** Production switched the `watchlist_film` writer to the Letterboxd Sync on 23 Nov 2026 UTC (marker `sync` generation 1), and the delivery hold was lifted afterwards by a separate AIR-572 change; in mode `sync` the Producer plans and writes no membership. The paragraph that follows records the state before the switch.

**Membership source (as it stood before ADR 0203).** Under `watchlist_authority=d1` the Letterboxd Sync retention effect writes `film_retention`, and Film admission never reads or writes `watchlist_film`; the **only live writer of `watchlist_film` is this Producer**, from the EPG-sealed copy of the Watchlist version pinned at `beginSync`. User-facing watchlist reads compose membership from the current snapshot (ADR 0186 decision 6), but `catalog-d1` (`surface`, `inWatchlist`) and the canonical read model read `watchlist_film`. The EPG pin does not prove the membership is current after later Letterboxd slots. The Producer therefore stays under the delivery hold until the boundary is decided (see the AIR-572 PR: version-pinned compare-and-set against `watchlist_current`, or membership taken from the current snapshot, both a material flow change).

## Watchlist membership writer (AIR-582, [ADR 0203](../../adr/0203-watchlist-membership-letterboxd-single-writer-projection.md), Accepted)

`watchlist_film` (membership of a Film in the current Watchlist) has two phases. **Marker `producer` (today, also while the table is absent):** the EPG Producer writes it through `writeFilmFacts`. **Marker `sync`:** only `setFilmMembership` (Letterboxd Sync `membership` effect, reconcile step and the one-time baseline) writes it. `setFilmMembership({id, member, version, at, guards?, baseline?})` never creates a Film, refuses a redirected or missing Film (`film_person_redirect`, `film_person_precondition`) and asserts, inside one fenced batch, the Film/Person marker, the membership marker and generation, the resolver's identity proofs and `NOT EXISTS watchlist_film_fence WHERE version > ?`. It upserts `watchlist_film_fence` (a tombstone `member = 0` after a removal) and then inserts or deletes the membership row. Results: `written`, `unchanged` (same version and state) or `stale` (a newer version is stored). `baseline: true` is the only way to write while the marker is `producer`. Whole-list identity resolution (needed to decide whether a Film is still listed by another lid) runs in chunks of at most 1000 lids; the Watchlist contract allows 5000. Identity merge still moves membership with a Film. Tables: `watchlist_membership_authority`, `watchlist_film_fence`, `watchlist_membership_handoff` (schema 37, [migration](../../../migrations/0037_watchlist_membership_projection.sql)).

## Discovery owner evidence rows (ADR 0215 decision 6, E2c; dormant)

| Owner intent | Row | Root | Observed at |

## Unified Film read contract (ADR 0216, Accepted, resolver and sections delivered dormant)

[ADR 0216](../../adr/0216-canonical-film-lifecycle-and-unified-read-contract.md) is **Accepted**. It defines `FilmAggregateV1`, a read-only composition of one Film's identity, display, Watchlist membership, availability, personal facts, predictions and a link to history. Its typed input takes `film:<id>`, `tmdb_<n>`, `tmdb_tv:<n>` or a lid, and an identity read-set fence covers the whole answer. No deployed route answers that shape yet: the resolver and the sections are delivered dormant (not bundled); activation is [AIR-648](https://trello.com/c/zh0cfDWK).

**Typed resolver and identity fence ([AIR-646](https://trello.com/c/OzZ7uZZj), delivery graph step 2, dormant).** `src/film-aggregate-resolver.js` is not bundled and no route or consumer calls it. It reads D1 only (SELECTs; no write, R2 or network) and requires the Film/Person marker `open`.

- `parseTypedFilmId` implements the decision 2 grammar: `tmdb_tv:<n>` and `tmdb_<n>` are checked before anything else (any other `tmdb_…` string is `400 invalid`), then `film:<id>`, then a bare lid. `tmdb_movie` queries only `film_external_id` scheme `tmdb`, `tmdb_tv` only `tmdb_tv`, a lid only `letterboxd`.
- `resolveFilmIdentity` returns the verdict (`found`, `redirected`, `absent`, `retired`, `conflict`, `unverified`, `cycle`, `unavailable`, `invalid`), the survivor, the redirect chain, the claims (a retired movie claim is reported with `retired: true` and is in no alias set), the authorized aliases per scheme (at most 8 each, overflow named in `aliasOverflow`), the permalink and the identity token `{generation, readSet}`.
- **Coherent capture form: bounded rounds with full keyed revalidation, one statement per round.** The read-set is a key set: the Films met (film row, redirect row, `canonical_revision`, all their claims), the `(scheme, value)` pairs queried (every row of any trust and any Film) and the retirement ledger by every requested **or claimed** TMDB movie id (the ledger retires a movie id whatever Film it names; a non-superseded `retire` makes the claim retired for every Film, a `restore` lifts it; a TV number is never looked up there). One SQL statement (`IDENTITY_SNAPSHOT_SQL`) reads the marker and the whole key set as one snapshot. A round that needs a key it did not read (owner, next hop, the survivor's claims, competing rows, Films met) grows the key set and re-reads all of it; the answer comes only from a round that read every key its evaluation needed. The digest covers the marker, the key set, every row, and explicit zero counts (a queried pair with no row, a Film with no redirect, a movie id with no decision).
- **Budget and final fence.** The 40-statement request budget counts actual D1 statements: the fence statement is reserved first, the three statements of the marker gate (`readAuthority`) are charged before they run, then one per round; section readers spend only what remains. `readFilmAggregateIdentity` runs the section readers and then, unconditionally on the found path (also when a section reader throws), re-reads the same key set with the same statement. Any difference, a marker move, an overflow, or a fence that cannot run returns `503` with only the identity verdict and no section. A section reader that throws loses its sections (`sectionsUnavailable: {reason: "section_read_failed"}`); `contract` and `identity` cannot be overwritten by a section.
- **Bounds.** At most 8 redirect hops (more is `409 cycle`, `identity_fanout`), 16 Films and 64 distinct claim rows (a row read both by Film and by pair counts once; ancestors' and competing Films' claims count), 13 rounds. Materialization is bounded in SQL (`LIMIT` cap + 1 per row column) and the key set is checked against the caps before a round queries it; fan-out is decided before any other verdict (`409 conflict`, `identity_fanout`).
- **Index rule (decision 7).** Every lookup is index-served. The retirement ledger is read by `movie_id` through the non-unique index `tmdb_retirement_decision_movie` on `tmdb_retirement_decision(movie_id)`, selected by the owner on 25 Nov 2026 (option A, not a full-scan exception). The index is created by migration `0040_tmdb_retirement_decision_movie_index.sql` ([AIR-667](https://trello.com/c/SbV827kN); [apply runbook](../../runbooks/retirement-movie-index-0040-apply.md)); its production apply and readback are a native prerequisite of activation ([AIR-648](https://trello.com/c/zh0cfDWK)), and until then the dormant statement would scan the ledger, so the resolver must not be activated before. The synthetic EQP test of the resolver runs on the real migration chain and asserts the indexed plan with no base-table scan; `tests/migration-0040-retirement-movie-index.mjs` asserts the scan before and the `SEARCH` after the migration for requested and claimed movie ids, hits and misses.
- `sectionsUnavailable` is a dormant defensive fallback for a throwing section reader; the per-section statuses and tokens are returned by step 3 ([AIR-647](https://trello.com/c/WKSwJOwm)), below.

**Sections and candidate coverage ([AIR-647](https://trello.com/c/WKSwJOwm), delivery graph step 3, dormant).** `src/film-aggregate.js` composes `display`, `watchlist`, `availability`, `personal`, `predictions` and `history` per request over the resolver, inside its 40-statement budget and before its final fence. Each section reads its data and its tokens in one statement and re-reads that statement after all section reads (`generation_moved` on a difference); a section that fails loses only itself. Sections read only the authorized aliases; G1 candidate discovery over the D1-owned ratings link index is bounded **in the statement that reads it** (40 parts / 2.4 MB measured and returned in one snapshot, 50 000 entries, 4 fact records of at most 8 parts each with one validated hop per root and cycle detection, 8 candidates, 64 settlement rows) and keeps `personal` and `predictions` `partial` until every candidate is settled by another Film's canonical claim; a known conflict between authorized keys stays `conflict`, and an unknown alias or an alias beyond the per-scheme cap is never negative evidence. Display uses the loader's own localization mapping over the rows it captured and reads the owner entries of the survivor and of every Film of its redirect tree (a recursive CTE through the index `film_redirect_by_target` of migration 0043, bounded to depth 8 and 64 ancestors inside the statement; an overflow is typed, never complete); without delivered ADR 0214 provenance a missing metadata field is `unknown`. `history` is `limited` (only when the legacy bridge would accept the Film and the coverage held through every fence) or `unavailable`, never `present`. Contract: [FilmAggregateV1](../api/film-aggregate.md).
