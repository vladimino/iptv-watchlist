## Context

### What exists (main `40c8a3a`, 25 Nov 2026)

- **The only canonical writer for a Discovery Film is minimal by design.** Since the projector was retired ([ADR 0155](0155-retire-enrichment-projector-cron.md)), a Discovery admission reaches canonical state only through the `film_mutation` unit of [ADR 0134](0134-discovery-admission-producer-wiring.md) decision 5 (`src/canonical-discovery-admission-producer.js`, origin `discovery`): the Film, the trusted `tmdb` claim and a title written only when blank. It writes no `film_metadata`, credits, Persons, aliases, localization or `canonical_identity`. When the Film already has its claim and title the mutation is a no-op with **no receipt**.
- **Every richer writer is bounded to another cohort.** EPG `film_facts` and credits target Watchlist Films only; `metadata_sync` is Watchlist only; the dictionary covers its entries; the Catalog Enrichment Producer ("C1", ADR 0211) is scoped to rated admissions (`film_rated_admission.verified_at >= cutoff_at`) with the widenings C3a (AIR-614) and C3b (AIR-615). C1's code and tables are **not yet in `main`**.
- **C1 already fetches what Discovery Films lack.** Its TMDB request is `movie/<id>?append_to_response=credits,translations`, one response with year, runtime, genres, poster, overview, the credits and the translations. Every Film/part/source has one state row and a stable receipt `operation_key` (ADR 0211 decision 3).
- **The Discovery payload is Discovery's own serving record**, not a source: it mixes TMDB values with EPG fallbacks, a poster that another path can fill, and aliases and availability that change on every EPG touch (`src/discovery.js:352`, `POSTER_UPDATE_SQL`). It is **not an input** of this design.
- **Identity facts already change under transactional writers.** `film_external_id` (claims), `tmdb_retirement_decision`, `film_redirect`, the metadata lock and the owner release rows are written by the canonical writers in fenced D1 batches, and the repository already uses database triggers for such invariants (ADR 0214).
- **Credit limits are scattered.** ADR 0211 bounds the expected set at 10 actors and 20 directors and the Persons at 30 per Film, within a unit cap of 50; the canonical credit reader refuses a Film with more than 200 credits (`CREDIT_READ_CAP`, `src/canonical-catalog-reader.js`); the EPG chain refuses a credit list over 50; the user interface shows the first ten.
- **EPG publication conflicts with Discovery over the catalog (resolver) lease and the projector window**, not with the canonical Film and Person tables (ADR 0135, 0175): enrichment takes neither the `discovery_lease` nor the `projector_window` and writes only canonical Film and Person state behind the Film/Person authority marker.
- **No Discovery Film has a Russian title in canonical state.**

### Census (AIR-627, L0, dated 25 Nov 2026, main `681b5c1`, Film/Person g2, EPG P519)

Dated aggregates, not live counts; the eligible historical counts per field and part are **not known**. Minimal Discovery Films (since 16 Nov, `film_mutation` only): 2 525, with no `film_metadata`, credits or aliases. Projector-era Discovery Films: 9 730, with projector credits and aliases but mostly no `film_metadata`. Future admissions: about 2 525 in about 9 days, so about 280 per day (a dated estimate). Rated (714, 473 with metadata missing) and Watchlist (391) Films belong to C1 and its widenings. `canonical_identity` NULL is not a proof of missing identity: each holds a trusted `tmdb` claim.

### The problem

Canonical Film/Person D1 is the authority (ADR 0216), but for Discovery Films it holds an id, a claim and a title, and nobody owns refresh. C1 is the accepted owner of enrichment, but its scope does not reach Discovery Films, and its accepted design is **polling-first**: a fixed per-tick quota, a scan that finds new work within hours and an anchor maintenance that finds changed identity within a day. The owner's direction is an event-driven producer: known work is enqueued **immediately** by the change that causes it.

### Settled decisions this record keeps

- `film_mutation` stays minimal (ADR 0134 decision 5). Discovery admission is unchanged except for the signal of decision 2.
- After enrichment the canonical row is the authority; the Discovery payload stays Discovery's serving record and is never copied into canonical state or read as source evidence.
- One producer, one queue and state owner: ADR 0211's planner, fetcher and state machine, with its anchors and agreement, the 72 h transport-only fallback (0211-7 = B), the credits namespace rule, write intent, epochs, lease, control, pause, shared upstream cooldown (0211-5 = C) and visibility; ADR 0214's rank and locks. No second producer, no Discovery ingestion producer, no parallel work queue, no observation pipeline.
- Identity is never inferred from a title, a year or a name. No Letterboxd or Wikidata anchor is invented. A 404 is neither a retirement nor proof of another Film.
- No unsolicited time-based **upstream** refresh.
- ADR 0218 keeps a `film_dimension` row only for owner or reviewed decisions, the ADR 0214 metadata lock, `titleSource` and the effective-localization projection.
- Mass enrichment waits for AIR-607 and AIR-636 natural acceptance; this gates activation, not this design.

## Decision

### 1. One producer, one queue and state owner

C1 (ADR 0211; AIR-605) is the only planner, fetcher and state machine, and **the only consumer of the signal inbox** of decision 2. Emitters append signals and never edit C1's private state. A Film has **one** state row per part and source and the **existing** stable receipt keys of ADR 0211 decision 3 (`enrich:<film>:core`, `enrich:<film>:localization:<source>`, `enrich:<film>:credits:<source>:<chunk>` (decision 8) and `enrich:person:<person id>`), whatever scope admitted it. A scope is a **membership**, not a task: C1's eligible set is the union of its enabled scopes (rated, C3a, C3b, the Discovery scope C3c and, later, the history cohort and the source-independent admissions of ADR 0217). A Film in several scopes is planned once.

### 2. The event contract: a shared signal and invalidation inbox

**Principle.** Known work is made durably pending **by the change that causes it, in the same transaction**, and executed as soon as its guards allow. Enqueue is immediate; execution still respects approved activation, locks, verified identity, resources, backoff and contention. This replaces the polling-first discovery of ADR 0211 and the former Choice 5.

**The inbox and its key.** One small source-independent table, `enrichment_signal`, is the whole contract between emitters and C1; there is no second bus or producer. Its key is **normalized and never null**: `PRIMARY KEY (kind, subject, source, part)` with `source` and `part` `NOT NULL DEFAULT ''` (a unique key over nullable columns would not coalesce, because SQLite treats NULLs as distinct). Columns: `version` (an integer **change token**, raised on every semantic re-arm), `acked_version` (the newest version the consumer has fully applied), `state` (`pending`, `consumed` or `staged`; a `staged` signal is inert until a transfer's commit settles it, decision 3), `cause_digest`, `armed_at`, `attempts`, `last_error` and `retry_at` (the **durable retry-due time** of a failed consumption, null when none). It holds at most one row per key, so it is bounded by the catalog, and an index on `(state, armed_at)` serves the consumer.

**Emit.** An emitter upserts: a new row has `version = 1`; an existing row gets `version = version + 1`, `state = 'pending'` and a new `armed_at` **whenever the cause is semantically new**. A digest-carrying signal re-arms only when `cause_digest` differs from the stored one, so an identical observation writes nothing and wakes nothing; a repeated identical event that is not digest-carrying coalesces into one row because the key is the same. No waiting window or timer is involved.

**Consume: one guarded batch, exactly once, tied to the exact version.** C1 reads a signal at version `v` and applies its effect and acknowledges it in **one D1 batch in which every effect statement carries the guard**, not only the acknowledgement:
- **the guard** is a single condition repeated in each statement's `WHERE`: the signal is `pending` at exactly version `v` with `acked_version < v`; **and** the control is `active` at the generation `g` the plan read, with **at least one enabled membership that justifies the effect** (for an `admitted` signal, its own scope enabled; for `anchor_changed`, `source_changed` and `protection_released`, at least one enabled membership of the Film, or of the Film it resolves or merges into, so a redirect or merge and a newly eligible pending admission are still resolved); **and** the lease is live; **and** the Film's identity and redirect resolution still equal the plan's (the anchor digest and no redirect of the Film);
- **the effects** (create or merge the retained membership, mark the part due, invalidate, **raise `refresh_epoch` once**) are written only under that guard, so a statement of a stale signal affects no row; an epoch increment is therefore not an unguarded counter bump, and a replay finds `acked_version >= v` and writes nothing;
- **the acknowledgement** is the last statement and carries the **same full guard** (not only the version): `UPDATE enrichment_signal SET acked_version = v, state = 'consumed' WHERE key = ? AND version = v AND acked_version < v AND <the guard>`. A D1 batch is one transaction and the guard reads no row the batch itself changes before the acknowledgement, so the guard evaluates identically in every statement and **the effects and the acknowledgement apply together or not at all**; an acknowledgement is therefore never recorded for a guarded-out effect;
- if **any** guard fails (a re-arm raised the version, the scope was disabled, the control paused or moved generation, the lease was lost, the identity moved), **no effect is applied** and the newest version stays `pending`; a re-arm during consumption is never lost and never acted on stale.
- A failed batch changes nothing: the signal stays `pending` and is retried with the ADR 0098 backoff tiers, recording `attempts`, `last_error` and the next `retry_at` (a signal is **retry-due** when `retry_at` is null or not later than now); **one failing signal never blocks the others**, and a repeatedly failing one is a repairable item (decision 7).
- **Scope changes fence every in-flight effect.** Every enable, disable or change of a scope **advances the common control generation**, which every batch of every Worker version already asserts (ADR 0211 decision 6), so a stale plan cannot commit after the last enabled membership of a Film is disabled, after a pause, or after a scope change; pause and resume fence in-flight durable effects exactly as accepted.
- **Causal emitters are not producer effects.** Emitters (triggers and code emitters) keep accumulating signals while the control is paused or a scope is disabled; only the producer's **effects** are gated by the control and the scope.
- **Coverage, not only a digest.** A `source_changed` signal is dropped without a fetch only when the applied `source_version` equals its digest **and the part is complete for that version** (every unit applied); with incomplete units the part is still due.
- **One digest definition, over what the observer verified.** The shared pure projection that observers use **is** the definition of ADR 0211's `sourceVersion` (the canonical-field digest of the source fields used, with no clocks) and includes the **source identity** (the anchor's scheme and value); C1 computes its own `sourceVersion` with the same function and a parity fixture proves they agree for every part. **An observer emits a digest only for a part whose section it completely verified**: an observer whose response lacks the credits or the translations emits **no** signal for that part, never an empty "complete" projection, so it cannot force an endless refresh.

**Atomicity, and no network call in the transaction.** Triggers fire inside the causal statement, so the signal and the change commit or roll back together. A code emitter appends its signal in the same batch as the change it reports. No emitter makes a network call. A crash after the commit and before any wake loses nothing, because the pending signal is durable.

**An unresolved `admitted` signal does not keep a Workflow spinning.** A signal whose Film does not exist yet is **not runnable**; the run parks. It is resolved by the **causal event**: when C1 consumes the `anchor_changed` of a Film it resolves every pending `admitted` whose subject `tmdb:<n>` equals the Film's trusted `tmdb` claim. **If the claim already exists** when the `admitted` signal is consumed (the anchor came first), it resolves at once. The bounded recovery scan is the only other path and runs at low frequency as a safety net.

**Prompt wake without a lost wakeup.** The ordering is fixed so that a runnable signal committed at any moment is seen:
1. The causal writer commits the signal, then calls `kick` (`ctx.waitUntil`, best-effort).
2. **Parking and retiring use one durable protocol:** the instance first makes its **intent durable** on the run record (`phase = 'parked'`, or `'retired'` for the terminal empty-queue exit, by compare-and-swap) and **only then re-checks** for **runnable** work; if any exists it cancels the park or retirement.
3. **Runnable means:** a signal that is `pending`, whose scope is enabled, that is resolved (its Film exists), whose `retry_at` is due and that the guard of the consume batch would accept; or a due part or a due retry (decision 7). **Held** (disabled scope), **unresolved**, **backed-off** and **deferred** items are **not** runnable, so they cannot keep a run spinning or cancel a park.
4. `kick` reads the run record: **`parked`** sends the instance a `kick` event (a `waitForEvent` with the earliest due time as its timeout); **`running`** needs no event, because that instance will make its intent durable and re-check, and the committed signal is already visible to it; **`retired`** or **no live owner** creates the instance; **a handover in progress** (a recorded successor intent) targets the successor, whose start is a full runnable check. `sendEvent` throws when the instance is not running, and that is read as "no live owner" and creates one.
5. **Event before the wait.** The platform buffers an event sent to an existing instance before it reaches `waitForEvent` and delivers it when the matching wait starts (Cloudflare Workflows documentation, read on 25 Nov 2026), so an event between the re-check and the wait is not lost; a stale buffered event only causes a harmless early re-check. A `waitForEvent` that times out **throws and fails the instance** unless wrapped, so the instance wraps it and treats the timeout as the due-time wake.
6. **Lease reacquisition.** After any wake the instance acquires a lease by compare-and-swap, as at a start, and **re-validates the control generation and epochs** before any write; an expired lease during the park is simply reacquired or, if another owner holds it, the run finishes.
7. A lost kick (a crash after the commit, a path without the hook, an old Worker version) is recovered by the reconcile cron, a **safety net and not the ordinary delay**.

**Due-time wake.** With every remaining item deferred (a future `retry_at`, an active cooldown) the instance parks with the earliest known due time as its timeout and resumes at that time or on a kick. A parked instance holds no lease, uses no CPU and does not count toward concurrency.

**Dormancy.** An emitter gated by a scope writes nothing before that scope has first been **activated** (the permanent `activated` flag); afterwards it keeps recording whether the scope is currently `enabled` or disabled, and only C1's **effects** need an enabled scope. C1 does nothing while its control is not `active`. The anchor and protection triggers write only the bounded coalesced inbox; C1 ignores subjects that belong to no enabled scope. **C1's own writes emit nothing**: no emitting trigger exists on `film_metadata`, `film_person_credit`, `person` or `film_localization_source`, the protection triggers fire only on protected-to-unprotected changes (which C1's provisional localization writes never are), and a fixture runs a complete write cycle and asserts zero inbox changes, so there is no endless self-wake.

### 3. Scopes: the Discovery scope starts at activation, the others bootstrap their existing members, membership is retained

**Authoritative event boundaries.** A signal is emitted only at an authoritative admission, never at staging, replay or restore.

**Discovery (C3c).**
- *Two writers of the catalog, read from the actual callers.* The **steady path** is `upsertAdmissions` (the paged chunk, `src/discovery.js` lines 990, 1033 and 1095, and `src/worker.js` line 2277): a genuinely new Film is an insert of a row that did not exist. The **whole-document path** is `saveDiscoveredFilms` (`src/discovery.js`, lines 1379 and 1415), used by the non-paged resolver chunk for a source that is not the D1 archive, which routes `epg/discovered-films.json` through `replaceCatalog`: it sets `discovery_transfer.state = 'loading'`, moves the current rows to `pending`, upserts batches and only then commits the transfer. **That path adds genuinely new Films** (`films[id] = catalogFilmFromDetail(...)` then a whole-catalog save), so the invariant "a bulk path only reconstructs history" is **false** and is not assumed; a transfer is not excluded as a persistence mechanism.
- *The cutoff is the activation, not a persistence path.* From the activation, every **genuinely new authoritative admission** is a stream admission, whichever path persists it; everything before the activation is the history cohort. **Discovery has no bootstrap**, by the owner's decision P1.
- *Emission gate.* The gated trigger fires on an insert **after the scope has first been activated** (a permanent `activated` flag on the scope row, set once by the activation batch and never reset), so an admission always leaves a signal from then on, whether the scope is currently enabled or disabled. No admission fact column, backfill or early migration exists, and none is invented.
- *Steady path:* the insert emits `admitted` as **`pending`**.
- *Transfer path: explicit provenance, staged facts, settlement at publication.* `replaceCatalog` always requires a lease (`context.lease || leaseOf(env)`, else `discovery_lease`), so a lease proves nothing about authority and **no "tool without a lease" case exists**. Authority is declared **explicitly at the caller boundary**: the resolver run that publishes the catalog passes `context.admission = { origin: 'resolver', programmeRunId }` together with the published-generation fence it already holds; any other caller (an import, a restore, an operator tool) passes nothing and is non-authoritative. The loading batch records the origin and `programme_run_id` on `discovery_transfer` (C3c-D adds one `origin` column; `programme_run_id` already exists and is the **transfer identity**, because `discovery_transfer` has a singleton `id`, `state`, `programme_run_id` and `batches`, and no transfer id).
- *Staging.* An insert of a **movie** admission (`media_kind = 'movie'`; a TV row whose id equals a movie id is never staged) **while `discovery_transfer.state = 'loading'`** emits `admitted` as **`staged`** whether or not the scope has been activated yet, carrying the transfer identity and its recorded origin in `cause_ref`. A staged signal is not consumable.
- *Settlement.* **`replaceCatalog`'s own commit batch** (the batch that sets the transfer `committed`) settles the staged signals **of that transfer identity**: when the origin is `resolver` and the scope has been **activated at that commit**, every staged signal whose admission row is `current` becomes `pending` and the others are deleted; otherwise every staged signal of the transfer is deleted. **The cutoff is therefore the first authoritative publication commit after activation**: a row staged before the activation but published after it is stream; a row published before it is history. Settlement runs inside the existing commit batching (the same per-batch statements), with no new subsystem and no artificial cap.
- *Failed, interrupted and old transfers.* A failed or partial transfer leaves its staged signals inert. A **retry of the same programme run** upserts the existing rows (no second insert) and its commit settles the same signals by identity. A **later transfer of another identity never promotes them**: at the start of a new `loading` the staged signals of a **non-resolver** origin are deleted, and those of an abandoned **resolver** attempt are kept and are promoted only by a later resolver publication that makes their row `current`, so a failed import or restore is never promoted, and a genuinely new Film of a failed resolver publication is not lost.
- *Deployment and rollback.* The **activation preflight** (C3c-A) proves a compatible emitter and settlement are deployed, no transfer is `loading`, and no stale staged signal exists, so an old-version `replaceCatalog` cannot strand staged facts across the activation; an interrupted transfer is recovered by the retry above; rollback to an old version leaves any staged facts inert and they are deleted by the next non-authoritative start or settled by the next resolver publication.
- *The races, closed by construction.* An insert before the activation batch is history and a published one after is stream; a new admission of an already existing Film and a no-op canonical receipt are admitted, because the trigger is on the admission row; a delayed canonical admission resolves on the claim event (decision 2); a pre-activation admission swept to its Film later emits only `anchor_changed`, which C1 ignores for a Film with no membership; there is no update trigger, so a row that existed before the activation can never become stream. A historical row updated by a transfer emits nothing.

**The other scopes bootstrap their existing members.** For rated (AIR-607), C3a (AIR-614) and C3b (AIR-615) the existing cohort is the point of the scope, and AIR-607's accepted cutoff may precede the activation, so a gated insert trigger alone would miss every qualifying row that already exists. Each scope's activation therefore runs a **bounded, version-fenced bootstrap**:
- the gated trigger is **enabled first** and the bootstrap runs **after**, so a row inserted during the bootstrap is caught by the trigger and a row that exists is caught by the bootstrap, with **no gap**;
- the bootstrap pages through the scope's existing members in a fixed key order (rated: `film_rated_admission.verified_at >= cutoff_at`; C3a: the existing admitted rated Films; C3b: the current `watchlist_film` rows), writes `admitted` signals through the same upsert, and the unique key **deduplicates the overlap** with the live tail;
- its position and state (`bootstrap_state`, `bootstrap_cursor`) live in the scope row and advance under the scope's `revision`, so it is **resumable**, idempotent and fenced: a changed scope configuration stops it rather than letting a stale bootstrap continue;
- it is paced by the same resource-bounded drainer and priority as any work (decision 5).

**The real Watchlist event.** The authority of Watchlist membership is the Letterboxd Sync's durable `watchlist_change` rows of a version, applied by the `membership` effect (ADR 0203), and `watchlist_film` is its **projection**, not the authority. The emitter is the point where the effect has resolved an id to a Film and written the projection (a trigger on the insert), so an addition that is unresolved or ambiguous, held in the handoff ledger, produces no enrichment until it resolves, and enrichment follows the effect's own resolution and lag. A missing `watchlist_film` row is never read as a negative membership. This is the same lag every canonical reader of membership has.

**Membership is retained (P2 = B), and priority is separate.** On consuming an `admitted` signal C1 creates a **retained membership** `catalog_enrichment_member(film_id, scope, admitted_at, signal_key)` once the Film resolves. A Film's **eligibility** persists independently of whether it is still current or airing in Discovery: a superseded admission, an EPG change, a transfer or a re-admission neither discards outstanding work nor excludes the Film. Kept as accepted: an explicit cohort disable or pause (a Film is processed while at least one of its memberships is in an **enabled** scope); an authoritative retirement; an invalid or conflicting identity; the redirect or merge rules (membership and state follow the survivor); the metadata lock and owner pins. One work item per Film, part and source; a re-admission coalesces. Retained eligibility is **not** current priority: priority is derived from authoritative facts at pick time (decision 5), so an old stream arrival is not given permanent priority.

**Global pause versus a disabled cohort; the cutoff is retained.**
- *Global pause or resume* (the control): signals and the new eligible work accumulate and are processed on resume, nothing is dropped.
- *An explicitly approved disabling of a cohort:* the cohort's emitter **keeps recording** signals (the emission gate is `activated`, not `enabled`), its signals are **held** and not consumed, and its memberships are not processed unless another enabled scope holds the Film. **The activation cutoff is retained**: on re-enable the held signals are consumed and the **eligible work is caught up**; nothing in the disabled interval is redefined as history. There is **no exclusion policy** in this record for a disabled interval; an operation that wants one (for example an import or a restore) states it in its own plan.
- *Re-enable of a rated, C3a or C3b scope* re-runs its idempotent bootstrap.
- *Pending events, merges and old-version writers:* pending signals survive; membership and state follow the survivor of a merge through `anchor_changed`; the triggers fire whatever the writer's version, an old version does not kick, and the reconcile cron recovers.

**Reconciliation with accepted records.** The existence of a Film is independent of membership (ADR 0216); this record changes no Film's existence. The rated and Watchlist scope boundaries are unchanged, and AIR-607's rated cutoff predicate stays: only the **intake** of its candidates becomes an event with a bootstrap. ADR 0217's source-independent admission is a future emitter of the same `admitted` signal; **a source-only Film outside every approved scope stays excluded**, and its integration is a later, separately approved delivery, not activated here.

**Sources, anchors and agreement are unchanged.** For a Film in C3c, C1 derives the applicable sources as ADR 0211 decision 2 does: the `tmdb` anchor makes `tmdb` and `wikidata` applicable; `letterboxd` only with an existing trusted lid claim, never invented. Agreement, the 72 h fallback, precedence and `identity_disagreement` are unchanged.

### 4. 2A: the typed Russian title comes from C1's own TMDB response

C1's `localization` part writes the `tmdb` source row from the response that already carries `credits,translations`, with **no separate fetch**. The selection reads the `translations.translations` entries with `iso_639_1 = 'ru'`: exactly one distinct non-blank `data.title` is the value; several entries are decided by `iso_3166_1 = 'RU'`, and if they still differ the selection is **ambiguous** and nothing is written (a recorded gap, owner-resolvable); no `ru` entry is a legitimate absence (`complete_with_gaps`); a missing or malformed section of an otherwise complete response is **unreadable** and is a repairable failure (decision 7). The response `title`, aliases and `movieTitles` are never read as a Russian title. The unit is the existing per-source key `enrich:<film>:localization:tmdb` (the `letterboxd` and `wikidata` keys stay distinct), written through the AIR-662 projection-aware operation with reviewed and owner precedence. The original 2A carried the translation through a Discovery observation; under 1B that transport does not exist, because C1 fetches the response itself, and reusing Discovery's response would need the rejected observation subsystem, so it is not reintroduced.

### 5. Priority and fairness (P3 = B)

**Supersedes the global strict oldest-first rule of ADR 0211 decision 4.** Retained eligibility (decision 3) is separate from **current priority**, which is derived at pick time from authoritative facts and is never frozen at admission:
- **Live:** a Film that is **personal** (an authoritative Watchlist or rated fact) or **currently relevant** (available in the pinned published EPG generation, ADR 0216 section 5). **Unknown is not false:** a Film whose membership or availability cannot be established is live. Personal and current Films form **one tier**, ordered inside it by age (oldest `due_since` first); no order between the personal and the current categories is invented.
- **Background:** a Film with a **known** absence of both (authoritatively not personal and not available in the pinned generation) and the history cohort (decision 10). A retained Discovery Film that has stopped airing therefore moves to background priority, **without losing its eligibility or its outstanding work**, and an old stream arrival never keeps a permanent priority over genuinely current work.
- **Precedence, unambiguous, with overlap and unknown facts.** (1) A **positive** authoritative live fact (a personal membership, or availability in the pinned generation) makes the Film **live**, whatever its cohort. (2) Otherwise a Film that belongs **only to the history cohort** is **background**: unknown facts neither promote it to live nor count as a proved negative, they simply do not promote. (3) Otherwise (a member of a live cohort: Discovery stream, rated, Watchlist) with unknown facts is **live** (unknown is not false), and with **known** absence of both is **background**. A Film in several cohorts takes the **highest** class this gives; nothing is frozen at admission.
- **Indexed due selection with bounded examined work.** Due items are indexed by `(cohort, retry_at, due_since)`. A pick examines **at most `K` rows per cohort** at the head of the index (a read page, an engineering default), classifies those by the authoritative facts with one indexed read, and applies the policy. An item that fails or is backed off leaves the head because its `retry_at` moves into the future, so the examined window always advances and **a failing background item can never prevent independent live work**; examined work is bounded and reported. The harness must **prove forward progress and fairness**: with `K` rows examined per cohort, a head of permanently failing or ineligible rows moves out of the window by `retry_at`, so a runnable row behind it is reached within a bounded number of picks.

**Starvation prevention, on meaningful work.** Background work always makes progress while live work keeps arriving. The share is accounted in **committed units and completed or progressed Films, not in starts**: a start that fails, or that only repeats a failing item, consumes no entitlement, and a very large Film earns its turn by the units it commits, so a failing or huge Film can neither burn the background share nor starve it. The default entitlement is about one in eight units of committed work for the background class; it is **engineering tuning in one module, measured and adjusted, not a newly approved quota**.

**Measured, not promised.** The harness reports completion latency per class, oldest due age, background progress under sustained mixed load, EPG holds and retries; no zero-degradation claim is made.

### 6. Execution: a resource-bounded queue drainer, event-woken

The producer is a **work-conserving, resource-bounded queue drainer from its first implementation (AIR-605), across every scope**. ADR 0211 decision 5's starting design values (a 25 s tick deadline, a 5 s reserve, 2 Films per hourly invocation, a 12-unit budget, a derived 8-request ceiling, a 10 s second-Film rule, a measure-first rule, hourly scheduling) were never a provider limit or a separate owner selection ([the executor's comment of 24 Nov](https://github.com/vladimino/iptv-watchlist/pull/975#issuecomment-7132176638)) and are removed as normative limits, **without being replaced by larger numbers**. Safety budgets stay; throughput quotas go. The execution reuses the proven Discovery pattern ([ADR 0083](0083-inert-discovery-workflow.md), [0093](0093-discovery-workflow-cron-reconcile.md), [0094](0094-discovery-workflow-invocation-budget.md), [0098](0098-discovery-workflow-step-failure-recovery.md), [0174](0174-discovery-only-fast-recovery-cron.md)):

| Part | Design |
| --- | --- |
| Executor | one Workflow class; an instance is a run; **a step is a bounded group of units of one Film** (sized by a statement slice, decision below), durable and replay-safe; one owner through the `projection_run` lease and a deterministic series and instance id |
| Resource guard | the instance counts the D1 statements it issues, **failing and replayed calls included**, and **hands over** to a successor at the budget, created before it finishes (ADR 0094) |
| Continuation | a run ends only for: no runnable work (it **parks** or finishes), the statement or step budget (**handover**), every remaining item deferred (it **parks** until the earliest due time), a pause, a lost lease, or a repairable step failure (decision 7) |
| Wake | the kick, the parked event and the reconcile cron of decision 2 |
| Cooldown, receipts, units, guards, epochs | unchanged (ADR 0211 decisions 3 and 5, ADR 0207 decision 3) |

**Resource guards and defaults** (engineering-owned tuning, constants in one module and reported in each run's outbox entry): the instance statement budget of **6,000** is the ADR 0094 initial guard, not a provider limit; **Workflow steps hand over at 5,000**, half the documented 10,000; subrequests share the statement counter against the documented default of 10,000; CPU is bounded by the configured `cpu_ms` (120,000), with the harness showing the statements bind first; the lease TTL is five minutes, renewed before each step; the per-request timeouts (TMDB 12 s, Letterboxd 15 s, Wikidata 15 s) are provider-response bounds and **no run deadline**; the per-scope upstream concurrency is one; a step takes a statement slice of about 500; read pages are 500 rows. **There is no whole-Film-fit rule:** a step starts when the remaining budget covers **its own unit group plus a finalize-and-successor reserve** (initially 1,000 statements, set by the harness), progress is durable per unit, and a Film with many missing Persons spans as many steps and instances as it needs and is **never deferred for not fitting**.

**Atomic handover.** The predecessor records the successor intent by compare-and-swap, creates the instance (idempotent by id), **releases its lease** by compare-and-swap and stops writing; the successor writes only after acquiring a new `lease_id`. A create whose response was lost, a crash between create and release, an expired lease during a parked or sleeping step, and a replayed step (which re-checks owner and lease first) are specified and tested. Platform step retries are capped (default three, the ADR 0098 tiers); the counters include failing and replayed calls.

**EPG priority without a blanket hold (P4 = B).**
- *Verified conflict surface.* EPG publication and Discovery contend over the catalog (resolver) lease and the projector window (ADR 0135, 0175). The producer takes neither, and writes only canonical Film and Person state behind the Film/Person authority marker with the Film revision, anchor and epoch fences. The surfaces it actually shares with EPG are D1 itself and those authority fences.
- *No hold.* **Enqueue is durable and immediate regardless of the EPG window.** The producer does **not** read the EPG priority gate as a stop, there is **no blanket dependency on EPG success**, and an unrelated failed or stalled EPG is never a stop. **The EPG pause of Discovery admission is unchanged and not weakened.**
- *Narrow yield, proactive and reactive.* **Proactive, on a concrete contention risk:** while the EPG run's own publication or seal phase (the fact the Discovery lease diagnostics already report as `sealPhase`) is **active and progressing** (its own lease or heartbeat live), the producer does not start the **write units** of a new step; fetching and planning continue, and the yield ends when that phase ends or the EPG run stops progressing, so a failed or stalled EPG never holds enrichment and **no invented timeout** is used. **Reactive:** when a canonical batch fails a **shared authority fence** (the Film/Person marker held or unreadable, a writer-route refusal) or a **contention class** (D1 overload, a reset), that item is deferred with a short bounded backoff and the run continues with the independent work; a repeated contention class backs the whole run off by the ADR 0098 tiers. No concurrent write violates a generation, lease, projector or authority boundary.
- *Evidence required.* Tests and harness measurements show safe enrichment while EPG is active, a contention that yields, an EPG failure with no artificial indefinite hold, recovery, and unchanged EPG priority; the harness reports EPG publication latency with the producer running. A real conflict found there is returned as a trade-off, not hidden behind a new hold or timeout.

**The 72 h fallback is unchanged by local stops.** A handover, a yield, a pause, a lost lease or a resource stop leaves `pending_since` unchanged, consumes no failure, sets no cooldown and writes no transport outcome. An active shared upstream cooldown counts as upstream unavailability, as ADR 0211 accepted.

**Cron delivery, declarative and durable.** `wrangler.jsonc` owns the cron definitions. The reconcile expression is delivered by the **activation change of AIR-607 as a reviewed, versioned configuration change under its owner GO**; AIR-605's deploy carries only the dormant dispatch and binding, which do nothing while the control is inactive. Readback is the trigger list with the control; rollback is the configuration revert and a pause.

**Resource facts, as read on 25 Nov 2026** (Cloudflare documentation, Workers Paid; the repository's `wrangler.jsonc`, handler and `src/discovery-workflow.js`): CPU per invocation defaults to 30 s and can be set up to 5 minutes (the repository sets `cpu_ms` to 120,000); subrequests per invocation or Workflow instance default to 10,000 and are configurable up to 10 million, and a D1 call is one; a Workflow step has unlimited wall time subject to the CPU limit, `step.sleep` is capped at a year, an instance allows 10,000 steps by default and waiting instances do not count toward concurrency; a Cron Trigger invocation is limited to 15 minutes of wall time. The Workers Paid baseline of `AGENTS.md` applies: these are measurements, not gates. TMDB's published protective limit (about 40 requests per second, `429` handling required, per the owner handoff and not fetched here) is respected by the one-request-per-scope rule and the shared cooldown with `Retry-After` and is not read as a throughput allowance; Wikidata's limits are not assessed here.

**Acceptance is the ordinary AIR-605 acceptance by its offline cost harness**, on the real migrated schema with representative mixed-scope fixtures: real resource use, stop reasons, completed Films against arrivals, oldest due age, per-class latency, partial-unit resume, overlap and restart safety, absence of busy loops and starvation, upstream errors and cooldown, and EPG contention. It must show that the design drains more than two Films when resources permit, crosses step and instance boundaries without waiting an hour, wakes on a kick and on a due time without a cron, and sustains the intended arrival workload with headroom; no production capacity is claimed from fixtures, and a real constraint is answered by identifying its bottleneck, not by a manufactured quota.

### 7. Failures and automatic recovery of repairable errors (P5 = B)

ADR 0211's six definite failures and its `needs_attention` stop were an owner-facing manual gate for every definite failure. They are narrowed. Three things are kept distinct: **platform step retries** (capped, default three), **item failure diagnosis**, and **eventual recovery of repairable work**.

| Class | Examples | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| transport | 403, challenge, 429, timeout, network, 5xx, an active cooldown | unchanged: no failure consumed; the 72 h fallback rules are unchanged |
| **repairable** | a response that does not parse, a missing required section, a schema change, a provider or system error, a code defect, **a 404 or other unavailability of an anchored entity** | bounded retries with backoff; after the sixth the item is **`deferred_repair`**, a quarantine with diagnostics and an **automatic recovery condition**, never a hot loop and **never a permanent terminal state** |
| **owner or evidence required** | a **verified** identity conflict or disagreement (the page agreement or another owner proves it), owner-protected data, an ambiguous translation, a truly permanent resolution that needs an owner decision | stays with the owner; no automatic fallback, trust relaxation or retirement |

**A 404 is availability, not identity.** It is recorded as an identity hint, **never a retirement** and not proof of a different Film, and it grants **no definite-failure fallback**; a temporary or misclassified 404 recovers through the repairable path. Only a *verified* conflict is owner-only.

**An unchanged failure is not proof of irreparability.** The same failure signature on the same code version and the same response digest after a recheck stays `deferred_repair` with **bounded, backed-off recovery while it is still plausibly repairable**: a provider may correct its response tomorrow, and an idle catalog has no other observer to show that. The recheck backs off to a **low-frequency cap** (1 hour, then 6, then 24 hours) and **continues**; the failure is never reclassified as permanent.

**Repair events make deferred work runnable and wake it, whatever its `retry_at`.** A **new code version**, a **source recovery** (a successful verified response of the same source and class for another Film), or a **changed response digest** sets the matching `deferred_repair` items runnable (their `retry_at` becomes now, an indexed update) and kicks the producer; the first reconcile after a deploy does so for a new code version, since a deploy is not a causal change the Worker can hook. Each failure records its class, signature, code version and response digest. Recovery is **scoped** to the failed part and source; the settled parts are kept.

**The receipt history cap is a safety limit, not a lifetime stop.** ADR 0207 decision 3 caps one key at 100 receipts and then refuses a new attempt for a person to look at the item. A refresh is event-driven and rare, but a unit refreshed for years could reach it, and a permanent stop would be an unapproved gate. The default safe recovery is a **narrow amendment of that decision, limited to Catalog Enrichment Producer keys** (decision 11), with a **durable, shared logical-unit generation**: the generation `k` is recorded once per **logical unit** (a part of a Film, or a Person unit shared by several Films), so every Film that uses a Person unit sees the same generation and the same key.
- *Rollover* happens only when a reservation is refused for `operation_key_history_exhausted`, the unit has **no unresolved receipt across its applicable history**, and the old history has been **reconciled and validated** (the fingerprint of the old history is recorded). One atomic statement advances `k` to `k+1` **only if** the stored generation and the history fingerprint still equal what was read, so a concurrent writer cannot roll the unit twice or skip an unresolved receipt, and a **late reservation under the old generation is refused** by the same fence.
- *Afterwards* the unit continues under `<key>:g<k+1>`, a key with an empty history; the receipts of the old generation are **retained** as history; a replay or restart **reuses the recorded generation** and never recomputes it; the reconcile-then-reserve protocol is otherwise unchanged and the new key is reserved under the same assertions, so nothing bypasses ADR 0207. No-op writes, which consume no receipt, and the event-driven refresh keep the cap far away in practice.

**Workflow recovery has no hidden manual gate.** The instance-level recovery of a step failure keeps ADR 0098's tiers and its **last tier repeats**, with a terminal `quarantine` that needs a person nowhere, and a new code version retries at once. The explicit 0211-7 = B 72 h transport-only fallback, the known-disagreement override and the trust rules are unchanged.

Fixtures (planned acceptance): six or more parser failures then a real fix; a single idle Film; the same digest on several failed probes then a fixed provider response; a deploy wake; a provider recovery; a changed digest; a temporary 404 that recovers; a permanent invalidity (still rechecked at the cap, never a busy loop); a true conflict (owner only); a crash and restart; no duplicate writes.

### 8. All correctly identified credits (P6 = A)

The catalog retains **every correctly identified credit in the supported roles (actor and director) available from the verified selected source**, with a stable Person identity and dedup by `(person, role)`; existing valid relationships are preserved, nothing is deleted, no role is broadened, no identity is inferred from a name, and no incompatible person namespace is mixed (the ADR 0211 one-namespace rule). The original top-ten-actors choice was a personal display heuristic and is **not** a catalog limit; source completeness is separated from display selection, and **no change to the user interface's top-ten display is made or authorized**. The requirement is the full supported verified relationships, not a claim that a provider supplies every credited person.

**Removing the hidden truncation from the common producer.**
- **The expected set** is every supported credit of the verified response (no 10 and 20). A response that is incomplete (a missing or malformed credits section) is **unreadable**, a repairable failure, and **never** a complete set.
- **Durably paged, not unbounded JSON.** The credits part has **one state row per chunk**, each holding at most 50 person ids with its own digest, so the expected set is paged by construction and a resume reads a page, never the whole set. The unit cap of 50 is kept as the chunk size.
- **Person acquisition has no per-Film cap** (the 30 missing Persons go) and runs one `enrich:person:<person id>` unit per missing Person, as many as the set needs.
- **Chunk keys and intent.** The key `enrich:<film>:credits:<source>:<n>` names the **slot** `n` of the expected set's deterministic order and, like every ADR 0211 key, names the unit and not the plan. The **attempt digest** binds what changes: the exact person ids and ordinals of the chunk, the **expected-set digest and generation**, the anchor digest and the epochs. A refresh, a source-order change, an identity correction or a merge that changes the plan therefore keeps the key and changes the digest, so the reconcile protocol abandons an unresolved receipt of the old plan before reserving a new one (ADR 0207 decision 3); a chunk's ordinal range alone is **not** the intent. The 100-receipt history cap per key is reached only by unusually many refreshes of one slot and is visible, as accepted. AIR-605 is not delivered, so **no receipt exists under the earlier unchunked key** `enrich:<film>:credits:<source>`; were it ever delivered, that key is chunk zero for reconciliation.
- **Add-only, ordinals preserved.** The writer keeps the ordinals of existing credits and appends a new credit after the highest ordinal of its role; the **relative order of the credits it adds follows the source order**, while the display order of existing credits is immutable. Completeness is judged by **person identity**, not by ordinal. Duplicates of a `(person, role)` are one credit.
- **No false completeness.** A part is complete only when **every** expected credit exists. A legacy partial coverage (for example the first ten actors written earlier) is never complete merely because ten exist; the missing credits are added in the one namespace.
- **The four-request cap is per acquisition, not per Film lifetime.** A Film makes at most four upstream requests **per acquisition** (one fetch round for the plan, as accepted). A large Film's **resume does not re-fetch**: the expected set is in the chunk rows, so a resume costs D1 only. A new acquisition (a refresh event) is another round of at most four.
- **Resources.** A step is a bounded group of units (decision 6), progress is durable per unit, and a Film of any size spans as many steps and instances as it needs, never deferred for not fitting.

**Limits found in the actual code and contracts, and their effect.**

| Limit | Where | Effect on the full contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| expected set 10 and 20, Persons 30, "≤ 30 credits (cap 50)" | ADR 0211 decisions 2 and 3 | **amended** (decision 11) |
| the upstream response | TMDB's credits arrays are complete in the response; the Letterboxd page and Wikidata hold what the verified page or item holds | source completeness is what the verified response holds, parsed without a cap; the parsers are checked in AIR-605 |
| the credits unit of 50 | the canonical credit writer | kept as a **chunk** size, not raised |
| the Person unit | ADR 0130: one Person, at most 40 external ids and aliases | per Person, unaffected |
| the EPG chain's refusal of a credit list over 50 | the EPG producer path | unchanged, outside the common producer |
| **the canonical reader fails the whole read above 200 credits** | `src/canonical-catalog-reader.js` lines 95 to 109 (`CREDIT_READ_CAP`, fail closed); **AIR-656 preserves this cap, so it is not an existing solution** | **a genuine prerequisite, below** |
| the read budget of 60 returned credits with `display.credits` partial | ADR 0216 decision 7 | a **read budget, not a persistence cap**; not to be confused with the whole-read refusal |
| the Discovery payload's ten and ten; the UI's top ten | serving and display | not inputs; unchanged |

**The reader prerequisite is explicit.** A Film with more than 200 credits would make the canonical read unavailable. Before the common producer writes more than 200 credits for any Film in production, the canonical reader must deliver **bounded, ordered display and page reads with an accurate partial-completeness report** (the ADR 0216 decision 7 contract), keeping the top ten of the interface, with **no permanent 200-credit storage cap and no silent skipping of a large cast** as a workaround. It is delivered by **amending the scope of the existing reader work** (AIR-656 for the canonical credits fallback, with the shared reader of AIR-647 and its consumer AIR-649 as appropriate), not by an unrelated project; the Coordinator reconciles it after acceptance. It is a **named prerequisite of AIR-607's activation** and of C3c-A, with its own delivery and verification boundary: a fixture of more than 200 credits proven end to end in storage and in every reader.

Fixtures (planned acceptance): a cast over 100 and one or two directors; **more than 200 credits end to end in storage and the readers**; an ensemble beyond ten; many chunks and Persons across a rollover; changes across partial chunks (a refreshed or reordered source, an identity correction, a merge); duplicate `(person, role)`; an incomplete response; legacy partial coverage completed and never marked complete; the one-namespace refusal; stable chunk keys on replay; no effect on small casts (one chunk).

**Apply chain, named.** **M1, AIR-603** (cited by its number only), is the apply of the ordered admission schema, including the receipt operation-key schema of AIR-600, with its own coordinated pending chain; it is **not** an unconditional approval of every later C1 file and nothing else is called AIR-603 here. The apply of **C1's own schema (CM) is [AIR-606](https://trello.com/c/ubBrQe49)**, a **separate** operation with its own readback and its existing scope and AIR-275 dependency; AIR-607 refuses to activate unless every required table is present (ADR 0207 decision 7, fail closed). Any later scope, provenance or Discovery migration is a further separate apply. There is no extra premature apply, and nothing here becomes an AIR-275 prerequisite.

**Costs and recovery scans.** The harness measures, on the real migrated schema, the **trigger fanout and the index and batch costs** (for example the signals one `film_external_id` change writes for the old and new holders of a value), the size of the inbox, and the cost of the bounded recovery scan; the scan is a safety net for the invariants the triggers cover and for lost kicks, bounded and low-frequency, and **not** the ordinary path.

**Coordination and boundaries.** A delivered migration is **never** edited because it has not been applied. AIR-605's schema is not in `main`; carrying the common additions in it needs AIR-605's explicit scope approval, otherwise they are forward migrations in the order above. The Workflow binding and the dormant dispatch are deploy artifacts that create no instance while inactive. **Rollback:** a pause stops all scopes at once; disabling a scope stops its **work** and holds its signals, while its emitter **keeps recording** after the first activation (decision 3); an interrupted or old-version transfer leaves only inert `staged` signals, settled by a later resolver publication or deleted at the next non-authoritative start; written values stay, corrected by the owner and review routes; a code revert stops future runs and leaves the inbox harmless.

## Alternatives considered (not selected; not normative)

- **1A** retain minimal admission and payload serving; **1C** a Discovery-published observation applied by a separate intake (five reviews; the owner selected 1B for a simpler architecture); **1D** widen `film_mutation`.
- **Polling-first with a scan and a maintenance interval** (the revision reviewed at `46fadce`): replaced by the event contract at the owner's direction.
- **A dedicated Discovery ingestion queue, or writers that edit C1's state directly:** rejected as a parallel queue and a cross-domain coupling; the neutral inbox and C1 as sole consumer replace them.
- **Writers append the signal in their own batch instead of triggers:** equivalent, but it misses every path that is not that writer (old Worker versions, manual statements) and must be repeated per writer; triggers cover them at the cost of a small write per change.
- **Admission fact columns (`first_current_at`, `fact_tracked`), a cyclic keyset traversal and an early migration:** withdrawn; the signal row is the fact under an activation-bound stream.
- **A blanket EPG hold, or reading the EPG gate as a stop:** rejected by P4; the producer takes no resolver lease and yields only per item on a real fence or contention.
- **Raising the fixed limits (2 to 20, 25 s to 5 minutes, 12 to another number), hourly-only continuation, a single long Cron Trigger run, a Queue or a Durable Object alarm:** rejected; the Workflow pattern already exists.
- **Keeping the top-ten and top-twenty expected set, or a larger arbitrary cap:** rejected by P6.
- **T1-b** a bounded legacy adoption of payload values: not selected.

## Examples

- **A new Discovery Film.** After C3c-A, `tmdb_123` is inserted into `discovery_admission`: the gated trigger writes `admitted` in the same transaction, and the Discovery code kicks. The producer wakes, creates the membership when the Film's `tmdb` claim exists (an existing Film immediately, a new one when `film_mutation` adds it, which is itself an `anchor_changed`), fetches `movie/123?append_to_response=credits,translations` and writes the core fields, **every** credit, and the `ru` selection, each with C1's own `observed_at`.
- **A pre-activation admission swept later.** Its row was inserted before the activation, so no signal exists. The sweep adds the Film's claim, which emits only `anchor_changed`; the Film has no membership, so the signal is ignored. It is history.
- **An EPG touch, a poster update or a revalidation.** No signal: nothing wakes and nothing is fetched. A verified Discovery revalidation whose digest equals the one C1 applied writes nothing; one whose digest differs marks the part due, and C1 fetches its own fresh response.
- **A superseded admission.** The Film's membership is retained; outstanding work continues. Disabling the Discovery scope stops it; nothing else does.
- **A lid claim appears on a complete Film.** The `film_external_id` trigger writes `anchor_changed` in the claim's own transaction; the producer wakes at once, reopens only Letterboxd's gaps and commits once; with nothing changed no run starts.
- **A burst.** Forty Films arrive; each coalesced signal kicks one producer, which drains them live-first with one history Film in every eight; at the statement budget it hands over to a successor and continues at once; with only deferred work it parks until the earliest due time or a kick; with none it quiesces.
- **An EPG publication runs.** Enrichment continues; one batch fails the Film/Person marker fence, that Film is deferred briefly, the others proceed; the EPG pause of Discovery admission is untouched.
- **A parser break.** A provider changes a section; six Films fail; each becomes `deferred_repair`; the next code version sets them runnable and wakes the producer, and they recover once the code is fixed. If the provider is at fault instead, an unchanged failure keeps being rechecked at a backed-off, capped interval, never a busy loop and never a permanent state, and a corrected response recovers it without anyone's intervention.
- **An ensemble cast.** A Film with 140 cast and two directors writes 142 credits in three chunks of at most 50 and the missing Persons as individual units, across two instances.
- **A lock released.** The owner clears a metadata lock; the trigger writes `protection_released` and the held part is wakened.

## Planned acceptance scenarios and test plan (the AIR-605 harness and C3c-D, on the real migrated schema; these are planned acceptance criteria for the implementation, not claims that any implementation test has run)

- **Signal contract:** the key is normalized and non-null, so a repeated event, including one with no part or source, coalesces to one row (a nullable unique key would not); every emitter of the matrix writes its signal in the **causal transaction** and a rolled-back change writes none; an identical digest writes nothing and wakes nothing; the **version token** and the **atomic acknowledgement** are tested with a re-arm **during consumption** (the signal stays pending at the new version), a failed consumption (it stays pending, retries with backoff, never blocks the others), a stale plan (the epoch rises for every kind, including a same-anchor `source_changed`), a signal dropped only when the digest equals **and** the part is complete, an incomplete part kept due, and **digest parity** between the shared projection and C1's `sourceVersion` for every part and source identity; `anchor_changed` covers an insert, an update of value, trust, conflict and owner (the **old and the new** holders of a value), a delete, a retirement, a redirect and a merge; a protection release emits only on protected to unprotected; EPG touches, payload and poster updates, `generation` moves, status flips, bulk loads and C1's own complete write cycle emit **nothing** (zero inbox changes).
- **Wake races:** the writer commits then kicks, while the instance makes its park intent durable then re-checks; the two orders are exercised so a signal committed at any moment is seen, including one already `pending` while the owner drains, parks or releases; a kick during a handover reaches the successor; a lost kick (a crash after commit, a path without the hook, an old Worker version) is recovered by the reconcile cron; a due `retry_at` or a cooldown end wakes the parked instance at its due time without a cron; an unresolved `admitted` signal does not keep a Workflow spinning and resolves on the claim event, and when the anchor came first it resolves at once.
- **Scopes and bootstrap:** a Discovery insert before the enabling batch is history and one after is stream; a new admission onto an existing Film and a no-op canonical receipt are admitted; a delayed canonical admission resolves on the claim event; a pre-activation admission swept later is not promoted; a genuinely new movie Film in a **successful resolver publication** is admitted when the commit batch settles its `staged` signal (a TV row colliding with a movie id is never staged), a **failed or partial** transfer leaves its signals inert and its **retry** settles them by identity without a second signal, a row staged before the activation and published after it is stream and one published before it is history, a **later steady upsert** of the same Film creates no second signal, a historical row updated by a transfer emits nothing, an **import or restore** (no `context.admission`) has its staged signals deleted and a **later unrelated transfer never promotes** them, an abandoned resolver attempt's facts are promoted only by a later resolver publication, and the activation preflight refuses with a `loading` transfer or stale staged signals; **negative cases** for the consume guard: a guarded-out effect never records an acknowledgement, and a redirect or merge with one enabled membership still resolves; the rated, C3a and C3b **bootstraps** page through the existing members with the trigger enabled first, deduplicate the overlap with the live tail through the unique key, resume after a crash, and stop on a changed scope revision; the Watchlist emitter fires at the `watchlist_film` insert and an unresolved addition in the handoff ledger emits nothing until it resolves; the membership is retained through a superseded admission, an in-flight change, a re-admission, a merge and a lock; a **disabled** scope keeps recording and holds its signals, a **re-enabled** one **catches up** the eligible work with the activation cutoff retained (nothing in the disabled interval becomes history), while a **global pause** only accumulates; overlapping scopes plan one Film with one state set and the existing keys.
- **Implementation must prove (existing review obligations, planned, none executed):** the consume guard is invariant across every effect statement of a batch (or a transaction-aborting assertion is used); the actual transfer provenance (`context.admission`, `origin`, identity), including the retry or recovery of a verified staged resolver admission as distinct from promotion by an unrelated transfer; old-version and recovery safety; and bounded forward progress of the priority selection.
- **Guarded consumption and wake:** a re-arm during consumption with a **stale effect** (no effect is applied and the newest version stays pending); a scope disabled, a control paused or moved, a lease lost, or an identity moved **during consume or commit** applies nothing; a merge during a plan; a **duplicate replay** writes nothing (the guard sees `acked_version >= v`) and an epoch is raised **exactly once**; a stale plan cannot commit after the last enabled membership is disabled; idle unresolved, held or backed-off signals neither keep a run spinning nor cancel a park; the terminal empty-queue exit uses the same park protocol, an **event before the wait** is buffered and delivered, a `sendEvent` to a non-running instance creates one, a lost wake is recovered by the cron, and a lease is reacquired after a wake with generation and epochs re-validated; an observer without credits or translations emits no empty projection and forces no refresh.
- **Receipt cap:** a key at its history cap with no unresolved receipt advances its generation and continues; one with an unresolved receipt first reconciles it; the guard is never bypassed.
- **Priority:** a Film's class follows the three-step precedence of decision 5 from the authoritative facts at pick time (a history-only Film with unknown facts is background; a live-cohort Film with unknown facts is live), the indexed due selection examines at most `K` rows per cohort and a failing background item leaves the head so it never blocks independent live work, a retained Discovery Film that stopped airing moves to background without losing eligibility, an old arrival keeps no permanent priority, background work progresses under sustained live arrivals measured in **committed units and progressed Films, not starts**, a failing or huge Film neither burns nor starves the background share; per-class latency, oldest due age and background progress are reported.
- **EPG:** safe enrichment while EPG is active; the proactive yield during an active, progressing seal phase and its end when the phase ends or stalls; the reactive yield on a held marker and a contention class; an EPG failure or stall is no stop; recovery; the EPG pause of Discovery admission is unchanged; EPG publication latency is measured with the producer running.
- **Recovery:** six or more parse failures then a real fix; an idle single Film; the same digest on several failed probes then a fixed provider response; a **deploy wake**, a source recovery and a changed digest each set the deferred items runnable and wake them whatever their `retry_at`; a temporary 404 recovers and is never a retirement or a fallback; an unchanged failure stays `deferred_repair` with backed-off recovery and **never** becomes permanent; a true conflict stays with the owner; no busy loop; a crash and restart and a replay write nothing twice; local stops never count toward the 72 h fallback while an active cooldown does.
- **Credits:** a cast over 100 and one or two directors; **more than 200 credits end to end in storage and in every reader**; an ensemble beyond ten; many chunks and Persons across a rollover; changes across partial chunks (a refreshed or reordered source, an identity correction, a merge); duplicate `(person, role)`; an incomplete response is unreadable and never complete; legacy partial coverage completed and not marked complete; the one-namespace refusal; stable chunk keys and a changed attempt digest on a changed plan; a resume reads D1 and makes no upstream request; no effect on small casts.
- **Execution:** more than two Films drain when resources permit; step and instance boundaries are crossed without an hour's wait; the statement, step and subrequest counters (failing and replayed calls included) hand over; a Film needing many steps is never deferred for not fitting; the handover cases (a create with a lost response, a crash between create and release, an expired lease during a park or sleep) behave as specified; no repeated empty handover.
- **Control and delivery:** the dormant dispatch does nothing while inactive; the cron comes only from the versioned configuration and survives deploys; the per-scope flags gate their emitters; old-version compatibility of every added object; **trigger fanout, index and batch costs** on the real migrated schema; the bounded recovery scan's cost.

## Consequences

- One producer enriches rated, Watchlist, Discovery and later historical Films, woken by the change that causes the work: no polling-first delay, no second queue, no handoff.
- ADR 0211 is amended in six decisions and ADR 0214 in one, narrowly and only on acceptance; the shared upstream coordination and the 72 h fallback are unchanged.
- One small inbox and its triggers are the cost of an event-driven contract; the admission fact columns and cyclic traversal are gone.
- The catalog keeps all supported credits; a canonical reader change outside this record is a coordinated prerequisite of writing more than 200 credits in production.
- Each Discovery Film costs a TMDB request Discovery has already made once; the owner accepted this for simplicity.
- The producer is a work-conserving drainer; real throughput and EPG effect are shown by the harness, not claimed here.
- History is not written; HA plans it, and a later HR needs its own decision.

## Recovery and migration boundary

This record authorizes nothing: no migration, trigger, control or scope change, no activation, no cron, flag or Task change, no production fetch or write, and no card creation. Each card of decision 10 has its own gate. Code rollback does not remove written values or signals; correction is through the ADR 0214 owner route or a forward write.
