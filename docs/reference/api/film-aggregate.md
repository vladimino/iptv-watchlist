# FilmAggregateV1 (internal, dormant)

The maintained contract of the unified Film read of [ADR 0216](../../adr/0216-canonical-film-lifecycle-and-unified-read-contract.md) (Accepted), delivery graph step 3 ([AIR-647](https://trello.com/c/WKSwJOwm)). The typed resolver and identity fence it builds on are delivery graph step 2 ([AIR-646](https://trello.com/c/OzZ7uZZj)); see the [Film/Person reference](../domain/film-person.md#unified-film-read-contract-adr-0216-accepted-resolver-and-sections-delivered-dormant).

**Status: dormant.** `src/film-aggregate.js` and `src/film-aggregate-resolver.js` are not in the Worker bundle (`build.mjs` does not name them, nothing outside the two modules and their tests imports them), so no deployed route answers this shape and no consumer is switched. The handler `handleFilmAggregateRequest` exists for the tests and for activation. AIR-647 is **offline delivery only**: code, tests, documentation and one forward-only migration file. Activation is [AIR-648](https://trello.com/c/zh0cfDWK), with its own owner GO and a mandatory pre-activation schema gate on **two indexes**: `tmdb_retirement_decision_movie` (migration 0040, [AIR-667](https://trello.com/c/SbV827kN), delivered offline) for the identity resolver's retirement reads, and `film_redirect_by_target` (migration 0043, this card, [runbook](../../runbooks/film-redirect-target-index-0043-apply.md)) for the display section's owner ancestry. Both are applied with the other approved pending migrations in a coordinated pre-activation window, each under its own approval: 0039 to 0041 belong to the M1 chain of [AIR-603](https://trello.com/c/wc7pHJTB); 0042 belongs to ECM ([AIR-638](https://trello.com/c/JyQ3UkbR)), whose card allows combining it with another apply only with exact files and a separate GO; 0043 is AIR-647's, verified by AIR-648 before activation. A coordinated pre-activation window may cover 0039 to 0043, but each card keeps its own approval and evidence boundary: M1 does not authorize 0042 or 0043, ECM does not authorize 0043, and a combined apply needs the exact files and a separate owner GO. AIR-648 reads both back from production before activation, reusing the evidence of whichever card applied them and never applying again, and stops if either is absent. Nothing here authorizes an apply, an activation or a live call. Bundling needs the identity digest moved from `node:crypto` to Web Crypto; that belongs to activation.

## Request

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Route (when activated) | `GET` or `HEAD /api/internal/film-aggregate?id=<typed id>` |
| Typed id | `film:<id>`, `tmdb_<n>`, `tmdb_tv:<n>` or a bare lid (ADR 0216 decision 2). Exactly one `id` parameter; anything else is `400 invalid`. |
| Other methods | `405` with `allow: GET, HEAD`; nothing is read |
| Caching | `cache-control: no-store` |
| Side effects | none (ADR 0107): only single plain `SELECT` statements (optionally behind a `WITH`) are prepared; the sections' binding refuses anything else before it reaches D1. No R2, no network. |

## Response

`{contract: "film-aggregate/v1", identity, display, watchlist, availability, personal, predictions, history}` with HTTP `200` for the identity verdicts `found` and `redirected`. Every other verdict (`400 invalid`, `404 absent`, `409 conflict|unverified|cycle`, `410 retired`, `503 unavailable`) returns `contract` and `identity` only, with no section. `503 identity_moved` (the final identity fence moved or could not run) also returns no section.

Every section has a `status` (`present`, `absent`, `unknown`, `unavailable`, `partial`, `conflict`; `limited` only for `history`), its `source`, and its `token`. An unreadable token is `token: null, tokenStatus: "unavailable"` with the section `unavailable`.

| Section | Reads (authorized aliases only) | Token | Negative is stated only when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `display` | `film`, `film_metadata`, `film_localization_source` and the localization `film_dimension` row (every column the mapping reads), the **owner-review entries of the survivor and of every Film of its redirect tree** (below), `film_person_credit` (60; the shared bounded selection of AIR-656: one indexed probe per role, directors first, each item carries the Person's usable `name` or `null`, an unnamed credit is kept in place) | metadata `content_sha256`, digest of the redirect edges, digest of the owner rows, digest of the localization rows | the metadata fields are never `absent`: per-field provenance (ADR 0214) is not delivered, so a missing or null field is `unknown` |
| `watchlist` | `watchlist_authority`, `watchlist_current`, the current snapshot's `watchlist_member`, `watchlist_admission_pending`, `watchlist_membership_handoff`; projection `watchlist_membership_authority`, `watchlist_film`, `watchlist_film_fence` | source generation, `version`, `snapshotId`, `listSha256`; projection mode and generation | `source.member: false` only with source `d1`, open writes, a complete snapshot (member rows equal `film_count`), a non-empty lid set read **in full**, and no unsettled listed lid (an admission row not `resolved`/`superseded`, or a handoff row not `settled`, of any due time) |
| `availability` | `published_epg_show` of the pinned `programme_run_id` for the storage ids (50 rows) | `programmeRunId`, `generation`, show-rows digest | the empty `current` list is proved by the pinned generation; no authorized storage id is `unknown` |
| `personal` | `ratings_view` of the storage ids, with the ratings coverage below | ratings generation, view-rows digest, coverage token | `watched: false` or a known null rating (`absent`) only when **every** authorized alias has a row that says so and coverage is complete |
| `predictions` | `prediction_pointer` of the storage ids (current only; the R2 body is named by `body.r2Key` and `body.contentSha256`, immutable, not read inline) | prediction generation, pointer-rows digest, coverage token | `absent` (`reason: none`) only with no pointer under any authorized key and complete coverage |
| `history` | nothing | none | never `present` in v1 |

**Storage ids** are each authorized lid and `tmdb_<n>` of the trusted, non-retired movie claim. A TV number is never a storage key. The resolver keeps at most 8 aliases per scheme; with more, the aliases that were not read are unexamined candidates: coverage is `incomplete` (`discovery_cap`, `skippedAliases: true`), the storage-id sections are `partial` with `reason: budget_exceeded`, and the Watchlist answers `unknown` with `reason: alias_overflow` unless a read lid is listed (then `member: true`, section `partial`). `alias_overflow` is added to the reasons of ADR 0216 decision 5 for that case.

**Several aliases (C2).** Values are grouped by ratings fact key, so one fact reachable through two aliases is one value with every `storageId`. Two different current values (two ratings, two predictions) are `conflict` with each value and its `storageId`; no value is chosen and no key is renamed. An alias with no `ratings_view` row, or whose row says nothing (`watched` NULL, rating not known), is **unknown** (`unknownAliases`): it is never negative evidence, a value or a negative is stated only when no alias is unknown and coverage is complete, and a known conflict is kept whatever else is unknown.

**History (decision 6a).** `limited` with `href` to the legacy `GET /api/enrichment/history?filmId=` only when all hold, taken from the same fenced identity read-set and computed **after** every section fence: the survivor has exactly one authorized lid, coverage is `complete`, the request is that lid or the survivor's `tmdb_<n>` and is not a redirect, and the legacy bridge (`catalogFilmIdentityBridge`) would accept the Film: none of its `tmdb` or `letterboxd` claims is untrusted, conflicting or retired (the bridge still counts a retired one, so it is treated as not proved) and it holds at most one claim of each scheme. Otherwise `unavailable`, `reason: history_migration_pending`, no `href`; a ratings movement during the read withdraws the `href` too. The route is never invoked. The multi-alias history that will replace this link is delivered offline and dormant ([AIR-650](https://trello.com/c/gYGqljS9), [film history](film-history.md#multi-alias-history-air-650-offline-not-live)); it is not live, and this section stays `limited` or `unavailable` and never `present` until the consumer migration ([AIR-651](https://trello.com/c/qOmC8529)) is separately accepted.

## Owner ancestry of the display title (ADR 0216 decisions 5 and 7)

`owner_review_entry.identity_link` is a snapshot that a Film merge does not rewrite ([ADR 0081](../../adr/0081-d1-authoritative-owner-reviews.md)), so the owner values of a Film are those of every link in the redirect **tree** that ends at it, whatever alias the request used. The display statement walks `film_redirect` backwards from the survivor with a recursive CTE through `film_redirect_by_target` (migration 0043), and finds the entries through `owner_review_entry_identity` (named in the statement, so the plan never falls back to a range of every `film` entry).

- **Bounds, inside the statement, on the work and not only on the output.** A recursive step enqueues every row its query returns for a dequeued parent, so a `LIMIT` on the CTE alone would cap the rows returned while the queue still took every child of a high-fan-out Film. The step therefore probes `film_redirect_by_target` per dequeued parent with its own `LIMIT` (65 children: more than that under one parent is already an overflow), and the CTE stops at depth 8 + 1 and at 64 ancestors + 2 dequeued rows (the survivor, its ancestors, and one more row that proves an overflow); the owner entries are `LIMIT`ed to 64 + 1. Parents dequeued times children probed is a constant, so the VM work of the statement does not grow with the fan-out or the depth of the data (measured in the tests as VM steps for stars of 64, 1 000 and 20 000 children, a tree with thousands of grandchildren under every child, and a chain of 3 000). An overflow of any bound is typed (`display.fields.title` is `unknown` with `owner_ancestry_bounded` or `owner_entries_bounded`, `display.ancestry.status` is `bounded`) and is never read as "no more ancestors". Exactly 8 levels and exactly 64 ancestors are read.
- **Precedence.** Owner value (any entry of the tree) > reviewed dictionary > automatic, through the loader's own mapping. Two different owner values anywhere in the tree are `conflict` with each value and its entry, no winner; equal values agree. Entries that are not `verified`, or are linked to a Film outside the tree, are not read.
- **Result.** With a complete ancestry and the rows within their bounds, `title` is `present` with its `provenance` tier and `source`; `display.ancestry` is `{status: "complete", films, depth}`. `display.status` stays `partial` while the metadata fields are `unknown`.
- **Fence.** The statement returns the edges (child, parent, depth), the owner rows and every localization column in one snapshot; their digests are the section token (`ancestry`, `ownerReview`, `localization`) and the after-read compares the whole result. A new, removed or re-pointed edge anywhere in the tree, a new or changed owner entry (including on a Film with no entry before) and a change of a consumed localization column make the section `unavailable` with `generation_moved`; an edge outside the tree does not. A leaf has no child row, so a child that appears under it appears in the edges.
- **Budget.** The ancestry adds no statement: it is part of the one display statement and its after-read, inside the shared 40-statement budget and the reserved identity fence.

## Candidate coverage (decision 6b, G1)

`personal` and `predictions` carry `coverage: {status, reason?, token, candidates, notes?}`. Discovery reads the D1-owned ratings documents only at ratings `d1`/`open`:

1. Seeds are the authorized aliases in link-index form (`letterboxd:<lid>`, `tmdb:<n>`).
2. `letterboxd/facts/links.json` is read by **one statement that measures and returns it in the same snapshot**: a CTE measures the key's parts, bytes and distinct etags over the primary key, and the part rows are returned only when parts are at most 40, bytes at most 2.4 MB and there is one etag. A document that is over the cap, or that was replaced by a larger one at any moment, is never returned (`discovery_cap`); the Worker never materializes more than the cap. The measure reads the part rows server-side (bounded by the document, not returned). The text is then verified (parts in order, one etag, SHA-256 of the text) and must be a JSON object.
3. A seed missing from the index is an absence predicate.
4. The fact records are read by the same bounded statement (4 distinct records per request). **The existing reader has no size cap for a record, so this card states one: 8 parts (480 000 bytes) per record**; a larger record is not read (`discovery_cap`). A record must carry the minimum recognized envelope: a plain JSON object with a **required** `links` object that names at least one valid identity, a Letterboxd id (LID-only), a TMDB movie id (TMDB-only: the owner's watched mark of [ADR 0032](../../adr/0032-owner-watched-mark-by-tmdb-identity.md) writes `letterboxd: null` and is a legitimate record) or both; every non-null member is validated by its own type (a lid string that is not spelled `tmdb_<n>`; a TMDB id as a positive-integer string or number, never an array or an object coerced to a string), and both null or absent is no identity. `schemaVersion` must be 1 when present, `factKey` must equal the key when present, and the record holds at most one edge, `redirectTo` or `migrating.to`, a valid key (both, a missing target or a malformed one is malformed). Two legacy tolerances are kept: an absent `factKey` (as the ADR 0184 planner tolerates it) and an absent `schemaVersion`. An arbitrary JSON object, `{}` included, is not a fact record and never certifies a traversal (`ratings_evidence_unavailable`). This is the shape check G1 needs, not the promotion-grade link proof of ADR 0184. Each root (the fact key of a seed) is walked on its own chain: one redirect or migration hop is followed and both sides count; a second hop (also when another seed reads the second record) is `discovery_cap`; an edge back to a key of the chain, including itself, is a cycle. Records read are counted before they are read (root records plus their hop targets against the cap of 4). The index is walked once (50 000 entries) for every other alias of the reached keys.
5. A candidate is settled only by another Film's single trusted, non-conflicting claim of that value, the Film present and not a redirect source, and for a movie id no non-superseded retirement. The survivor's own retired movie claim is not settlement. The claim rows and the retirement decisions read are `LIMIT`ed in SQL to 64 each (one more proves an overflow): more is `discovery_cap`, never a partial settlement.

`complete` needs all of: a non-empty seed set, no skipped alias, every seed present, the document within its bounds, every record valid and within its bounds, the traversal finished and every candidate settled. Otherwise `incomplete` with `ratings_evidence_unavailable` (held, unreadable, missing, malformed, cyclic or mixed-etag evidence), `discovery_cap`, `seed_absent_unproved` or `unresolved_candidate`; `notes` names unresolved candidates. A candidate's value is never returned. Incomplete coverage makes the section `partial` (never `absent` or a single current value) unless authorized values already conflict, which stays `conflict`.

## Consistency and budget

- **Identity fence.** The resolver's final capture re-reads the identity read-set after every section; a change returns `503 identity_moved` with no section.
- **Per-section fence (E1).** Each section reads its data **and** its tokens in one statement (so the token names exactly the rows the answer was built from) and re-reads that statement after all section reads. A difference makes that section `unavailable`, `reason: generation_moved`. The display statement carries every column the localization mapping consumes, and the title is built from those rows with the loader's own mapping (`localizationInputsFromRows`). The ratings after-read covers the marker, the etag of every document read, the `ratings_view` rows and the candidate settlement, and moves `personal`, `predictions` and the `history` link together. Availability fences the show rows too, because Discovery can change a run's rows without moving the generation.
- **Failure isolation.** A section whose read, parse or processing fails is `unavailable` with its own typed reason (`display_unavailable`, `watchlist_unavailable`, `published_epg_unavailable`, `prediction_unavailable`, `personal_unavailable`, `ratings_unavailable`) and gives its reserved statement back; the other sections are unaffected. A ratings failure degrades coverage (`ratings_evidence_unavailable`), so `predictions` becomes `partial`, and never erases an independent section. The resolver's generic `sectionsUnavailable` fallback is not reached by a section failure.
- **Statements.** At most 40 D1 statements per request, counted on the binding. The identity fence is reserved first; each section reserves its after-statement before its first read; the ratings coverage reads run first. A section that cannot get its statements is `unavailable`, `budget_exceeded`. A typical request uses about 20.
- **Index use.** Every statement is served by a primary key or an existing index, checked by `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` in `tests/film-aggregate.mjs` on a database built from the real chain, which allows no scan of a base table or of an index (only the bound key lists, a constant row, derived tables, the recursive queue and the measuring CTE of the bounded document read). The retirement reads use `tmdb_retirement_decision_movie` (migration 0040) and the ancestry step `film_redirect_by_target` (migration 0043); both migrations are delivered offline and applied in the pre-activation window above.

## Lid-only Film: `predictions.eligibility` (ADR 0217 decision 9, choice M1, AIR-673, dormant)

A Film minted `film:lb:<lid>` is read by `film:lb:<lid>` and by the bare lid; `tmdb_<n>` answers `absent` (404) until an attach, and the permalink is `/film/<lid>` until a trusted movie claim exists. The `predictions` section keeps ADR 0216 sections 5 and 6b unchanged: known historical values and conflicts under authorized aliases are reported as read, `none` only under full negative proof, coverage as it is.

Eligibility is a **separate** field of that section, `predictions.eligibility`:
- `{status: "not_eligible", reason: "lid_only_deferred"}` when the survivor has a trusted `letterboxd` claim, no trusted `tmdb` claim and a `film_lid_admission` row (itself or one of its redirect sources). It never turns a present or conflicting value into `partial` and never invents incompleteness.
- Absent after a successful attach (a trusted `tmdb` claim): the ordinary ADR 0154 gates decide, and the id of the Film does not change.
- `{status: "unknown", reason: "budget_exceeded" | "eligibility_unavailable"}` when the one extra statement cannot be spent or read; never an eligible answer. An ordinary movie Film carries no `eligibility` key and spends no extra statement. A table not yet applied means no lid-only Film exists.

The claim rows decide, never the spelling of the id.

## Derived readiness (ADR 0218 decision 4, AIR-665, dormant)

`src/film-readiness.js` derives `displayReady`, `catalogReady` and `predictionReady` from the response body above, and `src/film-readiness-composition.js` (`readFilmReadiness`) composes the real `readFilmAggregate` read with it. Both read nothing themselves and add no statement to the 40-statement budget (asserted in `tests/film-aggregate.mjs`: identical D1 query count, byte-identical database). The rules, the checked prediction-domain handoff (observation versus independently supplied context) and the placement of the D1 reader switch (aggregate activation, AIR-648) are in the [Film/Person reference](../domain/film-person.md#readiness-is-derived-not-a-new-entity-status). They add no field to the `film-aggregate/v1` response.
