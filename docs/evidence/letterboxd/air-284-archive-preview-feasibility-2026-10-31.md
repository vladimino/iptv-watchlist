## Seeded R2 objects

These are the production sizes named in the [AIR-169 reconciliation](air-169-reconciliation-2026-10-26.md) (2,460 films, 420 accepted).

- Heap after a collection was 12.1 MB before and after every request: nothing is retained between previews.
- Responses were 15–44 KB.
- **Capped heap (Node `vm`, `--max-old-space-size`, all five cases in one process):** fails at 40 and 48 MiB of old space and passes at 52, 56 and 64 MiB. This is a relative bound for the same code, not workerd's accounting.

## Reading

- **CPU and time.** Under one second of CPU for the worst accepted archive, against a 120,000 ms limit; the reader's own 20 s wall budget was never approached.
- **Memory.** One worst-case preview peaks at about 58 MB of live heap and about 110 MB of heap committed, inside the 128 MB isolate but not with room for two. That is why the route allows one preview per isolate (429 `preview_busy`).
- **The row cap came from this probe.** With 20,000 rows per file the same worst case peaked at about 103 MB used and 148 MB committed. Lowering the cap to 10,000 and releasing each CSV text once parsed gave the figures above. The owner's export is about a quarter of the new cap.
- **The upload's raw bytes** are the largest buffer (40 MB for a 9.9 MiB archive: the body, the form part and the copy for the reader). They are released before the comparison.

## Uncertainty

- Local workerd, not the production edge; the edge's memory accounting and request body limit were not measured.
- Heap samples are sparse during synchronous work (two or three per request); the true peak can be higher than sampled.
- workerd's process RSS (132–327 MB) is not the isolate's: V8 keeps freed pages and the local R2 simulator runs in the same process.
- The probe's R2 objects are synthetic; titles, years and identities are invented, so match rates say nothing about the owner's data.

**Authority:** none. This report authorizes no upload of a real archive, import, R2 or D1 write, or Access change.
