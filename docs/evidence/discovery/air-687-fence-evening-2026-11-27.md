## Change

1. Two decisions are kept apart. **Write safety** is unchanged: the refused hop writes nothing more, and the next chunk takes its own lease and is fenced again on every read and write; decisions and assignments are idempotent per key. **Wake** is decided by `assessFenceRefusal`, a read-only assessment (pointer, authority gate and stored lease compared by id and expiry; no renewal, no write, tested): another chunk is needed whenever the refusal can still clear, for every state except a positively superseded run. A lost or expired hop lease, a pointer that is missing, not `published` or unreadable, a not-ready, held or loading authority and unreadable state are re-evaluated; only the pointer naming another run as `published` or the authority bound to another run (`run_superseded`, `other_programme`) keeps the fail-closed `error` record. A held authority refuses the checkpoint write itself and is not retried behind it. The first delivery's review found the earlier "safe" framing conflated the two; the first delivery also called `renewDiscoveryLease` (a fenced lease mutation) while documenting a read-only check, which is corrected here.
2. The wait: 30 s, doubling per consecutive re-evaluation, capped at the existing five-minute backoff of a chunk that made no progress. There is no count-based hour tier (the earlier 1 min / 5 min / 15 min / 1 h ladder is removed): whatever blocks is re-read every time, work resumes within the cap once the obstruction ends, and a persistent refusal costs at most twelve light chunks an hour and stays visible through `fenceCollisions`, `fenceCause`, `fenceBasis` and the monitor's zero-work loop rule. The values are engineering intervals with this rationale, not product limits.
3. A busy without its own deadline, for the Workflow executor only (`caller: internal`), waits for the live lease's expiry, else one short recheck; external callers keep the hourly contract.
4. The Workflow state write: a same-owner lost compare-and-swap of a progress, final stop or recovery-arming checkpoint is contention, not lost ownership. After the three immediate retries the step sleeps a short durable interval (the existing window-write fallback) and re-reads the record, ownership and revision; the chunk is never repeated and its report contribution is keyed by the chunk, so it counts once. Another owner still ends the instance quietly; the existing step cap still ends a contention that outlasts it as deferred for the reconcile.
5. When the final status write is the refused one, the chunk's real counts travel into the re-evaluation checkpoint (report once, real progress for the monitor).
6. Diagnostics (bounded, no SQL or data): `fenceRevalidation`, `fenceCause`, `fenceBasis`, `fenceCollisions`, `failedAt` in the chunk summary and the continuation.

## Residual paths (listed, not changed)

- A typed `discovery_unavailable` (an unknown write outcome, for example an acknowledgement lost after a commit) keeps its existing contract: the next chunk's readback and the per-key idempotence reconcile it, but it is not a fence and still takes the existing `error` path; the owner's evening failure was a fence.
- A step cap exhausted by Workflow-state contention, a held authority refusing the checkpoint write, a lost lease before the chunk (`LeaseLostError`) and a genuine platform failure keep their existing recovery (reconcile or stall recovery); nothing here relaxes a fence or a lease.
