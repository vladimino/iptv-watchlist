## Rules that apply to every step

- **Windows.** No operation or deployment of this plan between **16:30 and 17:00 Europe/Madrid** (the 16:30 Letterboxd slot, the 17:00 daily EPG and its pinned last-good snapshot). No two authority cutovers share a window: B and C do not share a window with each other, with the AIR-551 activation or with any EPG, Ratings, Discovery or Predictions cutover. Madrid time is stated with UTC in every receipt.
- **Receipt per mutation.** Before: a Time Travel bookmark and a fresh read-only `status`. After: the operator's readback, a second bookmark and an independent read of the facts the step claims. A step whose outcome is `write_uncertain` is never repeated: stop, read, and ask for a new GO with new pins.
- **No forcing.** No manual EPG, Ratings, Letterboxd, Discovery, TMDB or Predictions run, no lease reset, no retry, no flag, no Scheduled Task. Natural runs only.
- **The old schedule stays.** The daily Ratings executor, its Cloudflare Workflow and the legacy `ratings_sync_*` entry are not removed before acceptance (O5); they are fenced by the mode protocol, not deleted.
- **One writer.** After every handover command `GET /api/letterboxd/sync/mode` shows the admitted writers; one is the goal, zero is a visible state, two must never be reached by these commands.

## The canary stage (F1) and the transition to the full schedule (F2)

The permanent hourly `30 * * * *` trigger would otherwise start the next four slots automatically whatever the first one did. The canary hold makes the gate enforceable inside the existing control plane (the mode row; no new state, table or endpoint):

- **Chosen before admission.** The owner route is FAIL-CLOSED: `complete` toward `cloudflare` REQUIRES the `slot` (`canary_required` otherwise, nothing written) and writes the completion and the hold `canary:<slot>` in ONE row write, so there is no interval in which the writer runs without a hold, even with the `30 * * * *` cron already deployed. The slot is the first natural Madrid slot after the activation deployment, for example `2026-11-23T1030` (a daytime slot outside the 16:30 to 17:00 embargo, so the observation is attended). Refused: `canary_required`, `invalid_slot`, `slot_in_past`.
- **The `canary` command** (slot, confirm, pins) is the recovery and retarget path on a running writer: it sets a hold where there is none, or REPLACES a settled one in one pinned write (the full schedule is never open in between). Replacement is allowed only when the previous canary is **settled**: never claimed and its window ended (a missed canary), closed (finished or failed), or the closed hold `canary:none`. An in-progress canary is preserved: `canary_active` with the reason `canary_pending` (still ahead), `canary_in_progress` (running inside its window) or `canary_instance_live` (the window ended but its instance has not ended). Also refused: `same_slot`, `slot_used` (that slot already has a row), `slot_in_past`, `invalid_slot`. `GET` shows `canary`.
- **Every return to `cloudflare` is closed.** An abort back to `cloudflare` through the owner route carries the hold, or ends in the closed hold `canary:none` when there was none; a later forward `complete` again needs a slot. A closed hold matches no slot, so nothing is claimed; `open_schedule` is refused on it (`canary_not_set`): the ONLY way from a closed schedule to one admitted slot is `canary` (or `complete` with a slot), and the only way to the full schedule is `open_schedule` on a real canary hold, a separate owner GO.
- **What the hold enforces.** `ensure` still delivers pending Events and runs the root mirror pass, but claims and creates ONLY the named slot. For every other slot it answers `canary_hold` and records nothing (no claim, no instance, no `skipped_missed` slot or Events). The Ratings, scan and effects work of the canary slot is the normal slot work. `drain` and `resume` keep the hold; an abort back to `cloudflare` keeps it (the marker is carried through the switching row), so no path opens the full schedule silently.
- **Expected observation.** The first slot at the named time (the `:30` tick at or after the slot's due instant claims it; a slot missed entirely is never run late), then: slot status closed, `ratings` and `watchlist` outcomes with exactly two Events, `delivery.mirror` delivered, the legacy Ratings entry fenced, the monitor quiet, the 17:00 EPG and Discovery unchanged.
- **Stop and reversal.** At any time: `drain`, then the reverse order. The hold also holds the schedule while the owner decides, so waiting costs nothing.
- **The explicit gate.** Only after the canary is accepted does the owner issue `open_schedule` (a separate GO, confirm, fresh pins). From that command on the natural ticks claim the four daily slots. To re-run a canary after a failed, missed or stopped one: `canary` again with a new slot (the retarget above; never `open_schedule` first).

## E in detail: the activation deployment

The code is already in `main` and dormant. What the deployment changes is configuration only, in ONE reviewed commit that the owner approves before it is deployed:

1. `wrangler.jsonc`: add the Workflow registration `{"name": "iptv-letterboxd", "binding": "LETTERBOXD_WORKFLOW", "class_name": "LetterboxdWorkflow"}` and add `"30 * * * *"` as the FOURTH expression of `triggers.crons`, keeping the existing three unchanged and in order (a Wrangler deploy REPLACES the cron triggers with this array). The Worker dispatches `30 * * * *` to the Letterboxd `ensure` and to nothing else (`LetterboxdSync.CRON`).

3. `docs/reference/feature-flags.md` and `docs/runbooks/deployment.md`: the binding is not a flag; record the fourth cron and the Workflow. No new flag is introduced.
4. **Before the deploy:** the preflight above, run inside a window outside 16:30 to 17:00 Madrid and not at a tick boundary (minute 29 to 31 UTC) so no `ensure` tick is mid-flight. **After the deploy:** `GET` the schedules (exactly four, the first three with unchanged `created_on`), `GET` the Workflows list (the Letterboxd Workflow present), and one natural `ensure` tick observed in the read model.
5. The mode must already be `cloudflare` with the canary hold set (D2) or the tick does nothing (`external`) or would start the full schedule: the deployment is NOT approved while the read model shows `cloudflare` without a `canary`. With a binding but a mode that is not `cloudflare`, a firing creates no instance.

## Rollback

- **Before D2** nothing writes the Letterboxd side: stop at the last read-back step. B is ignored while the marker is `epg`; C1 backs out with `--release-abort`.
- **After C2 (data rollback), in this order:** `drain` (wait for the effects drained on a natural slot), `close_admission` toward `external`, wait for the drain of the instances, `complete` toward `external`, then `hold-rollback`, `export-rollback`, `fence-root-rollback`, `revert-authority`, `release-rolled-back`. The legacy Ratings entry stays fenced by the Watchlist state until the marker is `epg`/`open`; only then is the Ratings executor re-opened through its own route. Each step is its own GO.
- **After E:** remove the fourth cron and the binding by a deployment of the previous configuration (the three existing crons stay), then the reverse order above for the data. Code rollback alone does not revert D1 rows, R2 objects, markers, the mode row, slots, outbox rows or Events.
- **Generation changes.** Every marker transition that bumps the generation invalidates earlier pins: a GO is only valid for the generation named in it, and the status is read again before each command.
