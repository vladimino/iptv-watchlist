# AIR-367: offline evidence of the canonical Film/Person foundation

**Captured:** 2026-11-07 (UTC). **Method:** offline tests on a D1 stand-in over the sqlite3 CLI (`tests/film-person-authority.mjs`), the full `npm test`, and `npm run build`. No production read or write, no schema apply, no flag, no R2 access. The marker, the fence, the revisions and the mutation API are **not bundled into the Worker** and nothing calls them.

## What was verified

- **Migration `0013`.** It refuses a database below a complete version 12 (versions 9 to 11 tested) before it creates a table; on a complete version 12 it creates the four tables, inserts the marker as `r2` / `open` / generation 0, an `empty` baseline and a fence row, copies no Film or Person, sets schema 13, and cannot be applied twice or downgraded.
- **State machine in SQL.** Only `r2`→`prepared`, `prepared`→`r2`, `prepared`→`d1`, `d1`→`held` and `held`→`d1`, one generation each. Refused: `r2`→`d1`, `held` before `d1`, a skipped or repeated generation, any move out of `d1` or `held` to `r2` or `prepared`, a delete, a second row, a bogus mode. A move out of `r2` is refused unless `film_person_baseline.state` is `verified`; the baseline moves only `empty`→`loading`→`verified` and cannot reopen while the marker is `prepared` or `d1`, so `prepared` and `d1` always stand on a verified baseline. A partial (`loading`) baseline cannot become `prepared`, from SQL or from `transitionAuthority`.
- **Fail-closed reads.** No table, a version below 13 and a missing binding are `legacy` (R2 allowed). From version 13: a dropped table, a missing marker, fence or baseline row, a read error and a malformed row throw `film_person_unavailable`; `held` throws `film_person_held` and never names R2; only `r2`, `prepared` and `legacy` name R2.
- **Mutations refuse unless `open`.** In `r2`, `prepared`, `held`, legacy and an unreadable marker the three operations raise before any statement (zero batches, nothing written).
- **Fences and revisions.** One atomic batch proves the marker generation and the expected revision of every touched entity. Refused whole with nothing persisted: a stale revision, a marker that went `held` between the read and the write, a generation that moved and returned to `open`, an entity bumped by another writer between the read and the write. Revisions move by exactly one; an unknown outcome is never retried blindly.
- **Credits.** An existing credit keeps its ordinal; a new one is appended after the highest of its role (after a D1-only credit); an explicit free ordinal is taken; a taken ordinal, an unknown person and a collision refuse the batch whole; a removal must name the credit; the D1-only TMDB credit (AIR-286) is never deleted by a roster write; reads per mutation do not grow with the roster.
- **Typed ids and conflicts.** `tmdb` and `tmdb_tv` with the same number coexist; a second owner of a trusted `(scheme, value)` is `identity_collision` with nothing persisted; a conflicting holder is never trusted.
- **Owner values.** An owner localization row survives every mutation (the API writes no localization, dimension, metadata, watchlist link, fingerprint or prediction).
- **Merge.** Two entities, all or nothing: revisions of both proven and both moved; the survivor may not be a redirect and a self merge is refused; the retired Film may not be redirected, have a generation or EPG history; different trusted values of one scheme need verified evidence; credits are adopted after the survivor's own, aliases, external ids and membership move, existing redirects are repointed (no chain longer than one hop); a repeated merge is a no-op and a different target conflicts; a late failing statement (a row elsewhere still references the retired Film) leaves credits, ids, aliases, redirects and revisions unchanged.
- **Inactive.** The new modules are absent from `build.mjs` and from the Worker, the projector, the repository and the catalog; they contain no R2, `fetch` or Wrangler reference.

Mutation checks (each removal of the part makes the test fail): the domain fence statement, the revision assertion, the writable gate, the in-batch survivor and retired-Film redirect assertions, the keyed credit upsert, the held handling in the writer and the reader, the baseline gate for the move out of `r2`, the baseline transitions, the reopen guard, the transition trigger, the no-delete trigger, the held to `r2` prohibition and the prerequisite check of the migration.

## Scenarios available now and later

Available now: all of the above. Verifiable only in later cards: the writer handoff with no gap or overlap (AIR-371, AIR-374), a complete full plus delta baseline and its parity (AIR-373; this card only provides the gate), the readers (AIR-372), producer integration (AIR-371), localization (AIR-368) and the admission and retirement models (AIR-369, AIR-370).

## Limits

Production counts and the real baseline are unknown; nothing here is a production run. The schema is not applied in production. The revision lives in a separate `canonical_revision` table, so the projector's writes do not move it; the revision is meaningful only while the projector is not the writer, that is from `open` on.
