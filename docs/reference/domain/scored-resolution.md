# Scored EPG resolution

[AIR-43](https://trello.com/c/7NY6n1Az) scores one observation in `src/domain/scored-resolution.js`. The acceptance gate in `src/domain/resolution-acceptance.js` decides whether that score may become an AIR-48 candidate decision. Discovery calls both on every resolver chunk: the scored gate is the only resolver and the only writer of candidate decisions and catalog identity ([ADR 0035](../../adr/0035-scored-resolution-only.md)). There is no runtime mode. The former `SCORED_RESOLUTION` variable is retired and ignored whatever value a stale binding carries, so neither `off` nor `shadow` can bring the legacy resolver back; rolling the resolver back is a code revert. The gate does not treat an accepted score as `verified`. For an accepted id with no same-key confirmation, the Worker fetches that movie and the gate can return `detail_verified`. That is the only scored outcome that creates or updates the catalog film. A verified decision is confirmation only for that same observation, not for every other row with the same TMDB id. A known alias in the index is a scorer candidate, never a verified identity by itself: it is verified by the same detail read, and a short title without a year or a collision between editions is decided from the index without any TMDB request.

[AIR-48](https://trello.com/c/rVf8rRoJ) still decides which observation is current. [AIR-42](https://trello.com/c/drLBuWjl) still owns Film identity and reviewed titles. [AIR-49](https://trello.com/c/YNaLFwtW) still owns where a decision is stored. This scorer does not import any of them. `observation.key` is returned as `observationKey` and is not a Film id. A reviewed title on the input is ignored. `acceptedTmdbId` is set only when the status is `accepted`.

`scorerVersion` is `1`. The weights below are the version-1 rules.

| Signal | Weight | Values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact normalized title, original title, or alias | 0.50 | 1 when exact, otherwise 0 and the row cannot be accepted. A reversible variant form also counts as exact when the candidate carries that claim and the scorer recomputes the same form from the observation |
| Year | 0.20 | exact 1; one year away 0.85; missing on a long unique title 0.75; missing on the candidate 0.40; missing on a short or non-unique title 0.15; two or more years away 0 and a year contradiction |
| Uniqueness | 0.15 | `1 / competitors`. A competitor is a distinct TMDB id with the same exact title and, when the observation has a year, a missing year or a year within one. A remake whose year is further away is not a competitor. Repeated input rows for one id count once |
| Runtime distance | 0.15 | within `max(8 minutes, 8%)` is 1; within `max(25, 20%)` is 0.55; within `max(45, 40%)` is 0.20; beyond that is 0 and a runtime contradiction. Unknown runtime is 0.50 and is not itself a contradiction |

A title is short when its normalized form is one token or eight characters or fewer. `Dune` is short. A missing year on a short title, or on a title shared by more than one competitor, cannot be accepted.

Acceptance requires a score of at least 0.82, no contradiction on the leader, a year relation other than `year_required`, and a gap of more than 0.12 over the next row. A score of at least 0.70 that fails those gates is `ambiguous`. A contradiction on the leader is `contradicted` even when the score is high. Anything else exact but weaker is `no_confident_match`. No exact title is `no_confident_match` with reason `no_exact_title`.

`confidence` is the leader's score, including when that leader is not accepted. `trusted: true` is recorded evidence for a known alias. It does not skip a collision, a missing year on a short title, or a contradiction. Records that share a TMDB id are combined first: titles, original titles, and aliases stay available for the match, and `sources` is the number of input rows. When those copies disagree, year and runtime use the value closest to the observation. The result has one row per TMDB id, ordered by score and then by that id, so the input order does not change the decision.

## Movie-detail verification

The chunk asks the gate once from the score. When that result is `needs_verification` and the score is `accepted`, the Worker requests `GET /movie/{id}` with credits, translations, and alternative titles, then asks the gate again with that payload. The catalog builder is the same function the legacy movie-detail path uses. There is no second film writer.

`detail_verified` requires the fetched id to be the accepted id, a release year within one when the EPG row has a year, and a runtime inside the detail tolerance below. The title check is the observation's normalized title, unless the score's `titleBasis` is structural. Then the fetched title, original title, translation, or alternative title must equal that recomputed form, and a rival with the same form and a compatible year blocks the write. A form the detail card does not repeat stays `no_confident_match`. A missing year on either side is compatible, as is a year within one. An observation with no year is therefore blocked by any other indexed film that shares the matched title. With a year the runtime tolerance is `max(25 minutes, 20% of runtime)`. Without a year it is `max(10 minutes, 12% of runtime)`. An identity collision on the catalog row also blocks it. A short title, one word or at most eight characters, whose release year is one away from the EPG year does not become `detail_verified` on the title, year, and runtime alone. The gate returns `needs_verification` with reason `short_title_adjacent_year` and keeps the accepted id on the score. Another indexed film with the same title, including one more than a year away, is listed as `competingEditions`. That list is a caution, not a finding that the nearer id is wrong. The same observation's owner review or its own `verified` decision is the independent confirmation that still allows `detail_verified`. An exact year is unchanged. `Чебурашка 2` is not a short title, so a neighbouring release year still verifies. The decision is then `verified`, with `identitySource: 'tmdb_detail'`, only when the gate returns `detail_verified`. Provenance records that the authority is the detail fetch, whether a search was needed, and that this write did not come from a prior alias or an owner review. The upsert adds the fetched aliases, credits, and the candidate's current shows. An id already on the watchlist keeps that film id and does not gain a second `tmdb_<id>` row. That film's availability lives on the published programme, so the candidate's current shows are attached there. A reviewed title on the programme film stays. The `verified` decision is written only after the catalog or programme write still belongs to the generation the chunk read. A generation change during the detail request writes neither the programme nor the decision. When a generation is published, that generation's programme file is the copy that receives the showing. The unversioned `programme.json` is left untouched, so a newer publish cannot be overwritten by this write. With no published generation, the unversioned programme is still the only copy and it is updated directly. The chunk's existing prediction-queue refresh sees a new catalog film, and a watchlist film whose programme shows changed.

These results do not create a canonical film:

| Failure | Stored result |
| --- | --- |
| Fetched id missing or different | `needs_verification` |
| Title does not match | `no_confident_match` |
| Release year disagrees | `no_confident_match` |
| Runtime missing or outside the tolerance | `duration_mismatch` |
| Another indexed edition is compatible | `ambiguous` |
| Identity collision | no decision |
| Short title and a release year one away, without an owner review or this observation's verified decision | `needs_verification`, reason `short_title_adjacent_year`; no catalog film |
| TMDB 429 or a network error | chunk `error` and `retryAt`; the attempt counter stays |
| Published generation changes before the catalog, programme, or decision write | `programme_changed` and no decision |
| Score is not accepted | the AIR-48 status already mapped from that score |

A failed detail uses the same attempt cap and evidence key as an uncertain match. The same evidence waits and then stops. A changed title, year, duration, or broadcast can be fetched again. A `verified` row is not selected again. A search hit, a high score, `trusted: true`, and another observation's `verified` decision do not pass the gate.

Historically a `shadow` mode let the legacy resolver write while the scored gate only produced a comparison preview (`agree_verified`, `legacy_only`, `scored_only`, `detail_not_compared`, `different_id`). Those measurements are in the [scored resolver evidence](../../evidence/scored-resolver/README.md); the comparison fields no longer exist and the preview carries scored outcomes only.

[AIR-49](https://trello.com/c/YNaLFwtW) owns a separate store for the score, the fetched card, and the decision. The unapplied tables are in [docs/d1-schema.md](../data/d1-schema.md). This path keeps a compact provenance on the current decision row and does not apply that schema. The owner-labeled review in `docs/air-43-verification-audit.md` is not built. [AIR-53](https://trello.com/c/gkuy1awG) is unchanged.

The programme marks `≈` only when the attached showing is `title_duration`. Every attached showing is scored first. The mark stays off only when that score is `accepted`: a year then uses `title_year`, and a missing year on a long unique title uses `title_unique`. A year by itself does not hide a runtime contradiction, a near-year collision, or any other result that is not accepted. Those stay `title_duration` and name the reason. An older snapshot that stored `title_duration` without a reason still shows the cautious mark until the next successful EPG scan replaces its showings.

`GET /api/enrichment/pipeline/status` reads `epg/discovery-score-preview.json`, which every chunk writes for its generation. `scoringPreview.needsConfirmation` counts accepted scores that are not verified. `scoringPreview.mode` and `scoringPreview.cutover` are fixed to `on`, kept in the shape readers already parse. A preview whose `programmeRunId` is not the pinned generation is `stale`, and its rows are not counted as the current generation. Reading the status does not write.
