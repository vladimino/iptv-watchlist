# EPG publication and availability

The active programme is a **published-generation projection**, not an append-only lifetime schedule. Accepted [ADR 0135](../adr/0135-epg-priority-discovery-pause.md) gives this publication priority over new Discovery admission until the generation is handed off. Its gate code is delivered inert (AIR-459) and is not activated, and the diagram below does not draw it. Sources: [EPG publication ADR](../adr/0008-atomic-epg-publication.md), [Film/Person availability contract](../reference/domain/film-person.md), [discovery reference](../reference/domain/candidate-discovery.md), and [Worker routing](../../src/worker.js).

```mermaid
    source["Configured EPG XML source"]
    parse["Authorized sync: parse broadcasts and match shows"]
    staging["Stage the generation:<br/>immutable discovery ledger in R2,<br/>staging payload; the run moves to stage seal"]
    seal["Bounded seal steps (about 30 s each):<br/>ledger rows and cursor in one D1 batch,<br/>archive, then the catalog difference"]
    pointer[("Published generation pointer:<br/>D1 published_epg_authority (d1 / open);<br/>R2 pointer is a compatibility mirror")]
    readers["Programme / film page / availability readers"]
    window["Availability projection:<br/>past 72h, future 24h for hot"]
    ensure["Hourly ensure: R2 mirrors"]
    previous["Prior published generation remains readable"]
    stale{"Writer still owns pinned generation?"}
    update["Apply generation-scoped catalogue/decision update"]
    reject["Abort stale update without touching newer generation"]
    source --> parse --> staging --> seal --> pointer
    pointer --> readers --> window
    pointer --> ensure
    seal -.->|until pointer switch| previous
    pointer --> stale
    stale -->|yes| update

```

## Film availability lifecycle

On the published shows a film is, at every instant, in one of three states ([ADR 0114](../adr/0114-film-availability-lifecycle.md), [reference](../reference/domain/film-availability.md)). The state is a **snapshot recomputed from the shows** at each instant and is never stored, so any of the three can be the first one seen; the diagram lists the transitions time or a new generation can cause. A repeat or a show on air never changes `available` while a ready recording exists. A ready archive outranks live and future; a show on air is not an archive until it ends; `soon` turns `available` at the show's effective end (the EPG end, else start plus the film runtime; a schedule-based calculation, not a provider probe).

```mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
    unavailable --> soon: a show starts within 24 h or is on air
    soon --> available: the effective end of the show passes
    soon --> unavailable: the candidate shows are gone without an archive
    available --> unavailable: 72 h after the start of the last ready show
    available --> soon: the last archive expires while a repeat is on air or starts within 24 h
    unavailable --> available: a published generation brings a show that already ended
```

The 72-hour catch-up and the rolling 24 hours are the existing [EPG horizons](../reference/domain/epg-horizons.md); the 168-hour retention is storage only.

The publication pointer changes **after** the complete generation exists, and with D1-authoritative discovery after its ledger is sealed ([ADR 0092](../adr/0092-resumable-bounded-ledger-seal.md)): the commit stages the generation and returns, bounded `epg_sync_step` calls seal the ledger, update the catalog and publish, and the previous generation stays readable meanwhile. A partial write does not expose mixed versions. A newly published generation replaces its current show lists; current availability does not carry forward old shows. Show observations and `epgLastAiring` are not permanent Film identity attributes. Where the published generation is known, absent or mismatched `showsProgrammeRunId` cannot prove the film is hot; explicit `shows: []` means no known availability.

After the pointer switch the commit opens the discovery series of the new generation before its memory-heavy tail (watchlist and events, prediction queue, ratings); the write is fenced to the published generation and keeps a live writer or an existing series of the same generation. See [ADR 0030](../adr/0030-discovery-queued-at-publication.md). Under a live D1 pin `persistWatchlist` does not call `saveWatchlist`: the publish commit already wrote the watchlist, and a second `generation + 1` is forbidden ([ADR 0142](../adr/0142-epg-owned-generation-addressed-discovery-slots.md) decision 14). If the pin cannot be read, the tail writes nothing and the error propagates so the bounded step can retry. A successful read that yields `null` still copies `watchlist.json` when `updatedAt` differs (R2 authority). The tail's prediction queue rebuild is a conditional write that rebuilds on conflict with another queue writer ([ADR 0064](../adr/0064-prediction-queue-conditional-writes.md), Accepted 2026-10-31). A conflict it cannot resolve is recorded as `enrichmentQueueError` and does not fail the commit.

Retention and display are separate from hot processing ([EPG horizons](../reference/domain/epg-horizons.md)): the published generation keeps reliable watchlist shows up to 168 hours ahead, and the 24-hour future window below remains the hot discovery, resolver, prediction and catalog horizon. Availability calculations use a 72-hour lookback for hot showings and a 24-hour future planning window; the last known airing supports a 72-hour availability horizon. These domain computations are not equivalent to ingesting a new EPG. Late resolver activity must respect the pinned generation before writing programme or decisions.

## Completed programme read ([ADR 0142](../adr/0142-epg-owned-generation-addressed-discovery-slots.md), stage 4a)

The published programme has two reads. The **sealed read** (`PublishedEpg.readDocument`) returns the document the EPG sync stored; it is internal to publication (staging, the publish commit, the watchlist part, membership checks, `importPublished`). The **completed read** (`PublishedEpg.readCompletedProgramme`, and `readDocumentAt` for the `programme` kind) adds the rows Discovery assigned to the run (`published_epg_show.basis = 'discovery'`) with one statement: each member's `shows` are completed and sorted by `(start, channelId)` with `availableUntil` recomputed, and the Films of the run that are not members are listed in the additive `catalogFilms` (identity and shows only; no Film data yet). With no assigned row the completed programme equals the sealed document. Assigned rows never change `published_epg_authority.generation`, the members or the watchlist document, so the existing generation check around a read still proves the sealed part, and every Discovery writer changes the assigned rows of an observation in one transaction.

Everything a reader or an R2 mirror sees is the completed programme: `GET /api/programme`, the catalog and film routes through `readDocumentAt`, the Admin snapshot, `repairMirrors` (the hourly reconcile rewrites the R2 programme mirrors when an assignment landed since the last write; that lag is accepted) and the programme comparisons of `revertPublished` and `activatePublished`. `revertPublished` reads `discovery_authority.generation` before it reconciles the mirrors and its mode flip requires the same generation, so a Discovery assignment or correction between the comparison and the flip fails the revert with `published_epg_fence` and nothing is flipped; after the flip the insert guard (`mode = 'd1'`) refuses every assignment. `authoritativeSnapshot` (the Film/Person projector input) and `scripts/published-epg-production.mjs` mirror parity (activation, hold, revert readback) also use the completed programme. Because the offline connector copy must hold what the projector reads, `published_epg_show` joined the moving Discovery copy tables (`DISCOVERY_COPY_TABLES`: copied last and re-read by the resync, so the copy is 40 tables). The revert reads `discovery_authority.generation` fail-closed: an unreadable, missing or non-finite value aborts before any flip. The prediction queue rebuild (`buildPredictionQueue`) under a D1 pin takes each Film's showings from the same completed programme (`films` and `catalogFilms` by TMDB id) and never reads `hotEnteredAt`: the late tie-break is the earliest start among those showings, then `discoveredAt`, then the id. A D1 pin whose `programmeRunId` is not the caller's completed programme is not treated as R2: the rebuild reloads that pinned generation, or fails closed (`published_epg_unavailable`) and writes no queue. Outside D1 the record's own `shows` still rank the queue, still without `hotEnteredAt`. Under a live D1 pin, `replaceCatalogAvailability`, `mergeCatalogIdentity` (the D1 publish/seal catalog path) and `touchCanonicalFilm` / `catalogFilmFromDetail` keep identity data and aliases and do not rewrite `shows`, `availableUntil`, `showsProgrammeRunId`, `epgLastAiring` or `hotEnteredAt`; R2 still stamps those fields. Discovery assignment, replay and those catalog writers execute no statement on `film_alias` (owner decision R3).

### Availability-field consumers

| Field | D1 published authority | R2 published authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `shows` | Completed programme (`films` + `catalogFilms`) for `GET /api/programme` (`presentCatalogFilms`), Finds (`catalogAvailable` via `withCompletedShows`), the film page (`catalogFilmPage`), the prediction queue (`buildPredictionQueue`), prediction coverage (`canonicalCoverageSummary` / footer and Event snapshots / MCP diagnostics), Admin snapshot, `authoritativeSnapshot` / Film/Person `observedShows` (members and `catalogFilms`, matched by id or TMDB), mirror repair and revert/activate comparisons. Catalog record `shows` are not a second source. | Record `shows` of `epg/discovered-films.json` / admission payload for Finds, the film page, the prediction queue, coverage and Film/Person when the sealed programme has no `catalogFilms`. EPG sync (`replaceCatalogAvailability`) and Discovery (`touchCanonicalFilm`) still write them. |
| `availableUntil` | Recomputed from completed `shows` on the programme route. | `withAvailability` / `availabilityDeadline` on watchlist/programme Films. |
| `showsProgrammeRunId` | Pin of the completed read. Finds/film page still require the overlaid run id to equal the pin. | Same-run gate on the record. |
| `epgLastAiring` | Derived from completed `shows` where a reader needs a latest start. `predictionPriority` may still fall back to a stored value when `shows` are empty (R2). | `filmIsHot` fallback and `replaceCatalogAvailability` / `touchCanonicalFilm` writes. |
| `hotEnteredAt` | Not read. Queue late tie-break is earliest start, then `discoveredAt`, then id. Not written under D1. | Not read. Still written by `touchCanonicalFilm` on R2. |
| `aliases` | Identity data on the catalog record and, for a watchlist Film, on the verified decision/observation. Scan and Discovery alias indexes (`src/sources.js`, `src/discovery.js`), Finds title rule, UI search (`public/app.js`). Never written into the programme document. Never written to canonical `film_alias` by Discovery. | Same identity writers. |

Stale availability bytes may remain on D1 admission payloads until a later identity write; D1 readers ignore them. Film/Person `projectFilm` / `observedShows` takes shows from the completed programme (`films` matched by id or TMDB, then `catalogFilms`) when `catalogFilms` is present (the D1 projector stamps `[]` when the pin has no assigned rows). A Film absent from that list has zero shows; body `shows` / `epgLastAiring` are not a second source. The R2 projector path still uses the member record, then the Film body. The programme screen lists presented `catalogFilms` in the watchlist grid with a NEW badge; they are not watchlist members. Owner visual acceptance of that layout is still required. Not remaining from the completed-read work: stripping stored bytes, natural-run delay measurement.
