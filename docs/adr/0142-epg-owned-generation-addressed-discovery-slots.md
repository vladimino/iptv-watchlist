## Context

**Owner directive (target, not the present state).** The programme is one ordered set of broadcast elements. Each element is either a broadcast or a Film with a showing. When Discovery recognises a broadcast, that element becomes a Film in the same place; no other element and no programme version changes. Only the EPG sync publishes a programme generation. Consumers and migrations adapt to this, not the reverse. In the preliminary directions the owner added: this holds for a verified Film that is not on the Letterboxd watchlist too, and it does not put that Film on the watchlist.

**What exists on `main` (commit `415d3f5b`, read from code).** There is no single list. The retained EPG scope of one run lives in three stores:

| Part | Store | Written by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watchlist Film with the showings the scan matched (window from 72 hours back to 7 days ahead) | Programme document in `published_epg_document`: every watchlist Film in watchlist order, each with `shows`. The same facts as `published_epg_member` and `published_epg_show` rows | EPG sync publish commit (`commitPublish`) |
| Broadcast the scan could not match to exactly one watchlist Film (55 to 300 minutes, from 72 hours back to 24 hours ahead) | `discovery_observation` and `discovery_observation_show`, active rows of the run, grouped by `normalizedTitle\|year` | EPG sync ledger seal |
| Verified Film that is not on the watchlist (catalog Film), with showings | `discovery_admission.payload_json` (`shows`, `showsProgrammeRunId`, `epgLastAiring`, `hotEnteredAt`) | EPG sync (`replaceCatalogAvailability`) and Discovery (`upsertAdmissions`) |

Broadcasts that the scan screens as non-film, that are shorter than 55 or longer than 300 minutes, that are on `XX:` channels or that fall outside the window are not retained anywhere (`scanEPG`, `src/sources.js:257` to `280`).

1. **A slot has a de facto address, but nothing makes it unique.** Every stored showing carries `channelId` and `start`. `dedupeShows` keys on them per Film and `discovery_observation_show` stores them per row. The primary key of `published_epg_show` is `(programme_run_id, film_id, channel_id, start_ms)`, so it allows two Films on one slot. The scan does not check the slot across owners: two feed elements with the same channel and start but different titles are retained under two Films, two observations, or a Film and an observation.
2. **Discovery republishes the programme to attach one showing.** When a verified candidate is a Film already on the watchlist, `attachWatchlistShowing` (`src/discovery.js:605` to `636`, called at `820` and `978`) loads the whole programme document, changes one Film (`shows`, `aliases`, `hotEnteredAt`, `showsProgrammeRunId`, `epgLastAiring`) and calls `PublishedEpg.patchProgramme`. `commitReplace` (`src/published-epg.js:318` to `343`) then deletes and rewrites the programme document, all `programme` members and ALL show rows of the run, and sets `generation = generation + 1`. The R2 `programme.json` mirror and the generation copy are written as well (`src/published-epg.js:447` to `448`). Every attach is one new published generation.
3. **The attach is not tied to the committed decision.** The paged resolver attaches the showing (`src/discovery.js:820`) BEFORE it writes the decision (`saveCandidate`, called at `831`, writing at `784`). `writeDecisionDelta` can skip that decision (`conflict`, `observation_gone`, `evidence_changed`), and the attached showing stays. An owner correction (`DiscoveryAuthority.ownerDecision`, `commitOwnerWrite`) changes only the decision.
4. **The decision and the programme use different Film ids.** A programme member carries its watchlist id and a `tmdbId`. A decision carries `tmdb_id` and a `film_id` that is the watchlist id only when the resolver found the Film on the watchlist; an owner assignment always stores `tmdb_<id>`. The link between them is the TMDB id (`knownTmdb`, `src/discovery.js:680`).
5. **A verified Film outside the watchlist is not an element of the programme a reader gets.** For such a Film the chunk writes `discovery_admission` and `discovery_decision`, which move only `discovery_authority.generation`. Its showings are fields of the admission payload. Two writers set them: the EPG sync for an observation the scan itself confirmed against a catalog alias (`replaceCatalogAvailability`, `src/sources.js:206`), and the cheap pass for a hot verified observation (`touchCanonicalFilm`, `src/discovery.js:307`). `GET /api/programme` returns watchlist Films only (`src/worker.js:1786`). The showings of a catalog Film reach a reader through Finds (`catalogAvailable`, `src/worker.js:283`, which also requires a current prediction above the Finds floor), the film page (`catalogFilmPage`, `src/worker.js:331`) and the prediction queue priority (`predictionPriority`, `src/enrichment.js:981`).
6. **A stored decision is not re-applied to a watchlist Film.** `resolutionActionability` returns `false` for a `verified` decision, and the cheap pass (`src/discovery.js:694` to `715`) touches only catalog Films, without comparing evidence. The scan's title index comes from the Letterboxd metadata, not from the aliases the attach wrote. So after the next EPG run the same broadcast is an observation again, and nothing puts it back on the watchlist Film.
7. **The evidence recorded on a decision cannot be compared across runs as a whole.** The evidence key is `normalizedTitle|year|durations|shows` (`src/discovery.js:35`, `src/discovery-authority.js:118`). Its show part is the list of `channel:start` of one run, so it differs in every run. The `durations` of an observation accumulate across runs, up to 12 values (`mergeDiscoveryCandidates`, `src/sources.js:65`). The duration of one slot is exact per row: `discovery_observation_show.end_ms - start_ms`. A decision verified from the TMDB detail records the evidence key it was verified against (`src/discovery.js:828`). A decision the cheap pass makes from a scan confirmation (`known_epg_alias`, `src/discovery.js:703`) and an owner assignment record no durations of their own.
8. **The cheap pass already pages through the current verified observations.** `selectActionableObservations` returns a keyset page of them with a cursor that wraps (`TOUCH_PAGE_SQL`, `src/discovery-authority.js:777` to `783`; `PAGED_CHEAP_FILMS` = 38 per chunk, `src/discovery.js:32`), for watchlist and catalog Films alike.
9. **All programme readers use one read seam, and so do the mirrors and the revert.** `/api/programme`, `/api/watchlist`, the catalog routes, the prediction queue, the Admin snapshot and the enrichment projector read the document through `PublishedEpg.readDocument`, `readDocumentAt` or `authoritativeSnapshot` (`src/worker.js:17` to `29`, `447`, `528`, `1786` to `1816`; `src/enrichment.js:1868`; `src/admin/read.js:232`; `src/d1-enrichment.js:190`). `repairMirrors` compares the R2 mirrors with `readDocument`, and `revertPublished` calls `repairMirrors` and then compares the mirror with `readDocument` again (`src/published-epg.js:520` to `540`, `666` to `692`). No runtime reader reads `published_epg_show`.
10. **The watchlist tail.** The EPG publish commit stores the watchlist with `updatedAt` equal to the programme's (`src/worker.js:1731`). `persistWatchlist` (`src/worker.js:711`) calls `saveWatchlist`, a second `generation + 1`, only when the stored `updatedAt` differs. By this code reading it writes nothing after a sealed D1 publication. This was not checked against production evidence.
11. **Producer input (dormant).** `planEpgUnits` (`src/canonical-epg-producer.js`) plans one `film_facts` unit per observed Film (`film`, `dimensions`, `localization`, `metadata`, `sync`, `watchlist`) and one release unit per departed member. Showings are not an input. The ADR 0141 draft labels a pass `P:g` and needs a pre-cutover gate (G-P) because "Discovery can increment `g` per attach".

## Decision

Terms. **Slot:** one retained broadcast element of one EPG run. **Sealed part:** the documents, members and show rows that the EPG sync published for a run. **Assigned row:** a `published_epg_show` row written by Discovery (`basis = 'discovery'`, the observation key in `match_reason`). **Member:** a Film of the `programme` members of a run; its TMDB id comes from the sealed document of that run. **Catalog Film:** a verified Film that is not a member and has a current catalog record; the record is `discovery_admission` (`film_id = tmdb_<id>`, `state = 'current'`) until the Film/Person cutover of AIR-374 changes the reader. **Anchor:** the set of broadcast durations in the evidence key that a decision recorded when it was verified.

1. **Slot contract and uniqueness (M1, M6).** A slot is addressed by `(programme_run_id, channel_id, start_ms)`. Its coordinates (`channel_id`, `channel`, `start_ms`, `end_ms`) are set by the scan and are never changed by anyone but a new EPG run. The EPG title of the element is stored per slot only on a sealed row (`published_epg_show.epg_title`). For an observation slot the D1 ledger keeps the title forms on the observation (`title` and up to 8 `rawTitles`), not on the slot: `discovery_observation_show` has no title column and `loadPageShows` returns none (`src/discovery-authority.js:634` to `644`). The order of the programme is `(start_ms, channel_id)`, derived from the coordinates, so no write can reorder it. A slot has at most ONE Film, enforced in two places and without an index:
   - **Scan (EPG sync).** `scanEPG` records the owner (one Film, or one observation key) of every `(channelId, start)` it retains. A second element with the same slot and the same owner is ignored. When two DIFFERENT owners claim one slot, BOTH records are discarded: after the stream the slot is removed from every owner and is not retained. There is no first-wins. The discard is reported as `duplicate_slot`: `stats` carries the COMPLETE count of discarded slots, and the `uncertain` list stays a diagnostic sample capped at 100 entries (`src/sources.js:279`). The count is the measure; the list is not.
   - **Assignment.** The insert of decision 4 writes only when no row of any Film holds the slot.
2. **One programme, whatever the status of the Film (M3).** The programme of a run is one ordered set of slots. A slot is a **Film** when a `published_epg_show` row exists for its address, and a **broadcast** otherwise (provenance: the observation key). The provenance of a Film row is its `basis`: `title_year`, `title_unique` or `title_duration` for the scan, `discovery` for an assigned row.
   - Whether the Film is on the watchlist, was watched or rated, or was found by Discovery does not decide whether its slot is a Film. A verified Film that is not on the watchlist takes its slot exactly as a watchlist Film does.
   - Being in the programme is not membership. An assigned row adds no `published_epg_member` row, changes neither the `films` of the programme document nor the watchlist document, and leaves `/api/watchlist` unchanged.
   - Each Film appears once, under its canonical TMDB identity (`tmdb:<id>`). The `film_id` of an assigned row is the member's id when the decision's TMDB id is a member's, and the catalog id `tmdb_<id>` otherwise; never both.
   - A Film is in the programme only while it holds a slot of the pinned run. A retained Film with no current showing is not listed. Its permanent page is the subject of [AIR-475](https://trello.com/c/9OniH9d9), which uses the same identity key and is not a prerequisite of this record.

   The single ordered list is this logical view over the existing tables. It is not a new stored object.
3. **Ownership.** Only the EPG sync writes `published_epg_authority.programme_run_id`, `generation` and `watchlist_generation`, the documents, the members and the sealed show rows. Discovery owns decisions, catalog records and assigned rows. After the implementation the only runtime statements that increment `generation` are the publish commits of the EPG sync.
4. **Agreement invariant and addressed write (M1).** *Invariant:* an assigned row `(P, F, slot)` of observation `O` exists only while the stored decision of `O` is `verified` with the TMDB identity of Film `F`. It is kept by an order and two rules:
   - **Decision first.** The resolver writes the decision through the unchanged compare-and-set of `writeDecisionDelta`. It assigns only for a key that write reports as `written`, never for a skipped one, and never before it. For a catalog Film the existing order stays in front: the catalog record, then the decision, then the rows.
   - **Conditional insert.** One assigned row per slot of `O`, each by a statement that writes only if ALL of the following hold inside that statement: the published authority is `d1` / `open` and still points at `P`; the stored decision of `O` is `verified`, is a movie, has that TMDB id and has the exact `decision_hash` the writer evaluated; `O` is an eligible observation of `P` (`source_key = 'active'`, `residence = 'active'`, `seen_in_latest = 1`) and the slot is one of its slots with the `start_ms` and `end_ms` the writer evaluated; the Film has a record in `P`, which means `film_id` is a `programme` member of `P`, or it is `tmdb_<that TMDB id>` with a current catalog record; no row of any Film holds the slot. The row's coordinates are copied from the observation's slot row inside the statement, so Discovery supplies no coordinate and no title. The `epg_title` of an assigned row is NULL, as it is for a showing the present attach writes under D1, and no runtime reader uses the title of a showing. Keeping the original title per slot would need a column on `discovery_observation_show`, which is a migration and is excluded by M1. The batch runs under the Discovery lease with the Discovery fence, so it advances `discovery_authority.generation` like every Discovery write and never the published generation.
   - **Removal with the decision.** Every writer of a decision (`writeDecisionDelta`, the whole-map `writeDecisions`, the owner's `commitOwnerWrite`) that changes the verified identity of `O` (the status leaves `verified`, or the TMDB id changes) deletes the assigned rows of `O` for the published run IN THE SAME fenced batch as the decision write. The batch commits or fails as a whole, so no assigned row outlives the decision it came from.

   Illustration (AIR-480 fixes the exact statements):

   ```sql
   INSERT INTO published_epg_show
     (programme_run_id, film_id, channel_id, start_ms, end_ms, channel, epg_title, basis, match_reason)
   SELECT :P, :film, s.channel_id, s.start_ms, s.end_ms, s.channel, NULL, 'discovery', :observation_key
   FROM discovery_observation o JOIN discovery_observation_show s
     ON s.source_key = o.source_key AND s.observation_key = o.observation_key
   WHERE o.source_key = 'active' AND o.residence = 'active' AND o.seen_in_latest = 1
     AND o.programme_run_id = :P AND o.observation_key = :observation_key
     AND s.channel_id = :channel_id AND s.start_ms = :start_ms AND s.end_ms = :end_ms
     AND EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM published_epg_authority
                 WHERE id = 1 AND mode = 'd1' AND cutover = 'open' AND programme_run_id = :P)
     AND EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM discovery_decision
                 WHERE observation_key = :observation_key AND status = 'verified' AND media_kind = 'movie'
                   AND tmdb_id = :tmdb_id AND decision_hash = :decision_hash)
     AND (EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM published_epg_member
                  WHERE programme_run_id = :P AND kind = 'programme' AND film_id = :film)
          OR (:film = 'tmdb_' || :tmdb_id
              AND EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM discovery_admission
                          WHERE film_id = :film AND media_kind = 'movie' AND tmdb_id = :tmdb_id
                            AND state = 'current')))
     AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM published_epg_show
                     WHERE programme_run_id = :P AND channel_id = :channel_id AND start_ms = :start_ms);

   -- in the batch of a decision write that changes the verified identity of :observation_key
   DELETE FROM published_epg_show
   WHERE programme_run_id = (SELECT programme_run_id FROM published_epg_authority WHERE id = 1)
     AND basis = 'discovery' AND match_reason = :observation_key;
   ```

   The statement can check that `:film` is a member, not that this member has this TMDB id: `published_epg_member` has no TMDB column. The writer takes that link from the sealed document of `P`, which cannot change while the statement's pointer guard holds. Discovery no longer calls `patchProgramme`, no longer writes the programme document, the members, the sealed rows, a Film field of the programme or an R2 programme object. A grouped observation with several showings writes one row per slot; each slot keeps its own coordinates and position.
5. **Replay through the existing cheap pass (M2).** One path repairs a missing assignment, whatever its cause (a crash or a lost response between the decision and the insert, an owner assignment, a new EPG run): the cheap pass of the existing Discovery Workflow chunk. It already pages through the current verified observations (context 8); it runs the conditional insert of decision 4 for them, for members and catalog Films alike, with no TMDB call and inside the existing per-chunk bounds. No scheduler, Workflow or cron is added, and the EPG sync never reads a Discovery decision. Replay starts when Discovery starts after the EPG priority pause of [ADR 0135](0135-epg-priority-discovery-pause.md) is released (the natural-run handoff is what [AIR-467](https://trello.com/c/yuBApRtz) verifies). No maximum latency is promised.
   - **Identity.** A decision is keyed by the observation key, which is the normalized title and the year, so title and year agree by construction. The TMDB identity is pinned by the decision hash in the statement.
   - **Evidence is checked per slot.** The show part of an evidence key is the per-run part and is never compared. For each slot of the observation in the pinned run, with `d = round((end_ms - start_ms) / 60000)` minutes, the slot is eligible when one of these holds:
     1. the owner assigned the decision (`identitySource = 'owner'`);
     2. the scan of the pinned run confirmed the same TMDB id on the observation (`confirmed_tmdb_id`);
     3. **anchor rule:** the decision has an anchor, and for some anchored duration `D` the difference `|d - D|` is at most `min(10, max(5, 0.05 * D))` minutes. That is 5 minutes at 90, 6 at 120, 9 at 180 and 10 from 200 up.

     Each slot is decided on its own: the eligible slots of an observation are assigned and the others are not.
   - **No chaining.** Replay never writes the decision, so the anchor stays the evidence of the verification. A slot is always compared with that anchor, never with a duration that an earlier run was allowed. Only a new verification (the resolver with the TMDB detail, or the owner) sets a new anchor.
6. **Evidence drift is a visible state, not a silent broadcast (M2).** A slot that is not eligible under decision 5 has **evidence drift**; a decision with no anchor that meets neither rule 1 nor rule 2 is in the same state. For such a slot:
   - the slot stays a broadcast, the eligible slots of the same observation keep their Film, and the decision is not changed;
   - the refusal is the counted outcome `evidence_drift`. The count is part of the Discovery status that the monitor and the pipeline status already read, and the Admin view of the current verified observations shows the observation with its anchored and its observed durations;
   - the state ends when the owner confirms the identity for the observation (an owner assignment, rule 1), corrects or rejects it, or a later run brings the slot back inside the tolerance.

   Whether the state can also end WITHOUT the owner is the open choice **R1**: an automatic TMDB revalidation was discussed and is not approved. Until R1 is decided nothing revalidates automatically: a drifted slot is explained and visible, and it waits for the owner with no time bound.
7. **Outcomes, concurrency and a stale writer.** Replaying an assignment inserts nothing. Every refusal is a counted outcome named by the check that failed (`stale_run`, `film_absent`, `decision_changed`, `observation_inactive`, `slot_absent`, `slot_taken`, `evidence_drift`); it is not an error and never reaches the EPG run. Two decisions cannot both hold one slot: the scan gives a slot one owner, and the insert guard refuses a second Film. A Discovery chunk that still holds an older run after a new EPG publication inserts nothing; the pointer guard is inside the statement, so no check-then-write window exists.
8. **Wrong match and owner correction.** The decision stays the record of identity and stays Discovery-owned. A correction is a decision write, so the removal rule of decision 4 applies to it: the owner's batch deletes the assigned rows of the observation, and the new rows (if the new identity has a Film record) come from the conditional insert, at once under the same lease or by the replay of decision 5. Discovery can delete only assigned rows; a sealed row is corrected only by the next EPG run. Coordinates do not change in either case.
9. **Unknown element and Film without a record.** A decision whose slot is not a slot of an eligible observation of the pinned run writes nothing. A decision whose TMDB id is neither a member's nor a current catalog record's writes nothing (`film_absent`); an example is an owner assignment to an id whose catalog Film does not exist yet, a state the Admin already reports. In both cases the decision is kept, and the replay assigns once the record exists. The catalog admission path is unchanged: Discovery still creates and updates the catalog record of a Film that is not on the watchlist. What changes is that this Film also takes its slots.
10. **New EPG publication.** Assigned rows are keyed by run, so a new run starts with its sealed part only and old rows are never read through the new pointer. After Discovery pins the new run, decision 5 assigns the eligible slots again. [ADR 0135](0135-epg-priority-discovery-pause.md) is unchanged and sufficient: assigned rows are written only inside a Discovery chunk under the lease, so the EPG priority pause stops them, and they hold no EPG stage, claim or fence.
11. **Two reads, one rule (M3, M4).** The **sealed read** (`readDocument`) returns the stored document. The **completed read** is built from the published tables alone and returns:
    - the sealed document with each member's `shows` completed by the assigned rows whose `film_id` is that member, sorted by `(start, channelId)`, and `availableUntil` recomputed;
    - the **catalog Films of the run**: one entry per `film_id` of the assigned rows that is not a member, carrying only identity (`id`, `tmdbId`, `canonicalIdentity`) and `shows`. The `films` of the document stay the watchlist Films, so no consumer of `films` starts treating a catalog Film as a member.

    | Read | Callers |
    | --- | --- |
    | Completed | `readDocumentAt`, `authoritativeSnapshot`, the Admin snapshot (`src/admin/read.js:232`), `repairMirrors` (the only source of a mirror rewrite between publications), the programme comparisons of `revertPublished` and `activatePublished`, and the mirror parity checks of `scripts/published-epg-production.mjs` |
    | Sealed | staging and the publish commit, `replacePart` for the watchlist, `payload_sha256`, and the membership checks (members against the document's Film list, which assigned rows never change) |

    The rule: everything a reader or an R2 mirror sees is the completed programme; the sealed read is internal to publication.

    **Presentation.** `GET /api/programme` returns `films` as before and the catalog Films of the run as an additive list (AIR-480 fixes the field name). The route fills the Film data of each catalog entry (title, year, poster, credits, aliases) from the catalog reader: the current `discovery_admission` record at present, the canonical Film reader after the Film/Person cutover. An entry whose record the reader does not return as current is not presented as a Film and is counted. A catalog reader that cannot answer (a Discovery fence, a ledger seal in progress) never fails or empties the member part: the response states that the catalog part is unavailable. The programme screen builds one list from both parts. Finds (`/api/catalog/available`) and the film page stay filtered views and take their showings from the same completed read, so the programme and Finds cannot disagree about a showing. `/api/watchlist` is unchanged.
12. **Consistency of the completed read without an EPG generation bump (M4).** The sealed part cannot change while `(P, g)` holds, and the existing generation check before and after the read proves that. The assigned rows are read by exactly ONE statement, and every writer changes the assigned rows of an observation in one transaction. So a completed read is the sealed part plus the assigned set of one instant: it never mixes two states of a correction. A caller that compares and then acts uses content, not the fence:
    - **Readers that cache or label content** may claim "unchanged" only when the fence is equal AND a digest of the completed content is equal (the Admin snapshot already digests the body it serves) or `discovery_authority.generation` is equal (the projector already re-checks it, `src/d1-enrichment.js:150`). The Producer plan contains neither showings nor catalog Films, so its label stays `P:g`.
    - **Mirrors** contain the completed programme, including the catalog Films of the run (identity and showings, no Film data), as they contain attached showings today. Discovery writes no R2 programme object. The existing hourly reconcile (`repairMirrors`) compares mirror text with the completed read and rewrites on a difference; an assignment that lands after its read is picked up by the next hourly tick, and this lag is accepted.
    - **Revert** must be exact, and its safeguards are kept. `revertPublished` reads `discovery_authority.generation`, reconciles the mirrors, compares the mirror with the completed read, and its mode flip adds the guard that the Discovery generation is still the one it read. Every assignment and every correction advances that generation, so any of them between the comparison and the flip fails the revert with `published_epg_fence` and nothing is flipped; the operator retries. After the flip the insert guard (`mode = 'd1'`) refuses every assignment, so R2 holds exactly what readers saw. An activation or import from a mirror stores the sealed part only, without the catalog list.
13. **Identity data stays with the Film; availability comes from the slots (M5).** Discovery stops writing availability into a Film:

    | Field the attach or the cheap pass writes into a Film | After |
    | --- | --- |
    | `shows`, `availableUntil` | Derived at read from the show rows of the pinned run, for every Film |
    | `showsProgrammeRunId` | Not needed: show rows are keyed by run, and a reader takes the run of the completed read |
    | `epgLastAiring` | Derived where a reader needs it (the latest start among the Film's rows of the pinned run); not written by Discovery |
    | `hotEnteredAt` | Not written and not read. The hot and near-airing ranks of the prediction queue already come from `shows` (`predictionPriority`) and are kept. The late tie-break that reads `hotEnteredAt` (`src/enrichment.js:1528`) uses a value derived from the same showings, then `discoveredAt`, then the id |
    | `aliases` | Identity data. They stay on the Film's identity record and keep growing there: Discovery still adds the EPG title forms of a verified observation to the catalog record. They are never written into the programme document |

    The old writes are removed only after AIR-480 has listed every consumer of the four availability fields and of `aliases`. Known consumers: Finds and the film page (`src/worker.js:298`, `336`), the prediction queue (`src/enrichment.js:983`, `1528`), the Film availability projection (`src/domain/film-person.js:313`, `src/domain/film-person-reader.js:163`), the catalog merge and the EPG sync's catalog availability (`src/sources.js:196` to `219`), the scan and Discovery alias indexes (`src/sources.js:258`, `src/discovery.js:681`), the Finds title rule (`src/worker.js:265`) and the UI search (`public/app.js:562`). For a watchlist Film there is no Discovery-owned identity record: the EPG title forms stay durable as the verified decision and its observation, which is what the replay uses. Carrying them into the canonical `film_alias` rows is the open point **R3**.
14. **Watchlist tail.** One EPG sync produces one increment of `generation`. The watchlist is published by the publish commit; the tail must not make a second one. AIR-480 adds the test for this and removes the tail's write on the D1 path if the test shows it reachable.
15. **Not decided, not authorized.** No physical single-list table, no migration, no index, no flag, no scheduler, no Workflow, no second published snapshot. The labels, filters and default sort the programme screen gives to a Film that is not on the watchlist are interface design for the implementation card and need the owner's visual acceptance. The R2-mode branch of `attachWatchlistShowing` (authority not `d1`) is outside this record. Nothing here runs in production.

### Deterministic acceptance examples

Setup: run `P1`, `generation = 41`. Members `F1` (TMDB 101), `F2`, `F3` (TMDB 103). Catalog Film `X` is TMDB 900 and is not on the watchlist. Sealed row `(P1, F2, ch7, T1, title_year)`. Eligible observation `O` of `P1` with slots `(ch3, T2)` and `(ch9, T3)`, 90 minutes each. Eligible observation `Q` of `P1` with slot `(ch5, T5)` of 118 minutes. `T1 < T2 < T3 < T5`.

1. **Assign to a member.** Discovery verifies `O` as TMDB 101 from the TMDB detail (anchor 90); the decision is written, then the rows `(P1, F1, ch3, T2, discovery)` and `(P1, F1, ch9, T3, discovery)`. `generation = 41`, `programme_run_id = P1`, `watchlist_generation`, the documents, the members and `payload_sha256` are byte-identical; the completed read returns `F1.shows = [(ch3, T2), (ch9, T3)]` and `F2.shows = [(ch7, T1)]`; the slot order is the same before and after. Each assigned row has the `channel_id`, `channel`, `start_ms` and `end_ms` of its observation slot row and a NULL `epg_title`; the sealed row of `F2` keeps its `epg_title`.
2. **Assign to a Film outside the watchlist.** Discovery verifies `Q` as TMDB 900. The catalog record is written, then the decision, then the row `(P1, tmdb_900, ch5, T5, discovery)`. No `published_epg_member` row exists for `tmdb_900`; the `films` of the programme document, the watchlist document, the `/api/watchlist` response, `generation` and `watchlist_generation` are byte-identical. The completed read lists `tmdb_900` once among the catalog Films of the run, with `canonicalIdentity = tmdb:900` and `shows = [(ch5, T5)]`; the ids in `films` are the same as before.
3. **One Film per identity, no membership side effect.** In example 1 no row with `film_id = tmdb_101` exists. Property tests over any sequence of assignments and corrections: the completed read never contains two Films with one TMDB identity; the set of `programme` members and the set of watchlist Films never change.
4. **Replay.** The same insert again changes zero rows and the read is identical.
5. **Stale run.** The EPG sync publishes `P2` (`generation = 42`) before the insert. Zero rows change (`stale_run`); no row of `P2` exists for `O`; `generation` stays 42.
6. **Re-application.** In `P2`, `O` has slot `(ch3, T4)` of 94 minutes and `Q` has slot `(ch5, T6)` of 118 minutes; both decisions are `verified`. The next Discovery chunk that pins `P2` inserts `(P2, F1, ch3, T4, discovery)` and `(P2, tmdb_900, ch5, T6, discovery)` with no TMDB call; `generation` stays 42.
7. **Bounded drift, per slot.** The anchor of `O` is 90. In `P2` it has two slots, of 94 and of 96 minutes. The first is assigned; the second changes zero rows (`evidence_drift`) and stays a broadcast; the decision is byte-identical. Boundaries: anchor 120 accepts 126 and refuses 127; anchor 180 accepts 189 and refuses 190; anchor 240 accepts 250 and refuses 251. With anchors 90 and 118, a slot of 122 is accepted.
8. **No chaining.** The anchor is 90. `P2` has a slot of 94 minutes (assigned). `P3` has a slot of 98 minutes: it is compared with 90, not with 94, and is refused (`evidence_drift`). The decision's anchor after `P2` and `P3` is the one recorded at verification.
9. **No anchor.** A `known_epg_alias` decision: in `P2` the scan confirms the same TMDB id on the observation and the slots are assigned; in `P3` it does not, and they are refused (`evidence_drift`). An owner-assigned decision is assigned whatever the durations.
10. **Drift is visible and ends with the owner.** After example 7 the Discovery status of the chunk counts one `evidence_drift`, and the Admin view of verified observations shows `O` with anchor 90 and the observed 96. The owner confirms the identity; the next cheap pass assigns the slot.
11. **Slot taken.** After example 1 an insert names `F3` for `(ch3, T2)`. Zero rows change (`slot_taken`); `F1` keeps the slot.
12. **Film without a record.** The owner assigns `O` to TMDB 777, which is neither a member's id nor a current catalog record. No row is inserted (`film_absent`); the decision is kept. When the catalog record of 777 exists, the replay assigns the slots.
13. **Owner correction.** The owner corrects `O` from TMDB 101 to TMDB 900. The decision batch deletes the two rows of example 1; the insert then writes `(P1, tmdb_900, ch3, T2, discovery)` and `(P1, tmdb_900, ch9, T3, discovery)`; coordinates and order are the same; `generation = 41`. A correction to "not a film" only deletes; both slots are broadcasts again.
14. **Change signal.** Before and after example 1 the fence is `d1:41:P1`; `discovery_authority.generation` and the content digest differ. A reader that reports "unchanged" from the fence alone fails the test.
15. **EPG priority.** During the pause of ADR 0135 no assigned row is written. At the store level, an assigned-row insert between the read of the fence and `commitPublish` leaves that commit's compare-and-set valid: the fence is still `d1:41:P1`.
16. **One increment per sync.** One EPG sync with a changed watchlist moves `generation` by exactly 1.
17. **Failed decision compare-and-set.** The resolver verifies `O` as TMDB 101, and `writeDecisionDelta` skips the key (`conflict`). No insert is attempted; no published table changes.
18. **Crash between the writes.** The decision of example 1 commits and the process stops before the insert. Stored state: decision `verified`, no assigned row. The cheap pass of the next chunk inserts the two rows; the result equals example 1.
19. **Racing owner correction.** The resolver holds decision hash `h1` (TMDB 101). The owner's correction to TMDB 900 commits first. The resolver's insert with `h1` changes zero rows (`decision_changed`). At no time does a row of `F1` exist for `O` after the owner's batch.
20. **Inactive observation or changed slot.** `O` has `seen_in_latest = 0`, or its `residence` is not `active`: zero rows change (`observation_inactive`). The slot's `end_ms` is not the one the writer evaluated: zero rows change (`slot_absent`).
21. **Duplicate slot.** The feed has two elements at `(ch7, T1)`, one matching `F2` and one matching `F3` (or one matching `F2` and one unmatched). The scan retains neither; no sealed row and no observation slot exists for `(ch7, T1)`. Property test: no two retained slots of a scan share `(channelId, start)`. With 150 conflicting slots in one feed, `stats` reports 150 and the `uncertain` list holds at most 100 entries.
22. **Read during a correction.** The correction of example 13 commits while a completed read is between its statements. The read returns the rows of `F1`, or no row of `O`, or the rows of `tmdb_900`; never rows of both Films. The fence is `d1:41:P1` in all three.
23. **Mirror reconciliation.** After examples 1 and 2 the hourly reconcile finds the mirror text different from the completed read and writes `programme.json` and the generation copy with `F1`'s two showings and the catalog entry of `tmdb_900`; `generation = 41`. The next tick is aligned and writes nothing.
24. **Revert after an assignment.** A revert after examples 1 and 2 leaves R2 with the same completed programme. A Discovery write between the revert's comparison and its flip fails the revert (`published_epg_fence`); the mode stays `d1`. After a completed revert an assignment insert changes zero rows.
25. **Catalog reader unavailable.** After examples 1 and 2 the catalog reader refuses (a Discovery fence). `GET /api/programme` answers with the complete member part, `F1`'s assigned showings included, and states that the catalog part is unavailable. It does not fail, and it does not present an empty catalog part as "no Films".
26. **One source of availability.** After example 2, Finds and the film page of TMDB 900 show exactly the showings of the completed read, and the prediction queue ranks `X` as hot from them. With `hotEnteredAt` absent from every record the queue order is deterministic, and its hot and near-airing ranks equal the ranks computed from the same showings.
27. **Aliases stay with the identity.** After example 2 the catalog record of `X` contains the EPG title form of `Q`. After example 1 the programme document is byte-identical: no alias was written into it.

### Implementation outline (estimate for AIR-480)

Existing D1 tables and code only: no migration, no index, no flag, no second publish pipeline, no shared snapshot, no continuous sync, no scheduler, no overlay store. `published_epg_show` already has every needed column (`basis` and `match_reason` are free text; no reader interprets `match_reason`; `film_id` has no foreign key, so a catalog id needs no schema change), and its existing index `(programme_run_id, start_ms)` serves the slot check and the read. `discovery_decision.decision_hash`, `discovery_observation_show.end_ms`, `discovery_admission.state` and `discovery_authority.generation` already exist and carry the guards.

The owner's direction M3 makes the change wider than the earlier estimate. It falls into three parts that can be delivered and accepted one after another, each leaving a consistent state:

1. **Store and replay.** Scan uniqueness and the count; decision-first conditional insert for members and catalog Films; removal with the decision; replay and drift report in the cheap pass; the completed read; mirrors and the revert guard; removal of the `patchProgramme` path. After this part the showings of a member come from rows, and the readers of catalog availability still use the admission payload, which is still written.
2. **Readers.** `GET /api/programme` and the programme screen list the catalog Films of the run; Finds, the film page and the prediction queue take showings from the completed read; `hotEnteredAt` is no longer read.
3. **Cleanup.** After the consumer list of decision 13, the availability fields are no longer written into Films.

Whether AIR-480 is split along these parts is the Planner's decision. The architecture and reference pages that describe the attach and the catalog availability ([EPG and availability](../architecture/epg-and-availability.md), [discovery and resolution](../architecture/discovery-and-resolution.md), [persistence](../architecture/persistence-and-migration.md), [UI and read models](../architecture/ui-and-read-models.md), the [D1 schema reference](../reference/data/d1-schema.md), the [catalog API reference](../reference/api/catalog.md)) change in the implementation PRs, not here, because no flow changes until then.

- **[ADR 0079](0079-d1-authoritative-published-epg.md) decision 4**, "A programme patch records the generation it read and commits only that generation; success is that generation plus one": qualified. Discovery makes no programme patch. Decision 1, "Show rows are the availability facts", becomes literally true for assigned rows and extends to a Film that is not a member. ## Alternatives considered

- **Derive at read, write nothing** (join `discovery_decision`, active `discovery_observation` and `discovery_observation_show` on every programme read). No write at all, but every programme read crosses into the Discovery authority, the active rows move to the new run before the published pointer does during the daily seal, and `discovery_decision` has no index for the lookup. Not recommended.
- **A physical slot table for every retained broadcast.** It would store the single list literally. It needs a migration, a second copy of the observation slots and a change of the EPG publish commit. Excluded by M1.
- **Keep the generation increment and make consumers tolerate it** (the G-P route of the ADR 0141 draft). Excluded by the owner directive.
- **Put a catalog Film into the `films` of the programme document.** One list on the wire, but every consumer that reads `films` as the watchlist (the watchlist fallback, the membership checks, the prediction queue, the mirrors) would treat it as a member. Excluded by M3.
- **Always key an assigned row by `tmdb_<id>`,** also for a member. One rule for the writer, but the statement could then not check that the Film has a record in the run, and the read would have to map every row to a member by TMDB id.
- **Compare the whole evidence key, or the observation's duration set, at replay.** The show part differs in every run and the duration set accumulates, so the comparison either never matches or accepts by accident. Replaced by the per-slot anchor rule of M2.
- **Teach the scan the Discovery aliases** so that the EPG sync itself seals the showing. It changes title matching quality and scope; it is a possible later step (see R2) and does not remove the need for an in-run assignment.
- **One batch for the decision and the assignment.** It removes the repair path for a crash, but the replay is needed anyway for a new EPG run, and one batch would join the decision write to the published tables for every decision. Decision first plus the conditional insert was chosen as the smaller change.

## Consequences

- One Discovery attach costs a few small inserts instead of a rewrite of the document, the members and every show row plus two R2 writes, and it can no longer change the published fence that an EPG publication captured or the `P:g` label of a Producer pass.
- An assignment can no longer outlive or precede its decision; a corrected identity removes its showings in the same transaction.
- The programme a reader gets contains every verified Film that holds a slot, on the watchlist or not. The list on the programme screen grows by the catalog Films of the run; how many that is per run is not measured.
- The showings of a catalog Film have one source, the show rows. Finds, the film page and the prediction queue change their input, and the availability fields of the catalog record stop being read and then stop being written.
- A programme read costs one more indexed statement over the show rows of the run, and the presentation of catalog Films costs a catalog read that `/api/programme` does not make today. The E6 measurement of 12 November 2026 saw 971 to 1,951 show rows per generation. The added latency is not measured.
- `GET /api/programme` gains a part that depends on the Discovery authority. It degrades to "catalog part unavailable"; the member part never depends on it.
- The sealed document no longer contains every showing a reader sees. Only publication internals read it; a caller that compares with a mirror or serves a reader must use the completed read, and a test must fail when one does not.
- `published_epg_show` gets a second writer and a second kind of `film_id`. The boundary is the `basis` value and the insert guards; a test must prove Discovery cannot change a sealed row.
- `basis = 'discovery'` and the observation key in `match_reason` reuse existing columns. This avoids a schema change at the cost of a convention that the reference page must state.
- An assigned row carries no EPG title of its own. The title forms of the broadcast stay on the observation named in `match_reason`.
- The decision writers of Discovery gain a statement on a published table. It is a delete of Discovery's own rows, inside the batch that already holds the Discovery fence.
- A revert can fail and be retried while Discovery is writing. It is an owner-run procedure, and a quiet Discovery is already its normal condition.
- The scan drops both records of a slot that a malformed feed gives to two owners. It can hide a real showing of a Film. How often the production feed does that is not measured; the complete count makes it measurable.
- A recognised broadcast is a broadcast again after each EPG run until the cheap pass reaches it, at 38 verified observations per chunk and with no latency bound. For a watchlist Film this replaces "a broadcast for the whole run". For a catalog Film the scan confirms, it is later than today (R2).
- A slot whose duration left the tolerance stays a broadcast and is reported. Without R1 (a) it waits for the owner.

## Recovery and migration boundary

This record changes no code and no data. The implementation needs no migration and adds rows only to an existing table. A code rollback leaves assigned rows that the older code never reads through its runtime paths and deletes at its next programme patch of that run, and the next EPG run starts without them. After such a rollback readers and mirrors lose the showings the new code assigned in the current run: the older code serves the sealed document and rewrites the mirrors from it at its next reconcile, and it reads the availability of a catalog Film from the catalog record again. For that reason the availability fields stay written until the cleanup part, which has its own rollback boundary. Programme documents already patched by the present attach stay valid as sealed parts of their runs. An authority revert of the published EPG keeps its own fenced, owner-approved procedure; with decision 12 it leaves R2 with the completed programme, exactly what readers saw under D1. Acceptance of this record, the implementation PRs, their deployment and any production observation are separate steps with their own approvals.
