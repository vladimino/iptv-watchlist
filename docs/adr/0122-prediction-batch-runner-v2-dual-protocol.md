# ADR 0122: Prediction batch runner v2 runs beside the v5 runner on one queue, one lease and one persistence path

**Status:** Accepted
**Original decision date:** 2026-11-12 (Product Owner acceptance of ADR 0122 including D7-A; design only)
**Recorded at:** 2026-11-12

**Supersedes:** None. [ADR 0113](0113-prediction-runner-advances-persisted-queue.md), [ADR 0064](0064-prediction-queue-conditional-writes.md) and [ADR 0078](0078-d1-authoritative-prediction-domain.md) stay in force; v2 reuses their queue, lease and D1/R2 persistence.

## Context

Production predictions run through the v5 runner: `prediction_start`, then for every film `prediction_next` (one film), `prediction_validate`, `prediction_submit` (one film), then `prediction_complete` ([contract](../reference/api/prediction-runner.md)). The [AIR-438 evidence](../evidence/prediction/air-438-step-latency-and-batch-sizing-2026-11-11.md) established, from code and an offline profile, that:

- one film costs one `next`, one `validate` and one `submit` whatever the selection batch is (12 while capacity is unknown, at most 20);
- a `next` makes 31 fixed calls plus 2 per selected film, and a one-item `submit` is about half fixed cost, so sharing a `next` and a `submit` among several films is the throughput lever and raising the selection batch alone is not;
- observed cycles on 11 November 2026 were 70–130 s per film (28–49 films per active hour); the model's best case for 10 films per `next` and 10 per `submit` is 57–112 films per active hour, assumptions named, not an SLA;
- the model quality of a multi-film answer, production D1 costs per step and Cloudflare limits above a selection of 20 were not measured.

[AIR-441](https://trello.com/c/fiOKmCHJ) and [AIR-442](https://trello.com/c/j564oeLD) lower the cost of the legacy `next` and `submit`. They do not replace the one-film protocol, which also sends the whole taste profile (about 12.5 KB of text) with every `next`, requires a separate MCP `prediction_validate` call per film, and re-reads the run state per film.

The owner's constraints for the replacement, recorded on the card: one `batch_start` returns a compact manifest of up to **100** eligible films with everything needed to generate them; one scheduled execution handles at most **100** films; generation starts at **10** films, `batch_submit` takes up to **10**; the best generation size (1–10) is chosen by an A/B run; the next hourly execution starts from a clean context; queue, lease, heartbeat and D1/R2 persistence stay shared; the legacy protocol and Task keep working in parallel; no new feature flag.

## Decision

Introduce a **batch runner v2** protocol as an additive second client of the existing prediction machinery. Nothing below is built, deployed or authorized by this ADR.

1. **Two protocols, one machine.** v2 adds `batch_start` and `batch_submit` (HTTP `POST /api/enrichment/batch/start` and `/batch/submit`; MCP `prediction_batch_start` and `prediction_batch_submit`). `heartbeat` and `complete` are shared. The v5 routes and tools are unchanged. A run records `protocol: "v5"` (absent on old records) or `"batch_v2"`; `next`/`submit` refuse a v2 run and `batch_submit` refuses a v5 run with `409 {code: "wrong_protocol"}`, writing nothing. The queue, the runner lease (one 15-minute lease, one holder), `already_running`, retry records, backoff, the fingerprint and prediction storage, the immutable bodies and the D1/R2 write order are the existing ones. A v2 run is a run: it appears in status, run diagnostics and Events like any other.
2. **Manifest.** `batch_start` takes the lease, selects once, and returns one compact manifest of at most **100** films (`maxManifestFilms`: the first 100 eligible films, or all of them when fewer). The manifest is **fail-closed on size**: `maxManifestBytes` bounds the whole serialized response body (profile, contract, schema and every film). `batch_start` first runs a **read-only preflight** (the same read path as the dry run, decision 10: it reads the stored queue, profile, programme and film states and does not heal, rebuild, settle, acquire a lease or emit events), selects the films and serializes the manifest. If it does not fit, `batch_start` answers `409 {status: "blocked", code: "manifest_too_large", bytes, limit, films, largestFilmBytes}` with **zero writes**: no lease mutation, no run record, no Event, no diagnostics row, no queue or film write. Only then does it take the lease, and it **revalidates the selected snapshot** (queue generation, profile, contract and each selected film's input) before it persists the run; a snapshot that moved answers `409 queue_changed`, and one that now overflows answers `manifest_too_large`, with only the lease acquire and release as effects (no run record, no Event; the narrow case in which the zero-write claim does not hold, and the only one). If the stored queue fails the advance gate, `batch_start` **invokes** the ordinary queue refresh that a v5 `start` performs and repeats the preflight. That refresh is a write of `batch_start` itself and part of its effects, reported by the answer (`queueRefreshed: true`), so a `manifest_too_large` that follows it is **not** write-free. The strict alternative, a read-only `409 queue_stale` refusal with no refresh, was not selected (D7, selected A). It never shortens the selection to fit, never drops or truncates a film's `modelMetadata` or stored fingerprint, and never skips a film that is too large on its own: a single film whose entry alone exceeds the limit gives the same refusal with `films: 1` naming `filmId`. The refusal is a stop the owner sees in monitoring (it repeats while the same film leads the queue), and what to do about it is an owner decision, taken on the dry-run evidence (D1). It holds: `run` identity and lease (`runId`, `leaseExpiresAt`, `leaseTtlMs`, `fence`); the **Taste Profile** body, `version`, `hash` and `lineage`; the **contract** (fingerprint schema and its version, prompt versions, registry digest, validation contract version and the schema the Worker validates with); and per film: the full `modelMetadata`, `lane` (`hot` or `cold`, decided when the manifest is formed), and a `plan` of `fingerprint_and_prediction` or `prediction_only` (for the latter the stored valid fingerprint object and its hash). The caller needs no `next`, no further metadata call and no cache in ChatGPT between executions or within one.
3. **Selection at manifest time.** Order and lane follow the existing rule (hot first; cold only while no hot film is outstanding, minus films held by a future `retryAt`), evaluated once at `batch_start`. A hot film that arrives during the execution is not added to the manifest: it waits until this run ends (completion or lease expiry) and the next `batch_start` selects it. A run of 100 films can last longer than an hour and overlap hourly triggers; an overlapping trigger gets `already_running` and stops. There is no maximum wait that the contract promises. `batch_submit` reports `hotArrived` as information only (D5).
4. **Execution shape.** At most 100 films per scheduled execution (the run's `delivered` count, also enforced by the Worker: a second `batch_start` for a running run is `already_running`; it never re-delivers a manifest). The caller generates in groups of `generationSize` (initial 10, A/B-selectable 1–10) and submits each group with one `batch_submit` of **1–10** items (`submitMaxItems`), then repeats, then `complete`. Generation and submit sizes are client choices inside Worker caps and are echoed in the manifest as `limits`; the Worker has no server-side generation size.
5. **Server-side per-film validation.** `batch_submit` validates every item with the same validators and issue codes as `prediction_submit`, with no MCP preflight. `prediction_validate` stays available and optional. A result is one of `created`, `recalculated`, `already_current`, `validation_error` (the retryable item failure with `validation {stage, reportedBy, issues[{path, code}]}`), `stale_profile`, `not_in_manifest`, `deferred_budget` and `conflict` (see the reference). The call is **partial success by design**: HTTP 200 with per-item results, and a non-200 only for a refusal before any item is processed (`lease_lost`, `contract_changed`, `wrong_protocol`, unknown run, run complete, `413`), or for a failure after processing started, which carries `results` and `committed` for the items that already committed (see item 8).
6. **Receipts and input identity, without a new store.** An item is identified by `(runId, filmId, inputDigest)`. `inputDigest` is **one** SHA-256 over the canonical JSON of a single tuple `{filmId, fingerprint, prediction, failure}`, where each component is its **reproducible input projection** or `null` when the item has none (a fingerprint-and-prediction item has both, a prediction-only item has no `fingerprint` component, a failure-only item has only `failure`): for a prediction, only the model-supplied fields after the Worker's own normalization of the submission (`predictedRating`, `probability4Plus`, `confidence`, `matchSignals`, `mainRisk`, `moodLabels`; never `generatedAt`, profile or lineage fields, `confidenceAssessment` or pointer fields), together with the `fingerprintHash` the prediction is for; for a fingerprint, its content `fingerprintHash` (the existing content hash, which already covers `evidence`); for a failure-only item, `{failure.kind}`. The stored prediction keeps the model's fields as submitted, so the same projection is recomputable from committed film state and the digest of a request equals the digest of its committed record. The implementation must prove `project(stored) == project(submitted)` on a corpus including optional fields and normalization. This is **not** the stored version object's own `contentSha256`, which also covers Worker-generated fields and is never compared with a request. The run record (saved per item, as v5 saves per-film results and retry metadata inside its loop) holds `results[filmId] = {outcome, inputDigest, committedAt}`; a failure-only item is recorded in the run record's per-film failure (saved before the queue write, as v5 does), so it needs no film state. Recovery by identity: an identical replay is recognized by the run record, or, in the crash window after the film's pointer commit and before its `results` entry, by the film's stored record carrying this `runId` and a projection whose digest equals the request's; it answers `already_current` with `replayed: true` and writes nothing, in particular no second version object, history row or view-index entry. A different payload for a film this run already committed answers `conflict` and writes nothing. An item with no committed state and no run-record entry was never processed and is processed normally. A crash between the fingerprint pointer and the prediction pointer is recognized per component: a stored fingerprint whose `fingerprintHash` equals the item's component is not written again, and the replay resumes with the prediction; the item's `results` entry is written only when both components are committed, and an optional field normalized differently makes the digest differ (`conflict`), never a silent match. No separate receipt table or object is introduced.
7. **Ownership epoch, same-owner serialization and lease fencing.** Three mechanisms, each on the one existing CAS-protected record, the runner lease. (a) **Epoch.** `epoch` is an integer in the lease value, set once when a run acquires or takes over the lease (previous epoch + 1; 1 when absent) and unchanged by renewal, heartbeat, checkpoint or release (release keeps it so the next acquire increments). The lease's CAS revision (the D1 row token, or the object etag in `r2` mode) changes on every renewal and is internal: never sent to the caller, never required from it. `heartbeat` and every `batch_submit` answer return `{expiresAt, epoch}`. A call checks, from a fresh read, that the lease names this `runId`, the same `epoch` and an unexpired `expiresAt`, then renews through its own CAS; a renewal CAS conflict is re-read and retried (bounded) and becomes `lease_lost` only if the re-read shows another holder, another epoch or expiry. Expiry or a new owner's epoch writes nothing for the old owner. (b) **Required code behavior.** Today's `runnerLease` builds a new lease value on every renewal and would drop an extra field, including on the shared `prediction_heartbeat`. The v2 implementation must make renew, release, heartbeat and checkpoint **merge** the existing value so `epoch` and `submitGate` survive, with v5's externally visible answers unchanged; a test must show a heartbeat between acquire and submit preserving the epoch. An additive field that "v5 readers ignore" is not sufficient by itself. (c) **Submit gate with a commit fence.** The epoch fences different owners, not two requests of the same owner. At the start of a `batch_submit` the lease CAS records `submitGate {requestId, bodyHash, startedAt, expiresAt}` (`expiresAt` = `callBudgetMs` plus a margin, initial 60 s). While an unexpired gate is set, any other `batch_submit` of the run, identical (a replay overlapping the original) or different, answers `409 {code: "submit_in_flight", retryAfterMs}` and does nothing; the caller waits and replays the same body. The call clears the gate with its own final CAS (conditional on its `requestId`); a gate whose handler died expires. **A pre-write check is not a fence**: a handler can pass a check, stall inside one storage call, and commit after its gate expired and was reassigned, and `callBudgetMs` does not bound one slow I/O. So the fence is at the commit boundary: the authoritative per-film commit is **one D1 batch** (the prediction and fingerprint pointers and records) that also carries a guard on the lease row, committing only if the lease still names this `runId`, this `epoch` and this gate `requestId` (the guard and the writes are one atomic batch, the same shape as the existing generation-fenced batches). A handler whose gate was reassigned, or whose lease epoch changed, therefore commits nothing and ends `gate_lost` or `lease_lost`; an immutable R2 body it wrote earlier is content-addressed, create-only and unreferenced, which is harmless, and the view index is overlaid from the D1 pointers on read, so a late write there cannot change a result. The guarded D1 commit requires the `d1` prediction authority marker: while the marker is `r2`, `batch_start` refuses with `409 {code: "batch_runner_requires_d1"}` and v2 does not run. The run-record `results` entry is written by a conditional read-modify-write under the same gate check; a late stale entry can only repeat an identical committed item. Heartbeats do not touch the gate. The alternative of holding the gate until lease ownership expires (slower recovery, no reassignment while the old handler may commit) is the fallback if the guarded batch cannot be built. Tests: an identical replay while the first request is still running (a stand-in blocking at item k), overlapping non-identical batches, a heartbeat between acquire and submit, and a deterministic stall **after the gate check and before the D1 commit**, then gate expiry and reassignment, asserting the stalled handler's batch writes nothing.
8. **Bounded failure and checkpoints, not all-or-nothing.** A `batch_submit` is a sequence of per-film commits followed by one queue settlement; it is not atomic. Per item, in order: validate; write the immutable bodies create-only (R2); commit the D1 pointer, the prediction record and the view-index entry (the existing per-film write path); save the item's result into the run record. After the loop one conditional `batch_progress` write settles every committed success and every recorded retry in the queue (the existing settlement, which removes films from the queue lists and recomputes counters, and is idempotent). Recovery follows from this order: a crash or `502`/timeout after item N leaves items 1..N committed with their results in the run record and the queue not yet advanced; replaying the same call returns those results as `replayed`, runs the items after N, and settles the queue; a crash between the pointer commit and the run-record save is recognized by the stored record's `runId` and the digest of its model-field projection (decision 6) and answers `already_current` with no new version or history row; a queue CAS conflict is retried up to five times as today, and if it is exhausted the committed results are still returned with `queueSettlement: "pending"`, to be settled by the next `batch_submit`, `complete` or a queue refresh (derived from the run record, no result is lost). A failure after processing began answers `502 {code: "storage_error", results, committed}`; only a refusal before the first item writes nothing. The caller recovers by replaying the same body. `callBudgetMs` (initial 25 s) is a **soft budget checked between items**: when it is spent the unprocessed remainder is returned `deferred_budget` without a write. It does not bound a single slow I/O call, so it does not guarantee that a call is free of partial effects. A call is limited to 10 items and to the **64 KiB** MCP inbound limit; a larger body is rejected `413` by the transport before any processing, and the caller halves the group. The retry policy (1-hour exponential backoff, maximum 6 hours) and `format_error_storm` (five consecutive distinct format failures) are unchanged and count items, not calls.
9. **Profile change.** `batch_submit` never answers a bare profile mismatch as a validation error. When the current profile differs from the run's, the call is evaluated by the **existing impact evaluator** ([profile impact](../reference/domain/prediction-compatibility.md), [ADR 0031](0031-selective-prediction-profile-compatibility.md), [ADR 0044](0044-retire-profile-compatibility-switch.md)): the response carries `profile {changed, from, to, scope: "global"|"selective"}` and each item `compatibility: "compatible"|"impacted"|"unknown"`. `compatible` items are written (their stored identity is the profile they were generated with, and freshness is decided by the existing selective rule, so nothing unaffected is recalculated); `impacted` and `unknown` items are returned `stale_profile` and written nowhere, with no retry record and no count towards `format_error_storm`. With `scope: "global"` the response says `action: "complete"` and the caller calls `complete` with the existing stop reason `profile_changed` (a `complete` without a reason is refused `409` while the profile differs, as in v5): the run ends `partial`, the lease is released, the stop writes the run's missing retries and settles nothing else, no unwritten film leaves the queue, and the next `batch_start` rebuilds the queue for the new profile. A changed fingerprint or prediction contract (`contract_changed`) is treated the same way with the existing reason `runner_aborted`; no new stop reason is added. with `selective` the caller may continue with the manifest films the response lists as `compatible`. Films never recalculated because they are `compatible` are not regenerated by the next execution either.
10. **Dry run on a frozen snapshot.** `batch_start` and `batch_submit` take `dryRun: true`. A dry run uses a **separate read-only code path**: it reads the stored queue, profile, programme and film states and calls no queue rebuild, retry-heal, lease, run-record, `sources`, Event, diagnostics, view-index or any D1/R2 writer; if the stored queue fails the advance gate it answers `409 queue_stale` instead of rebuilding. A write-counting storage stand-in test must show zero writes. The dry-run manifest is the **frozen input**: the caller keeps the one manifest it received and replays the same 100 films, metadata, plans and fingerprints from that held copy at generation sizes 1 to 10; it never re-reads the live queue between passes. The manifest carries `manifestHash` (SHA-256 of its canonical form: profile hash, contract, and every film's lane, plan, metadata and fingerprint hash). A dry-run `batch_submit` sends `manifestHash` and, per item, `inputHash`; the Worker has no persisted run, so it **cannot verify membership**: it validates each item against the current contract, requires the manifest's contract version to equal the current one (else `contract_changed`), recomputes each film's current input hash from live state read-only and reports `liveInput: "same"|"changed"|"not_eligible"` so drifted films are excluded from the comparison by the operator. Results and the comparison are not stored by the Worker (D2). A dry run may coexist with a live run; it takes nothing.
11. **Dual protocol and Task selection, no new flag.** The Worker exposes v2 additively; `enrichment_runner` publishes a `batchRunner {version: 2, ...}` capability block and leaves `pipelineVersion: 5` and `modelBatching` as they are for v5 callers. Which hourly Task is active is an owner action on the Task registry: exactly one hourly Predictions Task is enabled at a time (legacy v5 or Task v2). Overlap of two executions, in either protocol, is `already_running` with a stop and no adoption. There is no feature flag and no shadow mode switch; an unused route is inert and writes only when an authorized caller invokes it.
12. **Observation.** `batch_start` and `batch_submit` write the existing log-only `prediction_step_timing` line with `step` of `batch_start` or `batch_submit` and the same whitelisted counters (`items`, `created`, `recalculated`, `alreadyCurrent`, `failures`, `deferredBudget`, `staleProfile`, `manifestFilms`, `manifestBytes`). Monitoring, run diagnostics and the Events footer are unchanged and show a v2 run as a run.
13. **Authority.** No data migration: records, queue, immutable bodies, leases and D1 authority markers keep their shape; a v2 run adds only the `protocol` field and the counts above to the run record, which readers that do not know it ignore. D1 stays the owner of mutable prediction state and R2 of the immutable bodies.

**Not decided here** (open, listed under Open decisions; each has a default that acceptance fixes): the manifest byte budget, the `callBudgetMs`, the optimal generation size, the place of A/B results, whether one repair resubmit per film is allowed, and every production operation.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep v5 and expose larger `next` and `submit` batches** (`next.batchSize` up to 20, `submit` up to 10 items, as the HTTP routes already allow and as the [AIR-438 recommendation](../evidence/prediction/air-438-step-latency-and-batch-sizing-2026-11-11.md#7-recommendation) 4(b) names). Rejected as the target because every `next` still re-reads the whole profile and re-selects, selection above 20 is refused and each selected film adds two reads to every `next`, `submit` still advances the queue once per call and per film item, a separate validate call per film stays, and a change to a shared v5 contract would alter the live Task prompt for the one protocol that must stay unchanged. It remains the fallback if v2 is rejected.
- **One manifest page per `next`-style call (paginated manifest).** Not recorded as discussed by the owner. Rejected: the card asks for one `batch_start`, and pagination returns `next`-like round trips and a profile or contract refetch.
- **ChatGPT-side cache of metadata or profile between executions.** Rejected by the card (no persistent cache in ChatGPT; the next execution starts clean).
- **A feature flag to select the protocol.** Rejected by the card (no new feature flag); Task selection replaces it. A flag would also add a registered binding with its own contract.
- **The Worker generates predictions (a model call from the Worker).** Not discussed on the card; rejected here because it changes the trust boundary and the model credential location that [ADR 0005](0005-external-chatgpt-tasks.md) and the MCP boundary ([ADR 0004](0004-mcp-access-boundary.md)) keep outside the Worker.
- **Raise the MCP 64 KiB inbound body limit.** Not recommended here: the limit is a transport safety boundary ([MCP reference](../reference/api/mcp.md#transport)); the contract bounds the item count and tells the caller to split. Raising it needs its own security decision.
- **A server-side validation-only batch call (preflight in bulk).** Rejected: `batch_submit` validates per film anyway and a second call per group reintroduces the handshake the card removes.

## Consequences

- **Fewer, larger calls.** Per execution of 100 films: one start, ten generate-and-submit pairs, one complete (12 Worker calls instead of about 300 per execution on v5). The profile and contract cross the wire once instead of once per film. Throughput is not promised: the [AIR-438 model](../evidence/prediction/air-438-step-latency-and-batch-sizing-2026-11-11.md#5-throughput-model-assumptions-named-not-measured) gives 57–112 films per active hour for 10/10 under named assumptions, and model quality at 10 films per answer is unknown (D6).
- **Larger responses and a heavier start.** The manifest of up to 100 films is the largest answer the runner has produced (estimate 100–450 KiB, [analysis](../evidence/prediction/air-446-batch-runner-limits-2026-11-12.md)). It reads 100 films' state in one invocation (about 200 reads plus the by-id catalog read in chunks of 80), within the documented 10,000 subrequest default and the 30 s CPU default, but production wall and CPU are unmeasured. If the real manifest does not fit, `batch_start` refuses with `manifest_too_large` and the owner decides; the Worker never shortens films.
- **Context-window risk.** 100 manifest films plus generation outputs may not fit one model context; the byte cap (D1) and the 10-film generation groups bound it. A caller that loses its context loses only unsubmitted work, which stays in the queue.
- **A coarser checkpoint.** A crash loses at most the films generated since the last `batch_submit`, at most `generationSize` films, and nothing the Worker already committed. A `batch_submit` is a sequence of per-film commits, not an atomic unit: partial effects are possible and are recovered by replaying the same body (decision 8).
- **More states in a response** (`stale_profile`, `deferred_budget`, `conflict`) and a second protocol to document, test and retire. The v5 code stays until a separate cleanup card.
- **Cross-protocol guard.** A run belongs to one protocol; a client that mixes calls gets `wrong_protocol`, never a half-applied step.
- **Compatibility.** Records, the retry policy, hot-first selection and the validation contract do not change; old run records without `protocol` are v5.

## Recovery and migration boundary

- **Reversible.** Rollback is: disable the v2 Task and re-enable the v5 Task (owner action on the Task registry), and revert the v2 routes by an ordinary code rollback. A v2 run that stopped mid-way expires by lease like any run; its unsubmitted films never left the queue; its settled films stay settled and valid. There is no data migration, so none is reversed. Rolling the code back does not roll back D1, R2, flags, Access or Task schedules.
- **Owner approval is separate for** each production operation: deploying the v2 code, any dry run that spends model quota or reads production, enabling or editing a Scheduled Task prompt, the first natural v2 run, the A/B, and the switch of the active Task. The Product Owner decision on this ADR is a gate for Accepted, not production GO. This ADR authorizes no operation.
- **Plan (separate cards, no data migration):** manifest builder; `batch_submit` with validation, fencing, replay and profile handling; Task v2 prompt (proposed text only until the owner enables it); dry-run A/B; cutover (owner-approved holdpoints and the v5 Task disabled); cleanup of the v5 code after stabilization. Each step stays offline and tested before any production use.

## Addendum 2026-11-12: corrections found while implementing batch_submit

This addendum records implementation-time corrections to the decision text above. It does not rewrite the historical decision and it is not a new Product Owner decision: it follows the intent of decisions 6 to 8 and 10, and its source is the independent review of [PR #549](https://github.com/vladimino/iptv-watchlist/pull/549) (delivery of [AIR-448](https://trello.com/c/0Huo5rW6)).

- **Gate ownership (decision 7c).** The submit gate is `{token, requestId, bodyHash, startedAt, expiresAt}`. Ownership, renewal, clearing and the D1 commit guard use `token`, which the Worker generates for each call. The caller-supplied X-Request-Id is a log field only: it cannot identify an owner, because a superseded handler and its replacement can carry the same id. While any gate is live, no second acquisition succeeds, whatever its id or body. The text above says `requestId` for these four roles.
- **Gate before the writer lease (decision 7c).** The gate is taken before the global prediction writer lease, so an overlapping call is answered `submit_in_flight`; global writer exclusion is unchanged and the writer lease is taken under the gate.
- **Fenced writes (decision 8).** The commit guard covers every authoritative write of a call, not only the per-film D1 batch: the run record, a failed film's retry record, the queue settlement and the progress checkpoint. Loss of the gate or the epoch is terminal for all of them. A progress Event is written only after a guarded checkpoint that stores it as an intent; a handler that loses its gate before the Event write leaves the intent for the next holder.
- **No shared step recorder (decision 8).** A v2 submit is not passed through the v5 step recorder: that recorder appends diagnostics steps and, for a lost lease or a storage failure, writes a stop for the run without the gate or the epoch. An unexpected error after processing began is a recoverable `502` with the results of the films already processed, not a stop of the run.
- **Receipt identity (decision 6).** For every recorded outcome, a failure included, a changed digest is `conflict` and only the identical payload is a replay. A failed film is still not retried in the run.
- **Result names (decision 5).** The delivery scope fixed `needs_regeneration` (this ADR's `stale_profile`) and `validation_failure` (this ADR's `validation_error`); the semantics are those described above.
- **Epoch mismatch (decision 7a).** A request whose `epoch` is not the run's is `409 epoch_mismatch`, a caller error that never stops the run, rather than `lease_lost` (which would record a stop for the real owner).

## Addendum 2026-11-12: manifest ceiling raised to 256 KiB (AIR-450)

Decision D1 reserved the byte limit of `batch_start` for the owner after a measurement, and the accepted text above is not rewritten. The owner approved raising `maxManifestBytes` from the initial 160 KiB to **256 KiB (262,144 bytes)** after a bounded production canary was refused with `manifest_too_large` (`bytes` 239,968 for 100 films, `largestFilmBytes` 3,265) at 163,840. The fail-closed behaviour of decision 2 is unchanged: nothing is shortened, skipped or replaced, and an answer over the new ceiling is the same `409`. Only the constant changed; the 100 films, the compact format, `pin` and snapshot rules, the queue, the lease, D1/R2 authority and the submit limits did not. The measurement, the MCP result size (about twice the answer) and the unproven client side are in the [AIR-450 evidence](../evidence/prediction/air-450-manifest-limit-256kib-2026-11-12.md). No architecture diagram changes: no flow, authority or trust boundary moved.

## Addendum 2026-11-12: a read-only group preflight before submit (AIR-450)

The statement above that validation happens once, at submit, so that no `prediction_validate` call is needed, is refined by [ADR 0131](0131-batch-candidate-preflight-tool.md) (owner decision after checkpoint C6): the batch protocol gains an optional MCP tool `prediction_batch_validate` that checks 1 to 10 candidates with the same validators and writes and reads nothing. The validation at `batch_submit` is unchanged and stays authoritative (it also checks the manifest, `inputHash` and the live profile). The accepted text above is not rewritten.
