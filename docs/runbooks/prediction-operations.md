## Research depth and full-text fallback diagnostics

The Predictions Task's recurring schedule is a target, not a Worker Cron or an exact trigger guarantee. Read the live Scheduled Tasks registry to determine which task is enabled, its actual recurrence, `last_run` timestamps and saved report. Do not launch a manual run or reset leases to populate diagnostics. The [maintained prompt](../reference/scheduled-task-prompts/hourly-predictions-batch-v2.md) defines the per-film research audit and full-text fallback behavior.

For each naturally occurring run, compare the saved Task result with approved read-only Worker status and existing diagnostics when needed. Separate **technical success** (batch completed, submitted items and per-film validation valid) from **research success** (a genuinely read original review, a traceable author and URL, and a concrete execution finding beyond the synopsis). Research audit rows correspond only to films actually selected and examined; `already_running`, `noop` or an early block can produce zero rows. Count actual searches (maximum two per film), independent authored reviews separately from URLs, and full/partial/snippet accesses (F/P/S). Check `gateStatus`, `gateReason`, source URL, author, execution finding and `fullTextFallback` outcome. An opened but unread page is an attempt, not `full_text`; reported CAPTCHA, cache miss and other access failures must reflect observed responses. A successful Worker schema validator does not prove that a source was read or a finding was accurate.

When original full text is unavailable, verify a meaningful alternate-title/author/language pivot if a second search is allowed, actual attempts to read other discovered original reviews, and an honest final fallback reason within the existing budget. Preserve uncertainty about independent availability checks performed after the Task run: a later read cannot establish what the Task could access earlier. Do not modify Scheduled Tasks, Worker flags, the queue, or submission state without explicit authorization. [The historical prediction evidence ledger](../evidence/prediction/README.md) records dated rollout decisions and observations; this runbook states the reusable verification procedure.

**Batch v2 deferred submit integrity.** A `deferred_budget` result is an expected per-call budget boundary, not a generation failure. Preserve the exact submit-ready item accepted by `prediction_batch_validate` when re-grouping into the next submit. Do not reconstruct, omit or truncate its fingerprint, prediction, assessment or evidence. If that item is lost or modified, invoke authoritative `prediction_batch_validate` on the actual content before submitting and correct only returned Worker issues. No parallel client-side enum validator. This is not authorization for a manual production retry.

## Proportionate triage

For a single deferred film, ordinary backoff or an interrupted but recoverable run, prefer the next Scheduled Task and its built-in retry/resume. Do **not** require a complete queue audit, full R2 snapshot, new model batch, manual lease reset or per-item diagnostic inspection just to establish that one retryable failure occurred. Read the queue/runner summary first; use film diagnostics only for persistent, identity-sensitive or unexplained failures, and broad parity only for a demonstrated cross-film inconsistency. Keep owner gates for manual producer calls and hard ownership/lease fences: retrying a non-idempotent model submission blindly can duplicate work or corrupt run state. [Risk-proportional policy](../process/agent-workflow.md#risk-proportional-operational-safeguards).

## Hourly discovery preflight and orphaned checkpoints

An EPG run that is still in `seal` or `finalize`, or a programme report whose `runId` does not match a completed sync, is not a reason to skip Predictions. The Worker keeps scoring trusted films from the stored queue; showtimes affect only hot/cold order, and only from a completed snapshot or from last-known priority no older than 36 hours ([ADR 0154](../adr/0154-prediction-eligibility-independent-of-epg.md)). Do not start a manual EPG or prediction run to prove that.

The hourly Task's discovery catch-up runs **before** prediction processing, but a blocked discovery continuation must not stop safe prediction work. Read `probe_read`, `epg_status` and `epg_pipeline_status`; verify the currently published `programmeRunId` and the pinned continuation generation. Only a server-authorized existing-generation continuation may be advanced by a separate, owner-authorized writer execution. A healthy paused checkpoint may allow ordinary sequential `epg_sync_step` calls; each call runs no more than one discovery chunk.

For `continuation.stage=running`, do **not** treat elapsed wall time, a stale event, or the `running` value alone as permission to recover. Use the Worker's read-only `continuationHealth`:
- **`live` / live lease:** another writer may still own the work. Do not call `epg_sync_step`.
- **`orphaned` with `lease=expired|none`:** the Scheduled Task may issue one ordinary step **only** after independently confirming an exact match with the published generation in both read-only status responses, `generationConflict=false`, positive integer `remainingChunks`, `resumeBudget` within that budget, and no other active lease, hard wait or future retry timestamp. The Worker, not the caller, settles a hop-marked completed chunk or safely resumes an unmarked/incomplete chunk. A missing hop marker does not prove that the older chunk completed.
- **Missing/unknown health, wrong generation, `settled`, `busy`, or conflicting lease:** fail closed; never reset the lease, overwrite the checkpoint, force a retry, or launch a new EPG run from the hourly task.

After the step, inspect `discovery.nextAction`: `continue` permits only the next verified step on the same published generation; `wait` honors backoff and `nextCatchUpAt`; `stop` ends discovery. An MCP error, a timeout or a lost response is not proof the underlying write was rolled back: the chunk's checkpoint is written before the lease release, the diagnostics entry and the response, so re-read `epg_pipeline_status` first; a `settled` continuation whose `hopId` matches `lastBatch.hopId` means the step committed and only its response was lost, and the next ordinary step continues it (after the lease expires if its release was lost too). A published programme and a completed discovery continuation are separate milestones. For deeper checkpoint interpretation see [MCP connectivity and recovery](mcp-connectivity.md); for the caller's expected rules see the [Scheduled Task contract](../reference/scheduled-tasks.md).

## ChatGPT Scheduled Tasks: timing and troubleshooting

**The hourly schedule is a target, not an exact Worker execution time.** As read from the external registry on 17 November 2026, v2 Tasks 00/15/30/45 have nominal hourly triggers :00/:15/:30/:45 Europe/Madrid. The ChatGPT Scheduled Task is external to Cloudflare Worker `iptv` (there is no hourly Cloudflare Cron driving predictions). Recurrence lives in the Task registry, which the Worker does not read; the Worker does not encode `:00`, `:15` or `:20`. An invocation can begin well after its nominal minute; delays can vary and there is **no documented guaranteed window**. The footer's `Следующий запуск по расписанию: —` does not invent a Task time (see [timing](../reference/scheduled-tasks.md#timing-nominal-trigger-versus-server-gate)). It is not a countdown, SLA, or evidence that a new run has started or that the external task can run. A recognition warning that execution was not confirmed is not a stored usage-limit stop, and it is withheld when the discovery batch or the checkpoint time is missing, unparseable or from another generation: the recognition line is then unknown evidence. Only a prediction run that recorded `stopReason` `quota` (or another stored hard stop: storage, model, API) is shown as a usage limit (`prediction_blocked` / «прогнозы приостановлены»), and that code stays until a later run adds, recalculates, or drains — a newer `external_termination` with no recovery does not clear it. A later `partial` or `external_termination` of one run is kept on the last-run / Events line; it does not become `prediction_blocked` or the aggregate «Автоматизация: есть сбой» while recent diagnostics still show added or drained work. A `complete` that only recorded item failures is not that recovery, even when `stopReason` is the writer's normal `eligible_queue_empty` or `manifest_spent`. Repeated consecutive interrupts with eligible-now work use `prediction_runs_interrupted`; a six-hour gap with eligible-now work and no added/drained run, including an old attempt with no prior success, uses `prediction_no_recent_progress`. A deferred-only queue is not a stall. The Worker does not read the task registry or a usage account ([what the footer can tell](../reference/scheduled-tasks.md#what-the-footer-can-tell-about-a-missed-hourly-run)). Times shown in the app use Europe/Madrid. A discovery `nextCatchUpAt` on the whole hour is the server's earliest permitted step, not a ChatGPT Task time; `discovery.nextScheduledRunAt` is unused and `null`.

**No confirmed progress (AIR-556, [ADR 0182](../adr/0182-prediction-progress-policy-shared-by-footer-and-monitor.md)).** With eligible work and no productive batch or drained run for 60 minutes the Footer shows «нет подтверждённого прогресса прогнозов» (attention, with the last success time as history) and the Monitor sends one `predictions_no_progress` alert at the next hourly tick, 60 to about 120 minutes after the last progress. Both read the same facts. Requests that never reach the Worker (for example an expired MCP authorization) leave no record, so neither surface names `api_error`, an authorization cause or a count of missed Tasks; confirm that in the ChatGPT Task history. A live run counts as running for at most 60 minutes. A batch that saved some films and failed others is normal (the failures are in `lastRun.failed` and the item diagnostics, not in the aggregate); a failed-only batch is not progress, and the 60-minute rule or a hard stop (`api_error`, `quota`, `model_unavailable`, `storage_error`, `format_error_storm`) decides health. Nothing eligible, or everything deferred by its own retry time, is not a stall. Read-only checks: `GET /api/monitor/status` (stream `predictions`, `openIssues`), the footer endpoint, `enrichment_status`, `prediction_queue_overview`.

**Do not infer scheduler accuracy from /events timestamps.** A `Прогнозы запущены` event of the retired v5 runner (historical) recorded a server-side run start; `Прогнозы восстановлены` or `Прогнозы продолжены` describes a lease recovery/continuation, not necessarily a fresh scheduled trigger. A new batch v2 run has no start card: its one Events item is the terminal «Пакет прогнозов обработан» (or with errors / interrupted) after complete. Progress/completion events on the retired v5 path could appear much later. In the Events history captured on 15–21 October 2026, visible event times included minutes around `:50` through `:07`, plus a `:22` outlier; these mixed event types do **not** establish a reliable scheduler start-time distribution. For exact scheduler triggers, inspect the Scheduled Task execution history separately.

**An unchanged status is not a missed trigger.** The footer can retain a previous `storage_error` until a later run emits updated state, and an invocation with `already_running`, `busy`, or `noop` may not produce a new Events entry. Verify independently:

1. Check the ChatGPT Scheduled Task is enabled and review its last execution time/result; do not treat the next nominal clock hour as a deadline.
2. Via authorized IPTV Watchlist MCP, inspect `enrichment_runner`, `enrichment_status`, and `enrichment_queue`, including `runId`, `state`, `lastHeartbeatAt`, `hotRemaining`, `coldRemaining`, and retry/backoff.
3. `pendingCount=0` only means no immediate *hot/watchlist* work. It does **not** mean the full database is complete if `coldRemaining>0` (the queued runner can process off-watchlist cold films). Validate complete coverage using `confirmedFilmsTotal` versus `currentPredictionsTotal`, not the viewing-window counter. `localPercent` and `windowPercent` are tenths of a percent. The value `100` means the two counts are equal, or that the denominator was empty. An incomplete pair stays at or below `99.9`. A finished run is separate from a drained queue: `queue_empty` is stored only when the refreshed queue has no outstanding film, and `eligible_queue_empty` means films remain that the run could not take (deferred by retry or waiting behind hot work). Films with ten saved per-film retryable failures are `retryExhausted`: they are not eligible, not deferred, and not predicted. The footer line and the completion Event say which: «все фильмы с актуальными прогнозами» (drained), «N фильмов ожидают повторной попытки не раньше HH:MM» (deferred; the time is the film's stored `retryAt` in Europe/Madrid, not the next scheduled run) or «N фильмов готовы к следующему запуску» (eligible now). The footer detail «Отложены до повтора» and `prediction_queue_overview` (`pending.deferredByRetry`, `pending.retryExhausted`, `pending.nextRetryAt`) show the same slices; a deferred film needs no action, the ordinary schedule retries it; an exhausted film is an owner-review candidate (`prediction_pending` `filter: exhausted`), not a catalog exclusion and not a manual retry. See [the completion semantics](../reference/api/prediction-runner.md#run-completion-drained-queue-eligible-and-deferred-work) and [the recorded investigation of the earlier mismatch](../evidence/prediction/air-82-prediction-rca.md).
4. Check /events for the corresponding run: a batch v2 run shows **one** terminal packet Event (`presentation: batch_v2_terminal`) with «Обработано» and named success/failure groups when that object stores `details` (kinds, web-research access, and conflict authors only for a full or partial read). A start that fails after allocating the run (`storage_error` / CAS at persist) is still that packet: «Пакет прогнозов прерван», «Обработано: 0», no coverage lines. Older objects without `details` keep the earlier packet counters. `already_running` and idle `noop` write none. If a run is stuck or `storage_error` recurs, inspect authorized Cloudflare logs and stored checkpoints rather than forcibly clearing leases or resetting the queue.
5. When a run reports `failed > 0` with item `parse_error` rows, read `prediction_film_diagnostics` for each deferred film id (`prediction_pending` with `filter: deferred` lists them) and for exhausted ids (`filter: exhausted`, `cause: prediction_retry_exhausted`) and look at `retry.validation`. Do not treat exhausted films as deferred. `stage: fingerprint_schema` or `prediction_schema` with `issues` such as `fingerprint.execution.payoff invalid_enum` or `fingerprint.moodLabels required` means the model output violated the published contract: the fix is in the caller's generation (compare with `enrichment_schema` `contract`), not in the Worker. `stage: caller_reported` means the caller itself reported the failure; on a batch v2 write `validation.claimed` / `retry.validation.claimed` carries its required reason (`preflight_validation_failed`, `insufficient_evidence`, `json_unparseable`, `request_too_large`) and optional echoed preflight issues, shown with `provenance: caller_claim`: treat it as the caller's statement, not Worker telemetry; a historical record without `claimed` was stored before reason was required; `validation: null` with `classification: storage_error` is a system failure. A retry record without a validation summary has `validation: null`; it gains a summary only after a subsequent rejection. Do not reset `retryAt` or restart the runner to see the summary sooner; the deferred film is retried by the ordinary schedule. Field paths and codes are defined in [the submit validation contract](../reference/api/enrichment.md#submit-validation-contract).
6. Read-only check of the validation contract after a Worker deploy: call `enrichment_schema` and confirm `contract.version`, `contract.issueCodes` and the `execution` enum lists; call `prediction_batch_validate` with one deliberately wrong item (for example `execution.payoff: "medium"`, no `moodLabels`) and expect `success: false`, `valid: false` with those two issues on that item and `storage: not_accessed`, and with an item that follows the contract expect `success: true`, `valid: true` and empty `issues`; then read `prediction_film_diagnostics` for a currently deferred film and confirm its `retry.attempts`, `retryAt` and `classification` are what they were before the deploy. None of these steps writes. A live `results[].validation` on a real rejection is observed on the next authorized or scheduled run, not forced for a smoke test. If the connector does not list `prediction_validate`, its tool list is cached: refresh or reconnect the server in the client; do not work around it with a run. The hourly production task already requires `prediction_validate` before **every** `prediction_submit`. If the tool is unavailable, fail closed with `prediction_validate_unavailable`, never bypass it. For `fingerprint_and_prediction`, both checked flags must be true; for `prediction_only`, only the prediction is checked and the existing Worker fingerprint is reused. Require `success: true`, `valid: true`, matching contract version and the exact unchanged payload; correct and revalidate any changed fields. A tool error or a report without `success` and `valid` is the validator being unavailable, not a schema failure of the film: it must not be submitted as a per-item `validation_error`. Future changes to the task prompt or schedule still require separate owner approval and documentation review.

## Evidence confidence assessment

`src/domain/prediction-confidence.js` scores the fingerprint of one submit. The contract is [prediction confidence](../reference/domain/prediction-confidence.md). Submit stores `confidenceAssessment` on that new prediction snapshot and leaves the model's `prediction.confidence` unchanged. A caller value of the same name is discarded. Catalog, view, Finds, Similar, EPG and the hourly task do not call the function. A read does not recompute a stored object and does not add the key to an older snapshot.

Merging the Worker deploy is what makes the next natural submit write the field. A `prediction_only` item reuses its stored fingerprint: with no usable evidence the new snapshot is `legacy` or `insufficient` and its effective level is `low`, so it can leave Finds even when the model said `high`. That is the intended fail-closed result. It is also why merge needs a separate owner GO: a code deploy starts this write on the next scheduled submit. Do not start a model run, edit the hourly task, bump a prompt version, or recompute history to manufacture a sample. Production acceptance is a later read of one new snapshot after that GO, plus a Finds and Similar comparison. This page does not record that snapshot.

## Prediction domain marker

Mutable prediction state is D1-authoritative after migration `0007` and activation on 3 November 2026 ([ADR 0078](../adr/0078-d1-authoritative-prediction-domain.md), [D1 reference](../reference/data/d1-schema.md#prediction-domain-schema-v7)). A code deploy does not apply the file and does not switch the marker. Production cutover and the first successful natural run are recorded in [dated evidence](../evidence/d1-migration/air-317-production-cutover-2026-11-03.md). The external Stage B prompt is the evidence instruction in the section above.

Only mode `d1` is served ([ADR 0162](../adr/0162-d1-only-authority-retire-rollback-toolchain.md)); `writes` `held` is the normal state after activation and does not matter. A marker in mode `r2`, a missing table, a contract below 7, a missing `schema_contract` or a missing `DB` binding fails the prediction step with `prediction_domain_unavailable` and does not read the objects. The deployed Worker sets `PREDICTION_DB_REQUIRED` to `on`; only a local caller that omits that variable and the binding stands in with the objects. The batch runner v2 answers `502` `prediction_domain_unavailable` for such a marker, and `409` `batch_runner_requires_d1` only for the local stand-in.

The offline script `scripts/prediction-domain-authority.mjs` and the load, activate, revert, release and reconcile commands of the former cutover are removed ([ADR 0162](../adr/0162-d1-only-authority-retire-rollback-toolchain.md)). If a restore of the D1 database ever brings the marker back to `r2`, the Worker refuses it and there is no tool to reactivate: recovery is a forward-fix or an owner-approved whole-database restore ([recovery](recovery/d1-authority-and-time-travel.md)).

The production entry is `scripts/prediction-domain-production.mjs`. With no command it prints `{configured:false,writes:false}` and does not call the network. The status command (`--target iptv`) reads the marker raw, so it also shows an uninstalled domain and a marker in mode `r2`. A write names `--target iptv` and its own confirm: `--apply` / `--confirm apply` plus `--producers-drained`, or `--materialize-fingerprints` / `--confirm materialize`. `--load`, `--activate`, `--revert`, `--release` and `--reconcile` answer `retired` before any request. `--producers-drained` is the operator's statement that this Worker build is the one serving, that no prediction request is in flight, and that no request which read prediction state on the previous build is still between that read and its put. Deploying the code is not that drain. `--apply` then reads the runner and writer leases and sends no migration statement if either lease is active or those objects cannot be read. It sends only `0007` as one `{sql}` request when the contract is version 6 or higher and these tables are absent. Version 6 reads back as 7. A higher version reads back as that same version, mode `r2`, generations 0, `writes` `open`; it does not lower the contract, and the Worker refuses that marker. `--materialize-fingerprints` reads the mutable fingerprint pointers and writes only a missing immutable body, create-only, when stripping the pointer's provenance leaves a body whose content hash is the pointer's `fingerprintHash`. It repairs an immutable R2 body and is not a return to R2. An existing body is left in place when it is that body. A pointer that does not reconstruct, or an existing body that differs, refuses before any put. The command does not write a generation record, does not change D1 and does not apply `0007`. A put that has already created a body and then cannot prove the next object is that body reports `writeState` `partial`. An unacknowledged PUT is `writeState` `unknown`, `writes` true, and is not retried; a later run inventories create-only state again. A HEAD that fails before that PUT is not an uncertain write: acknowledged bodies stay `partial`, and a failure before any body is `writes` false. A lost D1 response to `--apply` is `writeState` `unknown` and is not retried. An unrecognized failure stays `reason` `failed` and adds `detail`: the error name, a short code or kind, and a message with hashes, film ids, credentials and stored bodies removed. `detail` names the failure. It does not authorize another attempt.

Do not perform those production state changes without the applicable owner-approved L3 plan and its holdpoints. Safe read-only preflight/readback may be included in that plan without separate per-read approval. Applying `0007` and `--materialize-fingerprints` are each their own approval.

While `mode` is `d1`, a prediction read that cannot verify the immutable body fails. Do not copy the mirror object back over the row. A queue conflict is still five attempts and then `prediction_queue_conflict`. The stored queue text is at most 900000 characters, and one film keeps at most 80 history rows. The queue is stored plain while its plain JSON fits and in the compact `dict1` encoding when it does not; readers always get the plain document ([reference](../reference/api/prediction-runner.md#queue-writes)). A write that does not fit even encoded fails and changes nothing.

## Prediction queue conflicts

A runner step (`next`, `submit`, `complete`) advances the stored queue and does not rebuild it, so Discovery chunks do not stall a run ([ADR 0113](../adr/0113-prediction-runner-advances-persisted-queue.md)); a `start` answer with `queueStale` means the rebuild could not read the catalog and the run uses the stored queue (read it as the state of its `refreshedAt`). Every writer of the prediction queue writes conditionally and rebuilds on conflict ([queue writes](../reference/api/prediction-runner.md#queue-writes), [ADR 0064](../adr/0064-prediction-queue-conditional-writes.md)). Until the schema v7 marker is `d1`, the condition is the object etag. In that mode it is the D1 generation. A conflict that outlasts 5 attempts is reported, never written over:

| Where | What it looks like | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| prediction run | a `storage_error` stop, with `Prediction queue changed during write (<trigger>)` in Cloudflare logs; `retryHeal: "pending"` in the stop answer if the stop could not write the run's retries either | Nothing. The next scheduled run continues. The run's retries are written by its stop or, failing that, by the next start before it selects anything. Do not reset `retryAt`, clear leases or delete the queue |
| prediction start | `503 {status: "blocked", code: "prediction_retry_heal_pending", pendingRunId}` | Nothing. The previous run's retries could not be written yet, so this start selected nothing. It is not a stop: it writes no Event, and the last run shown stays the previous run. The next scheduled start tries again. Do not reset or delete the runner lease: it is what names the run whose retries are pending. If it persists across many hours, report it to the owner with `pendingRunId` |
| prediction start | `503 {status: "blocked", code: "prediction_catalog_held", cause}` | Nothing. An EPG publication is sealing and holds the catalog (`cause` `discovery_fence` or `discovery_incomplete`). The start kept no lease and wrote no Event. The next scheduled start tries again after the publication; read `epg_status` to see the seal. Do not repeat the start in a loop and do not touch the Discovery authority or leases |
| prediction next, submit or complete | `503 {status: "blocked", code: "prediction_catalog_held", cause}` | Nothing on the Worker. The catalog is held during an owned run. The run stays `running`, committed predictions are kept, there is no stop Event. `submit` may already have written the film (`results`, `committed`); an identical retry is `already_current`. Retry the same step with the same `runId` for at most `leaseTtlMs` (12 minutes), with `prediction_heartbeat` at least every 5 minutes; if still held, stop calling and leave the lease to expire — do not `prediction_complete` and do not treat it as `storage_error`. The live Task prompt still treats a mid-run 503 as a systemic error until an owner-approved prompt edit Since AIR-431 this appears only when the step needs the catalog (a rebuild after a programme, profile or contract change, or the by-id read of a new batch); a Discovery chunk alone no longer causes it. |
| EPG commit | `enrichmentQueueError` on `sync.json` | Nothing. The commit is complete, and the next rebuild (any prediction step, profile update or resolver chunk) refreshes the queue |
| resolver chunk | `prediction_queue_checkpoint: …` in the discovery status | Nothing beyond the ordinary discovery steps |
| owner reconcile | `409 {code: "prediction_queue_conflict"}` | Another writer is active. Read `enrichment_queue` later; a repeated reconcile still needs its own owner approval |

Repeated conflicts across many hours are a finding for the owner. Look in the logs for which writers overlap; never fall back to an unconditional write.

**Budget refusals.** `enrichmentQueueError: prediction_domain_budget:<guard>` in `epg_status` names the limit that refused the rebuild; the Worker also writes one log line `{"event":"prediction_domain_budget","guard":"<guard>"}` without film ids. `queue_chars` means the document is over 900000 characters even encoded; `overlay_rows` means a prediction table holds more than 20000 rows (the view-index overlay reads the rows, not the index keys); `statement_bytes`, `bind_limit`, `history_limit` and `mirror_list` are the other fixed limits. A refusal writes nothing, so the queue keeps its previous generation and `prediction_queue_overview` shows `queue_programme_differs`. Do not start a prediction run to test it: a start that cannot rebuild the queue still takes the runner lease.

**A start that answered 502 `storage_error`.** The answer does not say where it failed. Read the Worker log line `prediction_boundary_failed` for that start (`requestId`, `runId` as in the run's diagnostics): `stage` names the step and `code`/`guard` the error. `stage: queue_refresh` with `code: prediction_domain_budget` and a `guard` is a queue or overlay budget refusal; `stage: unknown` means the error did not cross a named step. A failed start released its runner lease. Do not repeat the start to look again: each attempt takes the lease and writes a blocked run.

**A start whose answer never arrived.** A caller that reports an unserializable or missing `prediction_batch_start` answer while `enrichment_status` shows a `running` run with `delivery: unconfirmed` did not get a Worker error body. Read the Worker invocation for that minute in Cloudflare telemetry: `outcome: exceededMemory` with HTTP 503 means the isolate was ended after the run was saved and before the answer was formed; there is then no `prediction_boundary_failed` line, because the isolate did not survive to write it. The run is real: leave it to the ordinary lease expiry and recovery, do not adopt its `runId` from a read, and do not repeat the start to check.

**Rollback.** A code revert restores the previous blind writes; the stored queue format is the same while the queue is plain, so no data step is needed. Once the queue is stored in the `dict1` encoding, a build without that support cannot read it, fails closed (`prediction_domain_unavailable`) and cannot rebuild it, so a blind code rollback leaves the prediction steps failing. Roll back only to a build that still reads `dict1`, or first ship a compatible build that rewrites the queue as plain (a rebuild that fits under the cap writes plain text). Do not edit or reset the queue rows to bypass this. A revert does not restore retry metadata that an earlier overwrite already lost. Continue with the ordinary schedule; do not reconcile or edit the queue to "repair" it without a separate owner approval.

## Reading the step timing line (AIR-438)

`batch_start`, `batch_submit` and `batch_hold` log one `prediction_step_timing` line each ([fields](../reference/api/prediction-runner.md#step-timing-line)). It is a safe read: nothing starts, retries or writes. To see where a run's time went:

1. Query Workers Observability for the Worker `iptv` with the needle `prediction_step_timing` and the time range of the run (the recipe used for the `epg_phase` events works the same: `POST /accounts/{id}/workers/observability/telemetry/query`, view `events`, filter `$metadata.service = iptv`, a `needle`). The line is the event's `source`.
2. Compare `wallMs` with `busyMs`: a large `unattributedMs` is awaits and scheduling (the Workers clock does not advance during synchronous execution, so compute reads as zero, as the platform's `cpuTimeMs` of the invocation shows: read CPU there), a large `busyMs` is storage. `overlapMs` says how much of the storage time ran in parallel.
3. Read `classes` for the stage that holds the time: `r2:queue` and `d1:*` for the stored queue and the prediction tables, `r2:view_index`, `r2:film_state`, `r2:lease`, `r2:run`.
4. Count the D1 round trips (`d1.n`) of each batch step per Worker version; the Worker reads the authority marker in one batch and holds it for the request until the step's first database write ([contract](../reference/api/prediction-runner.md#step-timing-line)), which shows as the classes `d1:schema_contract`, `d1:sqlite_master` and `d1:prediction_domain_authority` disappearing from the line (the authority batch is one `d1:batch` operation). Reverting only the request scope (the commit that adds `scopeRequest`) keeps the one-batch read and restores a marker read on every call.
5. Group by `step`, by the deploy (`$workers.scriptVersion.id`) and by `notes.manifestFilms` or `notes.bodyItems` before taking p50 and p95; a mix of deploys or batch sizes hides the cause.

The one-film v5 `next` and `submit` steps are retired ([ADR 0167](../adr/0167-retire-prediction-runner-v5.md)); their lines appear only in telemetry from before that deploy and are not comparable with the batch steps.

A missing line for a step means the Worker that served it predates the instrument or the step failed before answering. Telemetry is not a stored record: its retention is the platform's (the Observability tool reported a seven-day queryable range on 11 November 2026; confirm it before relying on older runs). The query shape above is the one used for the `epg_phase` events on that date; the first read of a `prediction_step_timing` line is the check that `source` carries the fields described here. No production write or run is needed to read it, and none is authorized by this page.

## Batch runner v2 operation (active rollout, AIR-451)

The Worker implements the batch-v2 protocol, and the four independent, owner-enabled Tasks A/B/C/D invoke it on hourly schedules as recorded in the [Scheduled Tasks reference](../reference/scheduled-tasks.md#predictions-v2-four-active-hourly-tasks-00153045). A v2 run that stops without `complete` ends by lease expiry like any run, and its unwritten films stay queued. A successful deployment or Task creation does not prove productive execution.

**No further Task operation is authorized by this runbook.** The original rollout authorization followed [ADR 0122](../adr/0122-prediction-batch-runner-v2-dual-protocol.md). Contract: [batch runner v2](../reference/api/prediction-runner.md#batch-runner-v2); installed Tasks: [Scheduled Tasks reference](../reference/scheduled-tasks.md#predictions-v2-four-active-hourly-tasks-00153045).

- **Task text and installation.** The installed production prompt snapshot, the dry-run procedure, and owner-gated installation/rollback steps are in [`hourly-predictions-batch-v2.md`](../reference/scheduled-task-prompts/hourly-predictions-batch-v2.md). For any future Task edit, read the **live installed registry prompt** first, compare exact string/length/SHA-256, and obtain an operation-specific owner GO. The legacy v5 Task cannot run: the Worker no longer exposes its tools ([ADR 0167](../adr/0167-retire-prediction-runner-v5.md)).
- **Dry run and A/B.** The experiment procedure, frozen-sample and result contract and comparison criteria are in [the dry-run A/B runbook](prediction-batch-v2-dry-run-ab.md). Short-run completion uses `manifest_spent` ([ADR 0136](../adr/0136-prediction-batch-manifest-size-and-spent-complete.md); [owner activation gate](../reference/scheduled-task-prompts/hourly-predictions-batch-v2.md#owner-activation-gate-short-run-completion)): a normal complete, not a partial `time_budget` pause.
- **Which Task is active.** Read the live Task registry: batch v2 is the only protocol, and several independent v2 Tasks may be enabled. Overlapping runs show as `already_running` answers of the later start, not as an error of the Worker; since AIR-548 each refused start attempt is a neutral «Запуск прогнозов пропущен» card in `/events` (filter «Пропущенные», or «Прогнозы»), one per refused request. The count of those cards is a count of start **attempts** (request ids), not proof of separate Scheduled Task ticks; compare with the Task registry when that matters.
- **Reading a v2 run.** Status, run diagnostics and the Events footer show it as a run with `protocol: batch_v2`. Read the `prediction_step_timing` lines with `step` of `batch_start` and `batch_submit` as described [above](#reading-the-step-timing-line-air-438): `manifestBytes`, `manifestFilms`, `items`, `deferredBudget`, `staleProfile`.
- **Slow or repeated `batch_start`.** Read the `batch_start` line: `wallMs` against `stages` (`preflight`, `queue_refresh`, `preflight_lease`, `persist`), `preflights`, `refreshRounds`, `scannedFilms`, `settledFilms`, and the `r2:` / `d1:` classes. A start does not list the historic `run-diagnostics/` prefix; that walk belongs to a successful `prediction_complete` (at most 10 keys, resume cursor). A start refused with `already_running` carries `leaseRefused: 1` and `liveLeaseAgeMs`; a second request about a minute later with `liveLeaseAgeMs` near the first start's age is a repeated start meeting its own predecessor, not a Worker retry. A start whose `wallMs` is well above 60 s with **no** second request shows that a hard 60 s platform timeout is not universal. Dated captures: [AIR-468](../evidence/prediction/air-468-batch-start-rca-2026-11-14.md), [AIR-471 offline counts](../evidence/prediction/air-471-batch-start-latency-2026-11-14.md), [AIR-486 production before the persist change](../evidence/prediction/air-486-repeat-start-after-471-2026-11-14.md). A start never needs a lease reset or a manual retry to be observed.
- **Task reports it created a run but lost the film list or the submit buffer.** Worker `batch_start` HTTP 200 with `manifestBytes` around 40 KiB is not proof the Scheduled Task retained parseable JSON. A truncated prefix is not a usable manifest. Compare `selectedIds` and `manifestHash` with a later slot: the same five films can complete on one Task and fail on another (AIR-510 night series 16–17 Nov 2026, Europe/Madrid). Do not repeat `prediction_batch_start` on that run. Do not reset the lease. While the lease holds, `prediction_batch_manifest` returns one part per call (`envelope`, then `profile`, `contract`, each `film`, `hold`, or one held `item`). After a passing preflight, `prediction_batch_hold` CAS-merges a transferable stage. The next `prediction_batch_start` drains it (commit fresh, drop stale) without the previous runId. Same-run `useHeld` still works while that lease is live. `external_termination` is HTTP 200 and releases the lease. The live Task prompt does not call hold until a separate owner GO.
- **`queue_changed`.** A real start that lost the queue snapshot between preflight and the lease answers `409` with `code: queue_changed` and `cause`: `stale` (the queue failed the advance gate again), `revision` (the queue generation moved) or `manifest` (the same revision but the selected film input hashed differently). Nothing is adopted and the start is not retried blindly. The AIR-510 Canary `queue_changed` (Task A, 16 Nov 2026, 15:14 Madrid) is this refusal: no `runId` was issued. Which of the three causes that slot hit is not in the Task report. **AIR-542:** a priority-only drift does not produce this refusal. A concurrent Discovery assignment during the one queue rebuild is accepted as a bounded lag. A queue revision or manifest change between the baseline preflight and the post-lease preflight continues on the preflight taken under the lease only when `priorityOnlyRebaseCause` proves a re-rank from the queue itself: same programme run, profile and contract; every queue field outside the priority set (`PRIORITY_QUEUE_FIELDS`: ranks, `pending`, hot/cold lists and counters, signature, rebuild statistics) equal, which includes `retries`, `lastBatch` and the compatibility versions, canonically and independent of key order; the same set of queued films (the union of `pending`, `hotPending` and `coldPending`, because a real rebuild reorders and resizes `pending`, which takes every hot film); and `work` equal per film wherever both queues hold an entry (an entry may appear only for a film that entered `pending` and disappear only for one that left it; adding or deleting the entry of a film that stays in `pending` is refused). A rank-only revision that leaves the delivered manifest byte-identical (films outside the first `manifestSize` re-ranked) continues on the same manifest. When the manifest differs, films in both keep plan, metadata and fingerprint, and a film the new selection dropped was not skipped as no longer admitted or current and is not staged elsewhere. The step timing line carries `priorityLagAccepted` and `priorityRebased`; the next start actualises hot/cold. `stale`, an unreadable shows signature (`shows_signature_unreadable`), a changed manifest under an unchanged revision, and any change outside the priority set (a retry record, a `work` or migration change, a film leaving the queue) still answer `queue_changed`, with no run, Event or diagnostics row written.
- **`manifest_too_large`.** When the stored queue was current the refusal wrote nothing. A refusal that follows the queue refresh and settlement invoked by the start (`queueRefreshed: true` in the answer) has those queue writes as its only effect; one found at revalidation has only the lease acquire and release. Either way no run record, diagnostics row, `latest-run` or Event was created, and it is not a stop of anything. No run record or Event was created. It repeats while the same films lead the queue. Do not shorten anything by hand: record the reported `bytes`, `limit` and `largestFilmBytes` and take the decision to the owner.
- **Start refusals that write nothing about a run.** `batch_runner_requires_d1` (the prediction authority marker is not `d1`), `queue_stale` (a dry run found the stored queue stale and never rebuilds it), `queue_changed` (the queue's generation, the selection or the manifest moved between the baseline preflight and the lease, including a write by another writer to films outside the delivered 100; the lease is released, and no run, diagnostics row or Event exists). Repeat the start; do not reset the lease.
- **A lost response or `502` on a submit.** Replay the same body with the same `runId` and `epoch`: committed films come back `replayed`. A heartbeat in between does not change the `epoch`.
- **`submit_in_flight`.** An earlier call of the same run still holds the submit gate (a replay that overlapped it, or an overlapping batch). Wait `retryAfterMs` and replay the same body; do not send a different batch meanwhile. The gate expires by itself if the handler died.
- **`submit_in_flight` that does not clear.** If the gate does not clear within the caller's bounded retries, stop heartbeating and leave the run for lease expiry; do not reset the lease. A superseded handler commits nothing (`gate_lost`).
- **A stuck v2 run.** Wait for lease expiry (15 minutes after the last renewal). Do not reset the lease. Its unsubmitted films are still in the queue.
- **Idle starts.** A `batch_start` that answers `noop`, or `blocked` with reason `quota` or `retry_backoff`, took no lease and created no run, stop Event or `latest-run`; only the start-attempt row and the queue's `lastBatch` record it. `already_running` is the holder's run: the attempt wrote nothing to the queue, run or lease.
- **Submit results.** `needs_regeneration` is not a failure: the taste profile moved under the run and the change reaches that film; the answer carries the new profile and the film ids to regenerate, and `action: complete` (a global change) means call `prediction_complete` with reason `profile_changed`. `validation_failure` is the existing retryable item failure (sanitized stage and issue codes, backoff, never resubmitted in the run). `deferred_budget` films are submitted again as they were. `epoch_mismatch` is the caller's error and stops nothing. A group over 56 KiB is refused by the MCP tool with nothing submitted: split it.
- **`wrong_protocol`.** The run is a stored legacy v5 run (no `protocol`, or `v5`): the v2 tools refuse it. Stop; it ends by lease expiry or is settled by the next batch start. Do not reset the lease.
- **Rollback.** There is no protocol rollback by registry change: v5 is retired ([ADR 0167](../adr/0167-retire-prediction-runner-v5.md)). Bringing v5 back needs a code rollback to a Worker that still contains it plus separate owner GOs for every Task action. Saved predictions, D1 and R2 are not reverted by either.
- **Dry run.** Writes nothing to prediction state, but it spends model quota and reads production: run it only with the owner's approval and record the result in [evidence](../evidence/prediction/README.md).

## One film without a current prediction

| Verdict | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| `prediction_current`, `index_ahead_of_queue` | Nothing. The next queue refresh drops a film whose prediction is already current |
| `queued_ready`, `in_flight`, `run_active`, `waiting_behind_hot` | Wait for the ordinary schedule. The time shown for the next run is the nominal trigger, an estimate |
| `retry_backoff` | Wait for the shown retry time. Do not reset `retryAt` or restart the runner |
| `queue_refresh_pending` | Wait for the next refresh (any prediction run, EPG commit or profile update). A manual `POST /api/enrichment/queue/reconcile` is a production write that rebuilds the whole queue; it needs separate owner approval and is not a per-film fix |
| `ready_no_runs` | Check the prediction Scheduled Task as in the section above: enabled, last execution, result. Do not start a run from the page |
| `repeated_failures` | Follow the reason. Model output (`validation_error`, `parse_error`): read `prediction_film_diagnostics` for `retry.validation` and fix the caller's output against the submit contract (step 5 above). Infrastructure (storage, quota, time budget, model availability, lease): check that cause in run diagnostics, Cloudflare logs and the Task history; the model output is not at fault. Unrecognised kind: investigate the classification and the run history first. In every case do not reset `retryAt` or restart the runner |
| `index_inconsistent` | The view index and the film objects disagree: investigate the index, not the queue |
| `not_in_catalog` | The film is in neither the published programme nor the catalog: an identity question for the resolver, not a prediction one |
| `snapshot_partial`, `profile_missing`, `queue_unavailable`, `film_unreadable`, `queue_state_unknown` | Press «Проверить заново» later. Persistent disagreement after the next refresh is a finding for the owner, not a reason to write |

Before any confirmation, verify that the prediction marker is `d1` (its diagnostic `writes` is normally `held`) and obtain an explicit owner GO for the exact cohort. Contract: [scoped prediction recovery intent](../reference/api/admin-actions.md#scoped-prediction-recovery-intent).

1. Read `GET /api/admin/prediction-recovery-intent/preview?films=<≤50 ids>&mode=prediction_only` (or `fingerprint_and_prediction`). It writes nothing. Check `schema.ready`, `authority`, `runner`, every film's `outcome` and `reason`, and `budget`. `unchanged` films need no intent: the ordinary rule already queues them. `blocked` and `conflict` films are not written.
2. Confirm with the exact `scopeHash` and a fresh idempotency key (16–80 characters of `A-Za-z0-9_-`). A `stale_scope` answer means something the preview rested on moved: read the preview again; never retry with an old hash. A lost answer is safe to resend with the same key and hash: it returns `replayed` and the stored receipt.
3. Do nothing else: no queue reconcile, no lease change, no manual run. The next ordinary start rebuilds the queue (`recovery_changed`) and the ordinary Scheduled Task runs score the films. `GET …/cohort?id=<cohortId>` shows each film `pending` until a new version is committed, then `satisfied`.
4. To stop pending films, `POST …/cancel {cohortId}`. Already committed predictions stay; the old versions remain in `prediction_lineage` and R2.

A code rollback leaves the rows in D1 and an older Worker ignores them; the intents then stop applying until the code returns. Expired intents stop applying on their own.

## Hot/cold priority after Discovery assignments in the same generation (AIR-529)

Discovery can add shows to the published EPG generation without a new `programmeRunId`. The queue stores `showsSignature`; the next `prediction_batch_start` rebuilds hot/cold when the live signature differs. Diagnose a suspected stale ranking read-only: compare `prediction_queue_overview` `hotRemaining` with the completed programme's films in the window, and treat a queue with no `showsSignature` as due once. Do not run a manual reconcile POST for this; the natural next start performs the rebuild.

## Hot queue parity check

Use `prediction_hot_parity` ([contract](../reference/api/prediction-diagnostics.md#hot-queue-parity)) when the hot lane of the queue may not reflect what is on air, for example after Discovery assignments in a generation, a window edge or a generation rollover. It is read-only and owner-only, and it reads the whole catalog, so call it occasionally, never in a loop and never to decide a start.

1. Read `verdict` first. `match`: nothing to do. `degraded` with `window_transition_deferred`: the accepted 15-minute rate cap; the next natural `prediction_batch_start` after the cap rebuilds. `degraded` with `generation_rollover_pending`: the queue waits for its own refresh on the new generation. `mismatch`: read `reasons` and `diff.causes`; `shows_signature_changed` and `window_transition` are cured by the next natural start, `no_longer_outstanding` and `absent_from_queue` that persist across two natural starts are a finding to record. `unknown`: say so; it proves nothing, so repeat later or read the named source.
2. A repeated `unknown` of the same reason (for example `shows_changed_during_read` while Discovery assigns continuously) is not a failure of the queue; retry between chunks.
3. Record the verdict, `generation`, `cutoff`, `evaluated.films` and `elapsedMs` in the card or evidence. Do not report `expected.hotOutstanding` as a verified count when the verdict is `unknown` or `degraded`.
4. Do not respond with a manual `queue/reconcile` POST, a lease reset, a retry reset, a Scheduled Task edit or a model call. The natural start is the only repair path; a mismatch that outlives it is a code finding.
