## Context

### What exists (main `40c8a3a`, 25 Nov 2026)

- **The only canonical writer for a Discovery Film is minimal by design.** Since the projector was retired ([ADR 0155](0155-retire-enrichment-projector-cron.md)), a Discovery admission reaches canonical state only through the `film_mutation` unit of [ADR 0134](0134-discovery-admission-producer-wiring.md) decision 5 (`src/canonical-discovery-admission-producer.js`, origin `discovery`, operation `film_mutation`): the Film, the trusted `tmdb` claim and a title written only when blank. It writes no `film_metadata`, credits, Persons, aliases, localization or `canonical_identity`.
- **Every richer writer is bounded to another cohort.** EPG `film_facts` and credits target Watchlist Films only (`src/canonical-producer-workflow.js`); `metadata_sync` is Watchlist only; the dictionary covers its entries; the Catalog Enrichment Producer ("C1", ADR 0211) is scoped to `film_rated_admission.verified_at >= cutoff_at`, with the widenings C3a (the 714 existing admitted Films, AIR-614) and C3b (Watchlist members, AIR-615) as separate owner decisions. C1's code and tables are **not yet in `main`** (no `catalog_enrichment` object in `src/` or `migrations/` at `40c8a3a`).
- **C1 already fetches what Discovery Films lack.** Its TMDB request is `movie/<id>?append_to_response=credits,translations` (ADR 0211 decision 5), one response that carries year, runtime, genres, poster, overview, credits with stable person ids and translations. Each applicable source writes its own `film_localization_source` row; credits are bounded to at most 20 directors and 10 actors; every Film/part/source has one state row and a stable `operation_key` (ADR 0211 decision 3).
- **The Discovery payload is Discovery's own serving record.** `discovery_admission.payload_json` mixes TMDB values with EPG fallbacks (`candidate.year`, `candidate.description`, `src/discovery.js:352`), a poster that can be kept or filled by another path (`POSTER_UPDATE_SQL`), and aliases and availability that change on every EPG touch. It has no observation time of a TMDB response. The Film page and Finds read it directly. It is **not** an input of this design.
- **A Discovery admission has no durable first-admission time today.** `generation` is moved by every upsert, `discoveredAt` lives in the mutable payload, and a canonical mutation receipt is not the admission (the mutation is a no-op, with no receipt, when the Film already has its claim and title).
- **`discovery_admission` is unique per movie id** (`discovery_admission_identity (media_kind, tmdb_id)`), keyed by the Discovery key `tmdb_<n>`, not `film.id`.
- **No Discovery Film has a Russian title in canonical state.**

### The problem

Canonical Film/Person D1 is the authority (ADR 0216), but for Discovery Films it holds an id, a claim and a title, and for the minimal group nothing else. Readers that do not go through Discovery lose year, runtime, synopsis, poster, genres, credits and every Russian title, and nobody owns refresh. C1 is the accepted owner of exactly this enrichment, but its scope does not reach Discovery Films.

### Settled decisions this record keeps

- `film_mutation` stays minimal (ADR 0134 decision 5). Discovery admission is unchanged.
- After enrichment the canonical row is the authority; the Discovery payload stays Discovery's serving record and is never copied into canonical state.
- ADR 0218 keeps a `film_dimension` row only for owner or reviewed decisions, the ADR 0214 metadata lock, `titleSource` and the effective-localization projection kept for read cost; processing dimensions are neither written nor read for decisions here.
- ADR 0211 is the one planner, fetcher and state machine: anchors and agreement, the 72 h transport-only fallback, credits namespace and expected set, write intent, epochs, lease, control, pause, shared upstream cooldown, fair bounded scheduling and visibility. ADR 0214 is the one rank and lock rule. This record adds a scope to them; it adds no producer and no second queue.
- Identity is never inferred from a title or a year. No Letterboxd or Wikidata anchor is invented.
- Mass enrichment waits for AIR-607 and AIR-636 natural acceptance; this gates activation, not this design.

## Decision

### 1. One producer: C1 gets a Discovery scope

The existing Catalog Enrichment Producer (ADR 0211; implementation AIR-605) is the only planner, fetcher and state machine for Discovery Films. This record adds **no** observation table, publication step, sequence, cursor, intake unit, receipt origin, intent table, credit-attribution table or intake-to-C1 handoff, and it adds no second queue: a Film has **one** state row per part and source and the **existing** stable receipt keys of ADR 0211 decision 3, whatever scope admitted it: `enrich:<film>:core`, `enrich:<film>:localization:<source>` (one per source), `enrich:<film>:credits:<source>` and `enrich:person:<person id>`. None of them is collapsed or renamed here.

**A scope is a membership predicate, not a task.** C1's eligible set is the union of its activated scopes: the initial rated scope (AIR-607), the widenings C3a (AIR-614) and C3b (AIR-615), and the Discovery scope **C3c** of this record. A Film that belongs to several scopes is planned once. Scope activations are independent owner operations in any order; none widens another implicitly, and no activation is a prerequisite of another except as decision 8 states.

### 2. The Discovery scope C3c: a membership predicate over a durable Discovery admission fact

**Why a receipt is not the fact.** A canonical mutation receipt is **not** an admission timestamp: `planAdmissionFilm` and `applyRouted` (`src/canonical-discovery-admission-producer.js`) return a no-op with **no receipt** when the Film already holds its trusted `tmdb` claim and a title, so a valid new Discovery admission onto an existing Film can have none; `admitDiscoveryFilms` defers work and its sweep admits **earlier** current admissions later, so an old admission's first receipt can land after any cutoff; and a merge or identity correction can separate a receipt's original target from the current claim owner. The cutoff is therefore taken from a fact of the **admission state itself**.

**The fact.** Two additive nullable columns on the Discovery authority's own table: `discovery_admission.first_current_at` (ISO UTC), the time the row **first became `state='current'`**, never changed afterwards; and `discovery_admission.fact_tracked`, set to 1 only for a row **inserted after the fact exists and outside a bulk load**. Both are written only by database triggers, so every writer, including an old Worker version, gets them, and no code path can skip or move them:
- `AFTER INSERT`, **when `discovery_transfer.state <> 'loading'`**: sets `fact_tracked = 1` and, when `NEW.state = 'current'`, stamps `first_current_at`;
- `AFTER UPDATE OF state`, **when `NEW.fact_tracked = 1`, `OLD.first_current_at IS NULL`, `OLD.state = 'pending'`, `NEW.state = 'current'` and not `loading`**: stamps it;
- nothing else writes either column; `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` names neither.

**Why `fact_tracked` is needed.** The state `pending` is not evidence of a new row. The transfer path sets `discovery_transfer.state='loading'`, moves **every** `current` row to `pending`, upserts the loaded catalog back to `current` and moves the rest to `superseded` at commit (`src/discovery-authority.js`), so a historical row goes `pending` → `current` in an ordinary run. A rule on `pending` → `current` alone would stamp history as new. `fact_tracked` separates a row that was born tracked from one that was pending at the schema apply, imported, restored or loaded in bulk: those are **untracked**, their `first_current_at` stays `NULL`, and no later transition stamps them.

**Writer paths (read statically on `main`; C3c-F re-verifies them by test).** `INSERT INTO discovery_admission` occurs in the upsert (`ADMISSION_INSERT`, rows inserted `current`) and in the transfer's loading upsert; the other statements only `UPDATE` (`current` → `superseded` for an admission and for one Film, `current` → `pending` at the start of a transfer, `pending` → `superseded` at its commit, the poster update). **No `DELETE` and no `REPLACE`** of an admission row exists in `src/`, so a row is never re-created as new; a test fixture script (`scripts/footer-memory-profile.mjs`) inserts rows outside production. C3c-F's migration test enumerates every statement that writes `discovery_admission` and fails if a new `DELETE`, `REPLACE` or insert path appears.

**Semantics (nothing invented, nothing promoted silently).**

| Case | Result |
| --- | --- |
| rows that exist when the columns are added, **including rows already `pending` at the apply** (the migration backfills nothing and sets `fact_tracked` for none) | untracked: `NULL` = **unknown history**, permanently; no later transition (`pending` → `current`, `superseded` → `current`, a transfer re-confirmation) stamps them |
| a new admission after the column exists | stamped at its insert as `current` (or at its `pending` → `current` promotion) |
| a re-admission or a revalidation (the upsert) | the stamp does not move |
| `superseded` → `current` of a stamped row | the stamp does not move (it was first current earlier) |
| a canonical mutation that is a no-op (the Film exists with its claim and title) | irrelevant: the fact is on the admission row, so the new admission **stays eligible** |
| a deferred admission, and an old admission swept after activation | the stamp is its own first-current time, so an old one is history or unknown, never stream |
| a lost response | the admission batch is atomic: either the row (with its stamp) exists, or it does not and the retry creates it, so the stamp is the first **durable** creation |
| a transfer, an import or a D1 restore | rows inserted under `loading` are untracked (unknown); a historical row that a transfer moves `current` → `pending` → `current` keeps `NULL` (untracked) and a tracked row keeps its stamp; a restore of the table restores both columns consistently; a row restored or imported as `pending` and later promoted stays unknown; nothing invents a timestamp. Admissions that enter only through a bulk load are therefore unknown, by design |
| a merge, a redirect or an identity correction | membership resolves through the **current** trusted `tmdb` claim `n` to the admission row `(movie, n)`, not through any receipt target; redirect and retirement are the ADR 0211 eligibility and anchor rules |

**The membership predicate.** A Film is in C3c when **all** hold, read from authoritative tables at plan and asserted by C1's own guards in each batch:
1. The Film exists and is not a redirect source or retired (the ADR 0211 eligibility).
2. It holds exactly one trusted, non-conflicting `tmdb` movie claim `<n>` with no other trusted owner and no `tmdb_retirement_decision` (the ADR 0211 `tmdb` anchor).
3. `discovery_admission` holds the row for `(media_kind='movie', tmdb_id=<n>)` (unique by index) with `state='current'`, and its stored `first_current_at` is **not NULL and at or after the scope's `cutoff_at`**. The batch asserts that exact stored value by equality with the one read at plan, together with the Discovery authority route and generation and `discovery_transfer.state <> 'loading'`, so a moved or reloaded admission fails the batch and the unit replans.

**Classification, with nothing promoted silently.** Every Discovery admission is exactly one of: `new` (stamped at or after the cutoff: in scope); `before_cutoff` (stamped earlier: history, provable); `unknown_history` (`NULL`: history, not provable); `superseded`; or `unresolved_identity` (no unique trusted `tmdb` claim yet). All are counted per scope. An unknown admission is never inferred from `discoveredAt`, the payload, `generation` or a receipt, and only a separate approved historical scope (decision 8) can enrich history.

**The earliest possible cutoff.** The stream can begin no earlier than the apply of the column, because earlier admissions are unknown by construction. This follows from the exactness that 3A asks for and is not a reinterpretation of stream-first; it is why the fact (card C3c-F) should be applied **early**, independently of C1, so that new admissions are stamped from then on.

**Activation.** C3c is activated by its own owner operation (C3c-A), which sets the Discovery scope's `cutoff_at` **no later than the activation** and **no earlier than the apply of the column**, as C2 does for rated Films (ADR 0207 decision 7). New admissions are eligible from day one and enriched at the following ticks, not instantly.

**Revalidation, retries and source-set changes.** Discovery's revalidation never changes membership (the stamp is fixed). Retries, backoff, the 6-failure `needs_attention` cap, anchor corrections, retirements, a lid claim that appears later (which makes Letterboxd applicable and reopens only its gaps), redirects and `identity_disagreement` are exactly ADR 0211 decisions 3 and 3b.

**Overlap.** A Film also in the rated or Watchlist scope is one Film with one state set; C1 plans it once, with one set of existing keys, and no handoff exists because there is one writer. C3c reuses the AIR-614 and AIR-615 widening plans and neither duplicates nor widens them.

**Candidate discovery: a bounded cyclic keyset traversal, not a second queue.** Only the range `first_current_at >= cutoff` is ever traversed, so `before_cutoff` and `unknown_history` rows (irreversible classifications) are outside it and never examined. Within it:
- **Deterministic order.** The key is `(first_current_at, tmdb_id)`, the `tmdb_id` breaking ties, with an index `discovery_admission (state, first_current_at, tmdb_id)`; every step reads one keyset page above a cursor.
- **A bound on examined rows, not on matches.** A step examines **at most `E` rows** whatever they are (a read page, an engineering default set by the harness) and advances the cursor over the rows it **examined**, not only over the resolved ones. `LIMIT` on the resolved output and `EXPLAIN` alone do not bound scanned rows; the examined-row budget does.
- **No blocking by the earliest unresolved row.** The cursor does not stop at an unresolved admission (no trusted `tmdb` claim yet) or a superseded one; it passes it, so nothing behind it starves the rows after it.
- **Two modes of the same cursor.** In **tail mode** a step examines only rows above the cursor, which is where new admissions arrive; at the end of the range the cursor **parks**, and a probe for new work costs one index lookup. In **cycle mode** the cursor wraps to the start of the range, **at most once per maintenance interval** (decision 6), and traverses it again in pages of `E`, so every reversible classification is **revisited**: an unresolved admission whose claim has since appeared, a superseded admission that is current again, and a current-claim owner change behind the cursor. Only an irreversible classification is skipped for good.
- **What a row yields.** A resolved admission with no C1 state row has its state rows materialized; one that already has state rows is skipped (its anchors are rechecked by the maintenance of decision 6); an unresolved or superseded admission is left as it is and revisited next cycle. The cursor, the cycle's start and its completion time live in the scope row (decision 7), written under the control generation and revision CAS, so a stale run cannot move it.
- **Reported.** Examined, materialized, unresolved and superseded counts per step, the oldest unresolved age and the full-cycle duration are reported; C3c-D proves on the real migrated schema, by `EXPLAIN` and by a fixture with an unresolved row at the head of the range, that scanned rows are bounded and that a late-resolved hole is picked up on the next cycle.

### 3. Sources, anchors and agreement are unchanged

For a C3c Film C1 derives the applicable sources exactly as ADR 0211 decision 2 does: the `tmdb` anchor makes `tmdb` and `wikidata` applicable (Wikidata via P4947); `letterboxd` is applicable **only** when a trusted lid claim already exists, and is never invented or fetched without one. Agreement, the 72 h transport-only fallback, precedence and `identity_disagreement` are unchanged; credits keep the one-namespace rule, the expected set (at most 20 directors and 10 actors) and `superseded_credits`; the ADR 0214 rank, owner pins, the metadata lock, hold mode and the no-clear rule apply to the single writer as accepted. C1 obtains its **own** fresh response with a real `observed_at`; no mixed serving value is copied.

This record does not add a source or broaden a fetch. Whether Wikidata is worth its request for this scope is a measurement question of the AIR-605 harness; a change of applicability would be a scoped amendment of ADR 0211, not assumed here.

### 4. 2A: the typed Russian title comes from C1's own TMDB response

C1's `localization` part already writes the `tmdb` source row from the same TMDB response that carries `credits,translations`. 2A is applied there, with **no separate per-film translation fetch**:
- the selection reads the `translations.translations` entries with `iso_639_1 = 'ru'`;
- exactly one distinct non-blank `data.title` is the value; several entries are decided by `iso_3166_1 = 'RU'`; if they still differ, the selection is **ambiguous** and nothing is written (a recorded gap);
- no `ru` entry is a legitimate **absence** (`complete_with_gaps`, as ADR 0211 already records);
- a missing or malformed `translations` section of an otherwise complete response is **unreadable**: it is the ADR 0211 *definite* failure (a missing required section), consumes one of the six and is retried with the accepted backoff, ending in `needs_attention`; it is neither an absence nor a write of any title;
- the response `title`, `aliases`, `movieTitles` and any alias are **never** read as a Russian title.

**Keys and counts.** The unit is the existing per-source key `enrich:<film>:localization:tmdb`; the `letterboxd` and `wikidata` sources keep their own keys (`enrich:<film>:localization:letterboxd`, `:wikidata`), so distinct sources are never collapsed. **Ambiguous** is a recorded terminal gap (`translation_ambiguous`), not retried by itself and reopened only by an accepted ADR 0214 trigger; **absent** and **unreadable** are counted apart. The per-scope counts of ADR 0211 decision 5 report each state accurately (complete, `complete_with_gaps`, `translation_ambiguous`, unreadable-failing, `needs_attention`), and no source request is made solely for the translation.

The row is written through the AIR-662 projection-aware operation (the effective projection recomputed in the same batch, the Film revision CAS and the owner-review authority and content token, ADR 0218 decision 3), and reviewed and owner values keep their precedence through the existing resolver. ADR 0211 states the source row but not this selection rule; this record states it (decision 9) and adds no source.

**Adaptation from the original 2A.** The original 2A carried the translation from **Discovery's own detail response** through a Discovery observation. Under 1B that transport does not exist: C1 fetches the response itself, which already asks for translations. Reusing Discovery's response would need the observation subsystem, which is 1C and was not selected; it is not reintroduced. The consequence is one TMDB request per Film that Discovery has already made once, which the owner accepted by choosing 1B.

### 5. 4A: event-driven refresh through ADR 0214, with no extension assumed

Refresh for C3c Films is exactly ADR 0214 decision 10: fallback recovery, a source-set change, an identity correction, unknown or superseded fields met in a fetch that is due anyway, and an owner `refresh`; no periodic sweep. Anchor changes, lock release and retry, and the no-op rule remain as accepted, with `observed_at` set by C1's own response.

**A Discovery verification is not a trigger.** A Discovery revalidation or re-verification does not reopen anything in C1: nothing Discovery holds is a fresh verified response for C1, and copying its mixed serving values is excluded. So repeated unchanged verification and every EPG touch cause **no** redundant fetch, and no event intake exists to deduplicate. Consequently a complete C3c Film is refreshed only by the accepted triggers and by the owner, as a rated Film is; a periodic policy is the separate ADR 0214 PVT question.

Whether a Discovery revalidation should become an **additional** bounded, coalesced trigger is a new material question, not part of the recorded selections. It is presented separately as Choice 5 below and, unless chosen, is **not** part of this design.

### 6. The unified producer is a work-conserving, resource-bounded queue drainer from its first implementation

**The correction.** ADR 0211 decision 5 starts the producer at a 25 s tick deadline with a 5 s write reserve, at most 2 Films per hourly invocation, a 12-unit write budget, a derivative ceiling of 8 requests per tick, a 10 s rule for a second Film, and a rule that the bound is raised only by an owner decision after measurement. Those are **starting design values** chosen before any code existed ([the executor's comment of 24 Nov](https://github.com/vladimino/iptv-watchlist/pull/975#issuecomment-7132176638)); they are neither a provider limit nor a separate owner selection (the owner's 0211-5 = C concerned the shared upstream coordination, which is unchanged). By the owner's direction of 25 Nov 2026 they are **removed as normative limits**, and the unified Catalog Enrichment Producer is, **from its first implementation (AIR-605) and across every activated scope**, a **work-conserving, resource-bounded queue drainer**:
- it processes eligible due work **while work is runnable and real, safe resource headroom exists**;
- it persists progress and **continues automatically** with another bounded execution step when work remains after a resource stop;
- an empty queue, or work all deferred until a future retry or cooldown, **quiesces without polling spin**;
- it has **no fixed total Film count** per invocation or per hour, **no mandatory wall-clock deadline**, **no fixed total write-unit or derived request ceiling**, **no special rule for a second Film**, and **no hourly-only continuation**. The numbers are not replaced by larger numbers: the values 2, 25 s and 12 are not raised to 20, 5 minutes or another constant.

**Safety budgets stay; throughput quotas go.** Kept, because each is independently justified: the per-Film caps (at most 4 upstream requests per Film, at most 30 missing Persons, the credits expected set), the individual atomic units and their guards, the retry caps (six definite failures), the per-request timeouts, the lease, the shared cooldown, the epochs and locks, and the instance resource guard below. Removed: any cap on how many Films a run or an hour may complete.

**The one default execution model: the Discovery pattern, reused.** The repository already runs five Workflows and has solved bounded continuation for Discovery. The default is that pattern, not a new subsystem ([ADR 0083](0083-inert-discovery-workflow.md), [ADR 0093](0093-discovery-workflow-cron-reconcile.md), [ADR 0094](0094-discovery-workflow-invocation-budget.md), [ADR 0098](0098-discovery-workflow-step-failure-recovery.md), [ADR 0174](0174-discovery-only-fast-recovery-cron.md)):

| Part | Design | Reused from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executor | one Cloudflare Workflow class for the producer; one **instance** is one run; a **step is one Film** (durable, replay-safe, per-Film retry); one owner at a time through the existing `projection_run` lease and a deterministic series and instance id with a compare-and-swap on the run record | ADR 0083, 0093 |
| Resource guard | the instance counts the D1 statements it issues across replays and **hands over** to a successor instance (a new invocation) at the budget below, creating it before it finishes | ADR 0094 |
| Step failure | a transient step failure is recovered inside the instance with the bounded backoff tiers and `step.sleepUntil`; an errored instance is recovered by `ensure` with the same backoff and quarantine | ADR 0098, 0174 |
| Wakeup and recovery | **`ensure`**, below | ADR 0174 |
| Cooldown, receipts, units, guards, epochs | unchanged | ADR 0211 decisions 3 and 5 (shared cooldown), ADR 0207 decision 3 |

**Stop reasons and continuation.** A run ends only for one of these, each recorded in its terminal outbox entry:

| Stop reason | What follows |
| --- | --- |
| no runnable work | the run finishes and **quiesces**; nothing polls |
| instance statement budget reached with work remaining | **handover**: the successor is created first and the run finishes, so the series continues at once, not at the next hour |
| every remaining item is deferred (an active cooldown on each source it needs, or a future `retry_at`) | the run finishes and quiesces; `ensure` starts a run when something is due |
| the control is no longer `active` or its generation moved (pause) | the terminal `stopped` entry is written and the run finishes; resume calls `ensure` at once |
| the EPG priority gate is closed (the pure predicate above) | the run does not start a Film and finishes; `ensure` creates no instance while it is closed and resumes when it opens |
| a transient step failure | recovery inside the instance, else by `ensure`, as in ADR 0098 and 0174 |
| the lease was lost | the run writes nothing more and finishes |

**`ensure`: the wakeup, recovery and idle-maintenance contract.** One cheap, idempotent function derives everything from durable state, so no wakeup can be lost. It reads the control (one read: inactive or paused returns), then the run record (one read) and, only when the record says an instance is alive, makes one platform status call; a healthy live owner returns. It also evaluates the **EPG gate by the pure predicate** of `src/domain/epg-priority-pause.js` (ADR 0135 with the ADR 0175 completion qualification), from its due-day and run-completion inputs, **not** by reading a lazily armed pause row: a closed gate prevents creating an instance at all. With no live owner and the gate open it creates the instance, with the deterministic id, if and only if one of these is true; otherwise it returns:

| # | Runnable when | Indexed read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | **due fetch work:** a Film/part/source state row in an **active scope** is due or partly progressed, with `retry_at <= now`, its source **not under cooldown**, and it is not blocked: not `needs_attention`, not `identity_disagreement`, not terminal (`complete`, `complete_with_gaps`, `not_applicable`) and, for `core`, not owner or reviewed locked | one `EXISTS` per source that is not under cooldown |
| R2 | **receipt settlement:** an unresolved receipt of this producer's origin has `next_attempt_at <= now` | one `EXISTS` |
| R3 | **anchor maintenance is due** (ADR 0211 decision 3b, below): the oldest `anchor_checked_at` among in-scope Films, **whatever their status, terminal rows included**, is older than the maintenance interval | one `MIN` over an index |
| R4 | **candidate work:** for an active scope, a row above its parked cursor (tail mode, decision 2) or a cycle that is due (`cycle_completed_at` older than the maintenance interval) | one lookup per active scope |
| R5 | **eligibility re-evaluation:** the control `generation` differs from the run record's `evaluated_generation` (a scope, pause or resume operation) | two integers |

**What is deferred, and creates no instance.** The control is not `active`; the EPG gate is closed; a due part whose **every** needed source is under cooldown, or whose `retry_at` is in the future; a `needs_attention` or `identity_disagreement` row (an owner `retry` makes it R1); a terminal row with no anchor or maintenance due; a locked metadata part; and an **unresolved cohort** (no trusted claim yet), which is revisited only in the cycle mode of R4, not by an instance every five minutes. After a pass completes, none of R1 to R5 holds until something actually changes or the interval elapses, so a closed gate or an unresolved cohort does **not** create an empty instance every five minutes. The idle check is about eight indexed reads, and its measured cost is reported by the cron's outcome and by the harness.

**Anchor maintenance, preserved from ADR 0211 decision 3b and independent of due fetches.** A maintenance pass is **D1-only** and makes **no upstream request**: it examines in-scope Films by oldest `anchor_checked_at` (every status, terminal included) in bounded read pages, recomputes each anchor digest by the existing bounded read of `film_external_id` and `tmdb_retirement_decision` (the digest, its `holds` rule and the reopening on a changed anchor are exactly 3b's), applies 3b's reopening (a source set that grew reopens that source's gaps; one that shrank makes its rows `not_applicable`; an identity correction invalidates under its epoch), which in turn makes R1 runnable, and stamps `anchor_checked_at`. 3b's per-tick page of 20 becomes this traversal's read page, not a per-run quota: the pass continues across pages until every in-scope Film is within the interval or the instance guard hands over. Before a Film is planned its digest is still recomputed, as accepted. The default maintenance interval is **24 hours**: the whole catalog is rechecked at least daily, which costs D1 reads proportional to the in-scope Films and no fetch. **A changed anchor is an accepted trigger of ADR 0211 and 0214, not Choice 5B**: from an entirely complete or blocked catalog, a newly trusted claim or a corrected one is found by R3 within the interval, reopens exactly the right part, and is committed once.

**Delivery of the cron, declarative and durable.** `wrangler.jsonc` owns the cron definitions across deployments. The expression (every five minutes, a dedicated one, dispatching `ensure` and nothing else; no EPG, Monitor or Discovery work) is delivered by the **activation change of AIR-607 as a reviewed, versioned configuration change under its owner GO**, so a later deploy preserves it and no manual edit can be removed or bypassed by one. AIR-605's deploy carries only the **dormant dispatch** (the `scheduled` branch for that expression and the Workflow binding), which does nothing while the control is inactive because the control is read first. Readback is the deployed trigger list together with the control; rollback is reverting that configuration change and, at once, pausing (a paused control returns `ensure` at its first read even while the cron remains). Direct calls (activation, resume, scope operations) cover the cases that should not wait for the cron. The hourly cron is not involved.

**Fences, overlap and handover.** Every write batch asserts the lease (`lease_id`, `lease_until`), the control generation and the unit's epochs, as ADR 0211 requires. The lease is renewed before each Film and expires after a TTL, so a crashed run is recovered by `ensure` after the TTL and its stale batches cannot write. A deterministic series and instance id with a compare-and-swap on the run record admit exactly one owner. Continuation is idempotent because progress is canonical state plus the Film/part/source state rows: a lost update causes at most one redundant fetch, never a wrong write.

**Handover is atomic and recoverable, and creating a successor grants no writes.** The predecessor (1) records the successor intent (series + 1, the deterministic id) on the run record by compare-and-swap; (2) creates the instance (idempotent by id: a repeated create returns the existing one); (3) **releases its lease** by compare-and-swap on its `lease_id`, setting `lease_until` to now, and stops writing; (4) the successor writes only after it acquires a **new `lease_id`** by compare-and-swap on a free or expired lease. Failure cases are specified and tested: *create succeeded but the response was lost*: the intent exists and `ensure` finds the instance by its id, so no duplicate is created; *a crash between create and release*: the successor holds no lease, waits (`step.sleepUntil` the lease expiry, bounded) and acquires it after the TTL, while the predecessor's later batches fail; *an expired lease during a step sleep*: on wake a **fresh owner and lease check** runs, a lost lease stops the run, a free one is reacquired with a new `lease_id` and the control generation and epochs are re-validated before any write. A replayed step re-checks owner and lease before its first write. **A step is one Film, not permission for unbounded retries:** platform retries are capped (default three, with the ADR 0098 tiers), per-unit progress is resumable from the state rows, and the counters below include failing and replayed calls.

**Defaults, why, and how they are adjusted** (engineering-owned tuning inside the accepted envelope; not owner choices):

| Parameter | Default | Why | Adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| instance D1 statement budget | **6,000**, **counting failing and replayed calls** | ADR 0094, Accepted, an **initial technical guard and not a provider limit** (that ADR says the exact limit is not established): about half of the 12,287 and 12,192 statements at which two instances failed; counted by a replay-safe proxy | raise only after the harness shows the platform counts differently; Cloudflare documents a configurable `limits.subrequests` (default 10,000 on Workers Paid) |

**The 72 h fallback is unchanged by local stops.** ADR 0211's rule that a scheduling shortage cannot masquerade as 72 h of upstream unavailability is generalized from the tick's deadline and unit budget to **every local stop**: a handover, an EPG yield, a pause, a lost lease or a resource stop leaves `pending_since` unchanged, consumes no failure, sets no cooldown and writes no transport outcome. The 72 h window counts only continuous upstream transport failure, exactly as accepted.

**Resource facts, as read on 25 Nov 2026** (Cloudflare documentation, Workers Paid; the repository's own `wrangler.jsonc` and handler): CPU time per invocation defaults to 30 s and can be set up to 5 minutes (the repository sets `cpu_ms` to 120,000); subrequests per invocation or Workflow instance default to 10,000 and are configurable up to 10 million, and a D1 call is a subrequest; a Workflow step has unlimited wall time subject to the CPU limit, `step.sleep` is capped at 365 days, a Workflow instance allows 10,000 steps by default (configurable to 25,000) and waiting instances do not count toward concurrency; a Cron Trigger invocation is limited to 15 minutes of wall time. The Workers Paid baseline of `AGENTS.md` applies: these are measurements, not gates. Workflow step billing (500,000 steps a month included, then a per-step rate, from 10 Aug 2026) is negligible at one step per Film and is a measurement, not a decision. The repository already uses Workflows, so no additional service or plan is assumed. TMDB's published protective limit (about 40 requests per second, `429` handling required, per the owner handoff and not fetched here) is respected by the one-request-per-scope rule and the shared cooldown with `Retry-After`, and is not read as a throughput allowance; Wikidata's limits are not assessed here.

**Acceptance is ordinary implementation acceptance of AIR-605, with its already planned offline cost harness.** Representative mixed-scope fixtures on the real migrated schema (counts and shapes, no private rows) measure real resource use, stop reasons, completed Films against arrivals, oldest due age, scope latency, partial-unit resume, overlap and restart safety, absence of busy loops and starvation, upstream errors and cooldown, and EPG contention. They must show that the design **drains more than two Films when resources permit**, **crosses step and instance boundaries without waiting an hour**, and **sustains the intended arrival workload with headroom** (the rated stream, the Discovery stream of decision 2 at its dated estimate of about 12 Films per hour, Watchlist work, retries and a backlog). No production capacity is claimed from fixtures; if a real constraint prevents it, the actual bottleneck and its remedy are identified, **not a manufactured quota**. Discovery-specific acceptance is part of the later scope delivery and activation (decision 8), not of a separate scaling project.

**No material trade-off remains.** The model adds no paid service or plan, no new business policy and no bypass of owner authorization; it reuses an accepted pattern and the Workflows already deployed. Owner activation and production-change gates still apply.

- **Independent flags and cutoffs.** Enabling or disabling a scope and setting its cutoff are separate owner operations, each a compare-and-swap on that scope row's `revision`, so a stale request changes nothing and says so.

Discovery Films are planned only by code that has the predicate; an older version has no Discovery membership and never selects them.
- **Fail closed.** Without the scope table, or with the `discovery` row disabled or absent, C3c plans nothing.

**Coordination with AIR-605 and what is not edited.** A delivered migration is **never** edited because it has not been applied. AIR-605's schema is not in `main`; coordinating the per-scope table into AIR-605 before its delivery is possible **only with explicit scope approval of AIR-605**, which this record does not assume. Otherwise the table is a forward migration after AIR-605's. AIR-605's and AIR-607's initial rated scope and the AIR-275 boundary are preserved either way.

**Compatibility.** Dormant code reads "not active"; an old Worker version ignores the new column, trigger, index and table; activating C3c needs every apply prerequisite and fails closed otherwise.

**Rollback.** Pause stops all scopes at once (the existing control, generation fence and drain); disabling the `discovery` row stops that scope alone. Written values stay: they are valid canonical data under fill-only and precedence, corrected by the owner and review routes. A code revert stops future ticks but does not undo data, and the admission fact stays harmlessly in place.

## Examples

- **A new Film.** `tmdb_123` is admitted at 10:00 after the cutoff: the trigger stamps its admission row `first_current_at = 10:00`, it holds one trusted `tmdb` claim, and its admission is current. It is in C3c **whether or not its canonical mutation was a no-op**, and C1 fetches `movie/123?append_to_response=credits,translations` and Wikidata at a following tick, writes the six core fields, up to 30 credits and the `tmdb` localization source (the `ru` selection), each with C1's own `observed_at`.
- **An admission before the cutoff, or from before the fact.** A stamped admission earlier than the cutoff is `before_cutoff`; a row that existed when the column was added is `unknown_history` (`NULL`), permanently. Both are history, counted, served from the payload and decided only by HA; neither is promoted by a sweep that creates its canonical Film later.
- **An EPG touch or a revalidation.** The payload and the admission `generation` move; the stamp does not; membership is unchanged and no fetch is triggered.
- **A rated and Discovery Film.** It is in two scopes and planned once, with one state set and the one set of existing keys (`enrich:<film>:core` and the others of decision 1).
- **A lid claim appears later.** The catalog is entirely complete and idle. A trusted Letterboxd claim appears on one Film; within the maintenance interval (R3) the D1-only anchor pass finds the changed digest, Letterboxd becomes applicable and reopens only its gaps (ADR 0211 decision 3b), R1 becomes runnable and one run commits it once; with nothing changed no run starts and nothing is fetched. The Film stays in C3c.
- **An ambiguous translation.** Two different `ru` titles with no `RU` winner: nothing is written and the gap is recorded; the response `title` is not used.
- **A burst of arrivals.** Forty Films become eligible within an hour, from the rated and Discovery scopes. The `ensure` cron (at most five minutes later) finds runnable work and starts an instance. Its steps take the Films oldest `due_since` first, one step per Film, not two per hour. When the instance reaches its statement budget it creates its successor and finishes, and the series continues at once, not at the next hour. When the queue is empty the last run finishes and nothing polls; the cron's idle check costs about two reads.
- **A cooldown.** TMDB answers `429` with `Retry-After`: the shared cooldown is set and the run skips every TMDB-dependent source and keeps working on what does not need TMDB. As ADR 0211 accepted, an **active shared upstream cooldown counts as upstream unavailability** toward the 72 h fallback; a **local** stop (a handover, an EPG yield, a pause, a lost lease or a resource stop) does not. When only cooled work remains the run finishes; `ensure` starts one when the cooldown has ended.
- **A pause.** The owner pauses: the control generation moves, every in-flight batch fails its guard, the run writes the terminal `stopped` entry and finishes, and resume calls `ensure` at once with the cutoff, scopes and pending work intact.
