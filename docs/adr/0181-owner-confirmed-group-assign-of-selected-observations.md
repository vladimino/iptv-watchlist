## Context

Audit of `main` at 648800c2 (read-only; no production access):

- The only owner identity write is `discovery_identity_correct@1`: one observation key, one `discovery_intent` row, one fenced `DiscoveryAuthority.ownerDecision` batch (decision-hash CAS, lease, history row, receipt). `GET /api/admin/identity-work` and `/admin/identity` are read-only and prove no correlation between observations (ADR 0170).
- `discovery_observation` has the primary key `(source_key, observation_key)` and one other index `(residence, observation_key)`. `observation_key` is `normalizedTitle|year`. Plans checked with `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` on the schema through migration 0027: a key **prefix range** (`observation_key >= ? AND observation_key < ?` with `source_key = ?`) is an index `SEARCH` and can carry a `LIMIT`; `normalized_title = ?` and `confirmed_tmdb_id = ?` lead only with `source_key = ?`, so they scan every active row and are not bounded without a new index (a migration).
- Decisions are read by primary key (`discovery_decision.observation_key`) and shows by `(source_key, observation_key)`, so evidence for a known key list is a constant number of queries.
- The existing observation index is a bounded projection (cap 3000, scope `current_unresolved_with_decision`) and is partial by construction.
- `AdminActions` already supports a confirmation over many records: `ids`, a per-record `version`, a budget `maxRecords` (contract ceiling 50) and a receipt with one outcome per record. **But `previewAction` is eligible when ANY requested record is eligible**: ineligible, excluded and unread ids go to `excluded` or `missing`, `affected` holds only the eligible subset sorted by id, and `confirmAction` issues exactly `affected`. A group confirmation therefore must compare `affected` with the owner's selection itself (decision 4).
- The single-key gate (`identityCommand` with `catalogueFilmTrust`) refuses television, an unreadable catalogue, conflicting identity and a year more than one apart whenever both years are known (`year_conflict`, whether or not the id is the observation's confirmed id; a confirmed id only excuses an unknown year). It has no runtime check. The single-key offer (`identityOffer`) additionally requires a fresh, **complete** observation index, no live writer and a known series state.
- Every `DiscoveryAuthority.ownerDecision` advances `discovery_authority.generation` (the intent moving to `running` and the commit each move it) and needs a live lease (TTL 90 seconds). A group run therefore moves the generation by its own writes. A revoked owner assign leaves a targeted fence for `(observation, tmdbId)` ([ADR 0178](0178-identity-correction-revoke-event-contract.md)).
- The only owner correction stored in production is the AIR-498 reject; no group has ever been confirmed.

## Decision

A group is a **convenience over single-key owner writes, not a new authority**: each selected key is written by the existing fenced writer and keeps its own intent, history row and receipt.

1. **Anchor.** A group starts from one canonical movie `tmdb:<digits>` that exists in the D1 catalog as a movie (never television), under the published programme run. There is no title-only start and no wildcard over future observations.
2. **Candidate discovery, migration-free, deterministic and fully counted.** Anchors are the normalized primary title, original title and up to two alternative titles of the catalogue Film, in catalogue order, deduplicated; at most four, and any further distinct title is reported as `anchorsOmitted`. Each anchor runs one primary-key prefix range, `ORDER BY observation_key LIMIT 26`, with `residence = 'active'`; 26 rows returned means that anchor has `more: true` and 25 are used. Candidates are merged by key (one row per key, listing every anchor that found it), ordered by key, and cut at 50. The response carries per-anchor `returned` and `more`, `anchorsOmitted`, `merged`, `shown` and `dropped`, always `coverage: partial`, and the anchors used. Nothing is omitted without a count and a flag. Evidence is then read for the shown keys in constant queries (decision by key, observation row by key, shows by key), never per candidate. It never calls the list "all observations" and never finds translations or other spellings; that gap is stated, not hidden.
3. **Per-key proof and per-candidate assessment, fail closed.** The group path does not use the single-key *complete-index* requirement, because the discovery window is partial by design. It replaces it with a **complete per-key D1 proof**, read at preview, at confirm and again at execute: the observation row of that key (active, belonging to the published programme run, evidence key and payload present and parseable), the decision row of that key with `decision_hash` equal to the SHA-256 of its payload, the shows rows, and the catalogue Film of the anchor read from D1 as a movie with canonical identity `tmdb:<digits>`. The unchanged pure `identityCommand` and `catalogueFilmTrust` then run on a record built from that proof: the gate is not weakened, only the source of the record changes. The live-writer and series refusals of the single-key offer still apply, through the lease and the coordination read of each run. A key whose proof is missing, stale, torn or untrusted is not selectable at preview; at confirm or execute it refuses the whole group. Beyond the gate, a key is selectable only when the current decision is not an owner assignment of another id, the scorer's accepted id is not a different id, no revoked-assign fence names this id, and, new for groups, a known observation duration is within the larger of 10 minutes and 15% of the Film's runtime (an unknown runtime is shown, not blocked). Each row shows the year and runtime differences, the conflicts, the current decision, its hash, the shows and the exact before and after.
4. **Selection binds exactly the owner's explicit keys.** 1 to 10 explicit keys, no duplicates. The server re-derives the candidate set and the proof for every selected key at confirm and requires the previewed `affected` ids to equal the selected set exactly, each with the expected per-key version `decisionHash:assign:tmdbId`. Any selected key that is unknown, excluded, missing or whose version differs refuses the **whole** confirmation (`group_selection_changed`, listing the failing keys and reasons) before any intent is written; the issuance is never shrunk to an eligible subset. More than 10 is refused (`group_too_large`), never truncated. Execute repeats the same binding against the issued scope, then each key still passes its own decision-hash CAS before its commit.
5. **Confirmation and an atomic issuance barrier.** One new versioned capability `discovery_identity_group_assign@1` (subject kind `observation`, budget `maxRecords: 10`) with one issuance whose scope fingerprint covers exactly the selected keys and versions. Confirm inserts **all N child intents in one D1 batch** (N is at most 10, under the statement cap of 40, and a D1 batch is one transaction), so a partial issuance cannot be created. The children use the existing kind `identity_assign` (no migration) and the key `<groupKey>-NN`, where `NN` is the 1-based position of the key in the issued, sorted `ids`; the owner-visible group key keeps the existing 16 to 80 character pattern and is limited to 72 so the child key stays within 80. Each child carries `command.group {id, size, index, anchorTmdbId}` and its own expected hash. **Execution barrier:** before any write the run reads all N children by their derived keys and requires exactly N rows, kind `identity_assign`, the same group id, size and anchor, the index and `observationKey` matching the issued ids in order, each expected hash equal to the issued per-key version, and states only `approved` or `receipt`. Anything else (missing or extra child, mismatch, a `running` child) refuses with nothing written; a missing child is never recreated and a `running` child is `unknown`, never continued past. The selected scope can never change on replay.
6. **Execute.** Sequential in one request under one lease, every key through the existing `ownerDecision`. Per-key outcome: `applied`, `conflict`, `refused`, `skipped`, `expired`, `not_attempted` or `unknown`. No all-or-nothing promise. A conflict or refusal of one key does not stop the others. **Drift fence:** the run reads the authority once after taking the lease, records the programme run and the generation, and before each key re-reads them; the programme run must be unchanged, and the generation must equal the value the run itself observed after its own previous write (self-progress is allowed, any other movement is drift). Drift stops the run (`programme_moved` or `generation_moved`) and the remaining keys are `not_attempted`. **Lease:** the run renews the lease before each key and stops with `lease_lost` if renewal fails; it also starts no new key later than 60 seconds after taking the lease, leaving margin to the 90-second TTL. **Expiry:** the issuance expiry is checked before the run and before each key; a key not started by then is `expired`. An `unknown` outcome stops the run and the rest is `not_attempted`; nothing is retried blindly. A **replay** of the same confirmation first passes the execution barrier, never recreates a child, leaves receipts as they are, and re-evaluates only children without a receipt under the same confirmation, each again behind its CAS; receipt-only children return their stored receipts with no write. A refused key can be re-evaluated only by a new preview and a new confirmation.
7. **Reads and writes.** The group preview is a GET: no write, no TMDB or model call, bounded. POST only for confirm and execute, with the same Access, same-origin and action-header guards as the identity writes. No Discovery chunk, EPG or TMDB run is started; the published view updates on the ordinary next chunk and the UI says so.
8. **UI.** A separate owner page `/admin/identity/group?tmdbId=` with its own bundle, reached by a link from a Film row of `/admin/identity` and from the Limbo observation detail. The queue and its GET stay read-only (the link is an anchor, not a form).

Not decided here: an index on `confirmed_tmdb_id` or `normalized_title` (a migration, which would widen discovery and needs its own owner GO), group reject or group revoke, any automatic or future-observation promotion, and every production write.

## Alternatives considered

- **Loop the single-key flow in the browser.** Rejected by the card: it hides N confirmations behind one click and gives no exact group scope.
- **All-or-nothing in one transaction.** Rejected: the owner writer is fenced per key with its own CAS and a statement cap of 40, and one stale key would block the rest; the card also forbids promising it.
- **New group table or intent kind.** Needs a migration and adds no authority; per-key intents already hold the receipts, and the group result is derived by reading its children.
- **Add an index first (migration 0028) for confirmed-id candidates.** More complete discovery, but a schema change before the need is shown; left as an explicit follow-up.
- **Candidates from the existing bounded observation index only.** Reuses code, but depends on its scope and cannot find an observation the index omits.

## Consequences

Coverage is partial and says so: an observation whose title does not share a prefix with any anchor is not offered. Each key costs one fenced D1 batch plus its proof reads, so a run of ten is ten batches under one lease with the stop rules of decision 6 (a live Discovery chunk makes the run refuse with `lease_held`, as the single-key route does). Because the issuance is one atomic batch and the run is gated by a barrier, an interrupted run leaves a group that is either not issued or issued completely, and a replay cannot grow, shrink or reorder it. The group result after a reload is reconstructed from the per-key intents. Selecting keys that gain a conflicting decision between preview and execute makes those keys `conflict`, not silent writes.

## Recovery and migration boundary

Every key is an ordinary owner assignment: undoing one is the ADR 0178 revoke or supersede of that key, never a group undo. Code rollback does not revert D1 rows. No migration is proposed. This ADR authorizes no production write; the real group confirmation is the owner's own action in the UI.
