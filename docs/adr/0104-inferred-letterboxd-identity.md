# ADR 0104: A personal Letterboxd export may link a film to TMDB by one unique original title and year, recorded as inferred

**Status:** Accepted  
**Original decision date:** 2026-11-08 (owner decision on the AIR-388 card, Madrid)  
**Recorded at:** 2026-11-08  
**Approval source:** the owner's decision comment on the [AIR-388 card](https://trello.com/c/fFvwYLjM#comment-6af092e25b9e2259c53d3e52) (2026-11-08 15:18 UTC): pragmatic identity matching is accepted, the chance of a collision is negligible and can be fixed per film, and defensive mechanisms that only get in the way are not wanted. This is a decision about code and contracts. It is **not** authorization for any production read or write, snapshot, network request, import, flag, Task or cutover.  
**Related work:** [AIR-388](https://trello.com/c/fFvwYLjM); [AIR-185](https://trello.com/c/PKvrDGKZ) (rejected: Letterboxd challenge, no verified decision); [AIR-187](https://trello.com/c/JWCpAsY3); [AIR-340](https://trello.com/c/zWxj3laC); [AIR-387](https://trello.com/c/bhazAstV); [ADR 0009](0009-canonical-film-person-identity.md), [ADR 0087](0087-rated-archive-film-admission.md)  
**Supersedes:** None. It adds one narrow exception to the matcher's "a title and year agreement is never accepted" rule and changes no earlier Accepted record.  
**Superseded by:** None

## Context

The matcher of a Letterboxd account export ([export contract](../reference/integrations/letterboxd-export.md)) accepts a film only through a stored Letterboxd id or a trusted TMDB identity. A title and year agreement was always a `candidate` for manual review. The only way to turn a new code into a verified Letterboxd-to-TMDB pair is the film page behind the `boxd.it` short code, and that path ends at a Cloudflare challenge ([AIR-185](https://trello.com/c/PKvrDGKZ) found no verified decision; the 403 evidence is in the [Letterboxd evidence index](../evidence/letterboxd/README.md)). The owner's goal is that every film of their own export lives in the existing canonical catalog, with watched and rating facts. On the offline reconciliation of the 2026-11-08 export against the 2026-10-28 snapshot, 569 films were title and year candidates that already exist in the catalog (the snapshot catalog held 1,947 films; the live one is larger), 420 were verified, and 1,474 had no stored title at all.

## Decision

1. **A new status, `inferred`, for a personal export only.** With the policy switched on (`matchExport(films, index, {inferred: true})`; the baseline `plan --include-inferred`), a film is `inferred` when all of these hold: the export year is present and equals a stored year of the film; the normalized export name equals the stored **original** title of the film (an alias or localized title alone is not enough); the stored records lead to exactly one TMDB movie (one identity among all matching records, so a remake in another year is told apart by its year); no other export code claims that movie and no already accepted film holds it; no stored link joins that movie to another Letterboxd id or this code to another TMDB movie. The candidates are chosen among the films with that exact year and original title **before** any ambiguity is judged, so a namesake one year away is no ambiguity (legacy mode keeps the ±1 window). The method is `title_year_unique`.
2. **`accepted` and the shared lists keep their meaning.** `accepted` stays "verified". `STATUSES` and `METHODS` keep their keys, so every report built from them is unchanged; the new values are exported separately. The policy is off by default in the matcher and in `plan`. The display-exclusion builder, the reconciliation report and the Worker's archive preview do not enable it. The global rules for trusted identity of any other writer or reader are not changed.
3. **Everything else with a title match is a compact review bucket, not a claim.** Each stays a `candidate` with one reason: `year_drift` (a year off by one), `alias_title_only`, `tmdb_linked_elsewhere`, `tmdb_duplicate_in_export`, `tmdb_already_accepted`, `no_tmdb_id`, `missing_year`; ambiguous matches, conflicts, a TV page and an unmatched title are unchanged. The plan report counts them per reason without names.
4. **Provenance and correction.** A plan item for an inferred film carries `identity: "inferred"` and its TMDB id; its Letterboxd id is the export code. The route validates the kind (any other value is `invalid_request`), writes `identity: {kind, basis, sourceRef}` on the fact record that first receives the Letterboxd id in the same compare-and-swap as the first fact, never on a record that already holds facts and a Letterboxd id, and lists `identity` per film in the chunk receipt. A verified item keeps the exact old shape and chunk hash. Nothing is shown as verified. **Correction contract.** A wrong inferred link is corrected per film, with one small fenced action and the existing rollback, and no migration framework: `POST /api/letterboxd/baseline/unlink` (CLI `unlink --lid <code>`) takes a film of a committed chunk whose receipt entry is `inferred`, reverses its facts under the rollback rules (an observation a later writer replaced is kept as superseded), and removes the Letterboxd alias and the `identity` that import made, only while the record still carries that import's `identity.sourceRef`; the TMDB alias, other facts and verified links are never touched, a repeat changes nothing, and any other film is refused (`not_inferred`, `not_committed`). The chunk rollback now does the same unlink for every inferred film of its chunk after reversing the facts. After either, the code can be linked again to the corrected movie by a later export without an `alias_conflict`.
5. **Facts follow the existing contract.** The import still writes only `watched: true`, a rating or "not rated", and `watchlist: true`; it never sets `watched: false` or `watchlist: false` from absence, never puts a watched film on the watchlist, and uses the same newer-wins merge, receipts and rollback. The view of ratings stays the D1 or R2 projection the existing writer already chooses by marker.
6. **Films with no stored title are not decided here.** For export films the catalog does not know (1,474 in the offline run), the intended path is a bounded TMDB search by the same rule (one result whose original title equals the normalized export name and whose release year equals the export year, a movie only), admitted through the existing Film admission contract with the same `inferred` provenance and the rated and watchlist rows kept apart. That needs a TMDB request budget, a rate limit and its own review, so it is **not implemented or authorized by this record**. Until then those films stay unresolved and are reported, not dropped.

Not decided: any production plan, snapshot, validate, commit or rollback; the live catalog read that would shrink the unmatched set; the TMDB search step; the browser apply path (its index does not read the catalog, so it never sees an inferred film); AIR-321 Film authority.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep requiring a verified pair:** rejected by the owner; the only source is blocked and bypassing the challenge is not allowed.
- **Accept title and year as `accepted`:** rejected; it would silently change what every other reader of `accepted` (the display exclusion, the baseline plan) relies on and would hide the inferred nature.
- **Match any alias or a year off by one:** rejected for the automatic path; those cases are exactly the review buckets, because a remake or a namesake is the realistic mistake.
- **A broad correction framework or a Personal Library model:** not added; the owner asked for no extra protective machinery, so correction is one fenced per-film unlink plus the existing chunk rollback.

## Consequences

More of the export reaches the catalog without the blocked page lookup. The price is a small, accepted chance of a wrong link for a film with a unique original title and year; it is visible (the `identity` field, the receipt) and correctable per film (the unlink action). The snapshot's link index is an R2 copy that can be behind the D1 ratings domain, so the matcher may miss a recent contradicting link; the commit-time alias conflict check is the authority and counts a conflict instead of writing. New provenance is additive on the record; older readers ignore it. A code revert does not remove links or facts already written; use the unlink action or the receipt-driven rollback while the receipt exists.

## Recovery and migration boundary

Reversible: the code and the plan switch. Not reverted by code rollback: any fact, link, record or receipt a commit wrote, and any later D1 projection. No migration, flag, Task, network request, snapshot or production write is part of this change; an actual import needs its own owner-approved bounded plan with receipts and rollback.

## Evidence

- **Offline measurement:** the 2026-11-08 owner export against the 2026-10-28 local snapshot, counts only, recorded on the [AIR-388 card](https://trello.com/c/fFvwYLjM): 420 verified, 534 inferred, 35 review (18 `alias_title_only`, 17 `year_drift`), 1 ambiguous, 1,474 unmatched. The snapshot is old and its link index may be behind; this is not a production count.
- **Tests:** `tests/letterboxd-inferred.mjs` (matcher cases and every review bucket, plan switch and counts, route validation, provenance, replay, no relabelling), with the existing `letterboxd-reconcile`, `letterboxd-baseline-import`, `letterboxd-baseline-chunks`, `letterboxd-facts`, rated and archive tests unchanged.
- **Uncertainty:** the live catalog size, the behaviour on current D1-owned links, and the real rate of wrong links are unmeasured until an approved plan runs.

## Follow-ups

A bounded TMDB search and admission for the films the catalog does not know; a fresh catalog read to recount the unmatched set; the owner's approval of a concrete production plan.
