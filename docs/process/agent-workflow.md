# Agent delivery workflow

This document is the durable collaboration policy for the IPTV Watchlist project. It does **not** track today's active Trello card, latest PR, deployment version, or production feature flags. Those must be checked live. For code-agent Git preflight and repository safety, also follow [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md); for Trello formatting follow [trello-card-conventions.md](trello-card-conventions.md).

## Architectural decisions and visual QA

The owner decides **material new** architecture, security or product trade-offs before implementation, based on alternatives, consequences and rollback. An ADR documenting an **already approved** choice may be finalized as Accepted with the approval reference; never silently accept an undecided substantive change. Preserve accepted ADR history by superseding records.

### Architecture review checks

Before proposing a material design or presenting owner choices, the Coordinator and Independent Reviewer must apply the [decision ownership boundary](authorization-and-escalation.md#product-intent-and-engineering-autonomy). The Executor should use the same checks when preparing a design, not wait for review:

- **Intent and direction:** does the design deliver the agreed product outcome and fit the accepted strategic architecture? Explain effects on actual workload, freshness, coverage and priorities where relevant.
- **Necessity and reuse:** can an existing component fulfill the same responsibility? Compare end-to-end delivery, operational and maintenance cost before introducing another producer, queue, state store, control plane or similar subsystem. Explain why any added separation is necessary.
- **Constraint evidence:** distinguish real hard constraints, safety bounds, owner-approved product/cost limits and provisional tuning defaults. Identify the rationale, source or evidence, expected impact and safe continuation/recovery for material limits; do not turn estimates into arbitrary acceptance gates or cap throughput for convenience.
- **Decision ownership:** expose all material product/architecture/risk trade-offs and their consequences; leave ordinary engineering choices to the Executor and Reviewer. Do not manufacture owner choices for implementation details, or treat package acceptance as approval of an undisclosed compromise.
- **Delivery simplicity:** minimize independent infrastructure, duplicated logic, cards and dependencies without collapsing genuinely independent or unsafe rollout steps. Preserve explicit authority, safety, review and production gates.

Blocking findings include an unjustified material constraint, hidden product compromise, unnecessary material duplication, or a conflict with approved direction. Return these to the Executor for correction before presenting a design for final owner disposition; where a material choice is necessary, present the actual trade-off and recommendation. Apply this as part of the existing independent review and decision facilitation, proportionate to impact, not as a new ceremony, separate approval or automated CI gate. Routine reversible details do not need a separate ADR or owner decision. Existing accepted contracts may only be changed through their established amendment/supersession process.

### Conditional Concept Review

Choose the architectural direction while changing it is still cheap. This is an early part of the existing decision facilitation protocol, not a separate delivery phase for every ADR.

**Trigger and responsibility.** Before assigning detailed architectural design, the Coordinator checks the approved product intent, existing contracts and plausible alternatives. Use Concept Review when an unresolved choice between credible concepts would materially change the product outcome, system responsibilities, data authority, persistent model, infrastructure, risk, or delivery and maintenance cost. Examples include extending an existing producer versus adding another, or choosing a new domain authority. A material new direction discovered during design or repeated review findings caused by the same structural choice also triggers reassessment before further expensive detail. If a material direction is already explicitly approved, cite it and proceed; do not ask again. Routine engineering choices (indexes, batch sizes, algorithms within approved intent) do not trigger this protocol merely because alternatives exist. A material trade-off hidden in such a choice is not routine.

**Concept Brief.** The Coordinator's initial handoff states the problem, desired outcome and whether Concept Review is needed. If triggered, the Executor first researches the current code, accepted ADRs, actual dependencies and applicable constraints, then posts a short brief in the existing architecture card (or links an existing durable proposal). Include:

- The product problem, desired outcome and realistic workload or success criteria.
- Existing capabilities and opportunities for reuse; concrete constraints and relevant evidence.
- Genuinely different feasible concepts, including the simplest viable option, with stable labels.
- Material consequences for product behavior, system responsibilities, cost, risk, recovery, delivery and maintenance; approximate magnitudes where useful, not fabricated precision.
- The Executor's reasoned recommendation and the specific unresolved decisions requiring the owner.

Do only enough investigation, sketches or bounded feasibility analysis to compare the concepts responsibly. Do not elaborate full schemas, API contracts, recovery algorithms, test matrices or implementation breakdowns for competing concepts before direction is chosen. If essential feasibility evidence is missing, identify the uncertainty and resolve it proportionately; do not present an infeasible option as ready or require unnecessary experiments. Existing authorization applies to all investigation.

**Decision and handoff.** The Coordinator independently challenges the brief using the architecture review checks above, including reuse, realistic workload, strategic alignment and whole-system simplicity. Return ordinary defects and delegated engineering details to engineering; do not ask the owner to arbitrate them. Present only genuinely unresolved material choices to the owner using the existing facilitation rules (clear alternatives, consequences, independent recommendation, and individual choices). Record the explicit direction, qualifications, evidence and unresolved questions in the existing card or linked decision record. If no material choice remains, the Coordinator records the applicable prior decision or delegated technical rationale and proceeds within scope. Silence, general support and an Executor recommendation are not owner approval.

**Proceed with the chosen concept.** The Coordinator sends a consolidated handoff with the selected direction and constraints; the Executor details that concept in the Proposed ADR. Preserve concise rejected alternatives and their rationale, not full speculative designs. Independent review verifies the detailed contract and selected direction. Reopen concept selection only if new evidence exposes a materially different trade-off, and stop detailing the affected choice until resolved. At final ADR disposition, reuse recorded direction decisions rather than asking the same questions again; the mandatory ADR-specific final disposition and merge gate below still apply. Concept direction is not implementation GO, a production authorization or permission to change accepted contracts.

Keep the brief proportional to the decision. Use the same architecture card, roles and existing PR/review workflow; do not create a separate Concept Review card, list, ADR number, mandatory template artifact or approval ceremony. The Coordinator owns screening and handoff; the Executor must raise an overlooked material fork before expensive design. Independent review checks compliance as part of the existing review, not a new automated CI gate.

### ADR decision facilitation protocol

The Coordinator owns preparation and facilitation of material new architecture decisions, not merely collection of approvals. This protocol applies before requesting an owner decision on a new or substantively changed ADR. Where the conditional Concept Review above applies, start facilitation at concept level before detailed design; apply the same rules to any remaining material choices in the detailed ADR. It supplements, and does not replace, the merge gate below. Do not repeat the process for an already explicitly approved decision; verify and link that decision instead.

1. **Prepare from live evidence.** Read the current PR, actual ADRs, affected contracts and code as needed, review findings and prior owner decisions. Verify the current HEAD, ADR numbers and cross-ADR dependencies. Independently assess technical feasibility, safety and the architecture review checks above before presenting a design for acceptance. Return technical defects to the Executor rather than asking the owner to resolve ordinary engineering errors. If an unresolved defect depends on a material owner choice, make that dependency explicit and distinguish direction-setting from final acceptance.
2. **Present one ADR at a time.** Explain the current behavior, the concrete problem and why it matters, then the proposed design, realistic alternatives and consequences. For every material undecided choice, identify the problem it resolves, options with stable labels, benefits, drawbacks, scope/dependency and operational implications where relevant, and the Coordinator's own reasoned recommendation. Do not simply repeat the Executor's recommendation. Use examples or diagrams where they improve understanding. Distinguish settled decisions, new choices and technical details delegated to engineering; do not reopen approved choices without a material reason.
3. **Make decisions individually actionable.** Provide an interactive picker with separate options and an optional comment for every open choice when the interface supports it; otherwise provide an equivalent numbered text response format. Allow partial answers, alternative proposals and clarification. A recommended-default shortcut may preselect options only on the owner's explicit action; preselection, silence or a general expression of support is not a decision. Never substitute a single package approval for distinct material choices. Present the next ADR only after the owner has resolved the current one or explicitly deferred its remaining choices.
4. **Collect before handing back.** Maintain a decision matrix keyed by ADR and choice, preserving exact selections, qualifications, deferred work and unresolved questions. For a multi-ADR package, retain decisions during the discussion and do not send incremental implementation or revision instructions to the Executor unless the owner requests it. Reconcile cross-ADR dependencies, summarize the complete outcome for the owner and send one consolidated handoff after the discussion. Preserve the owner's actual decision and its provenance in a durable accessible record; if the session ends prematurely, preserve a clearly labeled incomplete handoff without treating it as approval or a request to implement.
5. **Close the decision loop.** The Executor revises the proposal, registry and affected delivery plan to match the decisions, with explicit follow-up work and gates. The independent Reviewer verifies the revised actual HEAD against every selection and qualification. Present only genuinely new or changed material trade-offs for further choice, not previously resolved questions. Separate architecture disposition from implementation authorization and production authorization. Before finalization, verify the explicit ADR-specific owner disposition and its direct durable link under the merge gate below. A Coordinator's transcription, a completed picker alone without durable decision provenance, or technical review is not a substitute for the required owner decision.

The expected output is an informed, traceable decision per material choice, an internally consistent revised design and an executable delivery plan. A summary of recommendations, a diagram alone or a request to approve all recommendations does not satisfy this protocol. Keep the effort proportional to the number and impact of open choices; do not manufacture alternatives for routine technical details.

### ADR merge gate

**This is a mandatory exception to ordinary PR merge authorization, even for docs-only PRs.** A PR adding or modifying any numbered `docs/adr/NNNN-*.md` record, or adding or changing its registry row in `docs/adr/README.md`, MUST remain open while any affected ADR is `Proposed`, is undecided, or lacks verifiable owner disposition. A proposal may be reviewed and revised in an open PR, but it is not mergeable historical evidence.

Before merge the Delivery Controller MUST check the latest PR HEAD and the actual owner decision, not only an Executor report:

1. Identify each ADR number touched by the PR (including changed registry rows). Verify an **explicit Product Owner decision on that ADR and the selected design/option**. Approval to investigate, implement offline, create an ADR, open a PR or deliver the parent Trello card is not ADR acceptance. A prior explicit decision can be reused without requesting approval again, but must be linked.
2. Verify that each affected ADR and its registry row agree on a final **`Accepted` or `Rejected`** status and give the selected option, the decision date and an `Approval source` link to the recorded decision. The PR body must include `ADR owner decision: <URL>` for the affected record(s). An agent's assertion or a link to the card without the recorded decision is insufficient.

   **Who records the decision.** The Product Owner decides and gives the approval to the Delivery Controller in whatever channel they use with the Controller; chat is fine, and the owner does not have to write on the PR or link their own conversation. The Controller then informs the Executor with a comment on the pull request that states the ADR number, the selected option and the date. That Controller PR comment is the recorded approval source: the Executor links it as `Approval source` in the ADR and in the `docs/adr/README.md` registry entry, together with the selected option and decision date. A comment written by the owner on GitHub or Trello is also an acceptable source, but is not required.

   **When the Executor finalizes.** The Executor does not mark an ADR `Accepted` or `Rejected` on the basis of its own question to the owner, a relayed chat quote that the relaying role itself calls interim, or a reviewer's remark. It waits for the Controller's PR comment recording the owner's ADR-specific decision (selected option), then updates the ADR, the registry row, any `Amended by` / `Supersedes` cross-references and the PR body in one change. Until then the ADR stays `Proposed` and the PR MUST NOT merge.
3. For **`Rejected`**, allow a docs-only archival PR recording the rejected alternative; never merge code or a data/operational change implementing the rejected design. For **`Accepted`**, the approval covers the recorded design, not production mutation or other named operational gates.
4. If one PR combines independently deliverable evidence with a still-`Proposed` ADR, split the evidence into a separate PR and leave the ADR PR open. Do not merge an undecided ADR to make the evidence available; do not merge related implementation in another PR to bypass this hold.
5. **STOP rather than merge** if the status, chosen option, the Controller-recorded (or owner-authored) decision or the `Approval source` link cannot be verified, regardless of independent review, resolved threads, green exact-head CI, implementation authorization, routine-merge delegation or a `docs-only` designation. A status edit by an agent is not itself an owner decision. On the latest HEAD the Controller verifies the status and that the linked comment records the owner's decision for each affected ADR.

The independent Reviewer checks this gate during review; the Controller checks it again immediately before merge. The policy is effective now as a manual gate. Any automated CI implementation must examine only affected ADR files and changed registry entries, not reject unrelated PRs because historical `Proposed` records already exist in `main`.

Changed Mermaid diagrams require actual rendered GitHub visual QA, legibility and independent semantic review against code/contracts. Routine diagram edits do **not** require owner sign-off; retain an exceptional visual gate explicitly named in approved scope. Product UI acceptance stays with the owner when scoped.

The Executor handles ordinary ADR/Markdown/Mermaid fixes and re-runs CI; no special handoff to ChatGPT solely to flip an approved ADR status. See [authorization and escalation](authorization-and-escalation.md).

## Roles and sources of truth

- **Product Owner:** intent, priorities, material technical/product decisions and final product acceptance.
- **Planner / Coordinator:** scopes and orders approved work, verifies prerequisites, handles routine exceptions and prepares major decisions.
- **Executor:** any authorized coding/documentation agent implements work and fixes.
- **Independent Reviewer:** checks actual PR diff, safety and acceptance on exact HEAD, independent of implementation context.
- **Delivery Controller:** tracks approved merge, CI/CD, safe verification and Trello status/evidence; may be an agent or durable external service, never automatically an interactive chat.
- **Trello:** current approved scope, prerequisites, status and product disposition. **GitHub:** main, PR, reviews and CI. **Production:** verified readback of state and authorities.

Read live card, comments, dependencies, current main and affected runbooks before acting. Handoffs persist in Trello/GitHub, not in chat memory. See [authorization](authorization-and-escalation.md).

## Language and public-repository boundary

- **Trello:** Russian for cards, comments, dependencies, delivery reports, and instructions intended for Cursor.
- **GitHub:** English for PR descriptions, review summaries, inline review comments, code discussions, and repository documentation.
- **Technical names:** preserve function/class names, API paths, field names, error/reason codes, commit SHAs, and other identifiers exactly.
- **ChatGPT conversation:** Russian by default, unless the owner asks otherwise.
- This repository is public. Never post secrets, authorization tokens, private production payloads, raw model responses/prompts, personally identifying data or confidential operational records to GitHub. Use sanitized aggregate evidence where appropriate.

## Work item scope, acceptance and dependencies

- Each Trello card must deliver one independently reviewable and closeable outcome. Avoid numbered implementation epics hidden in one card. Split separate shadow/cutover/stabilization or live-migration outcomes into independent cards before work starts.
- Follow the detailed [Trello card conventions](trello-card-conventions.md) for Russian descriptions, '[AIR-N]' names, a current plan-context introduction, relevant functional labels, scope, objectively verifiable Done, tests/evidence and rollback.
- Real prerequisites belong in a **native Trello checklist**. **Read existing checklists first; reuse an existing prerequisites checklist instead of creating a duplicate**, and deduplicate prerequisite items across legacy checklists. Each item must be **only the bare Trello card URL**, never a Markdown link or an AIR/title-prefixed label; this is required for Trello to display the actual linked card title and status. Do not create false blockers or empty checklists. Reconcile checkmarks with *actual* Done status after closing upstream cards.
- Give direct Trello links to relevant prerequisites and successor work in PR descriptions and final delivery reports. Do not assign the next AIR number without checking whether it is already used.
- **Ambiguous Trello write results are not permission to retry.** After a timeout, tool error or missing/unclear write response, read or search live Trello to determine whether the mutation actually succeeded before repeating it. Especially never retry `create` blindly: check the intended card's name, AIR number, board/list, and existing matches first. If a duplicate is found, preserve the canonical card and its activity, then archive only the confirmed duplicate. Apply the same verify-before-retry rule to moves, comments, checklist edits and other non-idempotent mutations.
- Code ready, PR merged, successful deployment, read-only production smoke test, shadow verification and cutover are different claims. Record precisely which of them is proven.
- The board's **«Верификация»** column is the explicit post-delivery, pre-acceptance state. Use it only when the approved implementation is merged into current `main`, required post-merge test/build and Cloudflare deploy jobs have succeeded (or the card's explicitly approved delivery boundary is otherwise complete), and the only remaining acceptance evidence requires a later natural production run, a scheduled external observation, a bounded stabilization interval, or an owner visual/product check. Record the outstanding gate and its expected trigger in the card. Do not confuse this state with `Готово`.

## ChatGPT Scheduled Tasks: explicit owner permission only

Agents must **not create, enable, disable, reschedule, modify, or manually trigger a ChatGPT Scheduled Task unless the owner explicitly authorizes that exact operation**. This covers temporary and one-off tasks as well as recurring tasks, and applies even if the task appears harmless, read-only, useful for monitoring CI/CD or intended to spare the owner a handoff. Approval of a Trello card, PR, normal delivery, monitoring goal or deployment does **not** imply permission to use the Scheduled Tasks scheduler. Tool availability and generic product suggestions do not grant authorization.

For routine PR/CI/CD follow-through, poll within the active session when possible; otherwise leave an accurate handoff in the existing Trello card and GitHub PR. Do not secretly create a timer or Scheduled Task to resume later. Before an explicitly approved Scheduled Task operation, identify its exact task (or proposed new task), action, schedule/timezone and effects; follow the authorization policy and relevant operational contract. These restrictions apply to task-management actions, not to read-only inspection of an existing task registry when otherwise authorized.

## Risk-proportional operational safeguards

IPTV Watchlist is a single-owner service. Select safeguards by **blast radius, data authority, existing writers, reversibility, and recovery cost**, rather than by the blanket rule that every production write needs a full snapshot, multiple parity passes and a large restoration plan. A bounded failure that the existing pipeline can safely reprocess is not the same as irreversible loss. Do not add recovery ceremonies whose cost exceeds the plausible damage.

| Classification | Observable criteria | Proportionate checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lightweight, additive** | One specifically identified key is absent; only a new object is created, with no overwrite/delete or schema/authority change; the bytes and SHA-256 are approved; there is credible evidence of a single writer for this key or a safe conflict stop; the downstream consumer is idempotent or can re-project; rollback is a bounded delete or superseding correction | Target-specific GET/absence precheck, exact payload + digest and **one** owner approval for the action; one write (no blind retry); immediate GET/bytes or digest readback; one scoped downstream status/consistency check when that consumer next runs; documented bounded recovery. **No default full-bucket fenced snapshot, whole-database bookmark, global parity or duplicate planner rerun.** |
| **Elevated / uncertain** | The key already exists; competing writers cannot be excluded; a conditional write is unavailable and overwriting would be damaging; rollback or source-of-truth is unclear; or a failure can poison shared state | Stop and resolve uncertainty first. Use a conditional write/CAS or lease when supported, narrow before-image, ownership check and the smallest extra verification needed. If single-writer and reversibility cannot be established, use the high-impact procedure, not an optimistic unconditional PUT. |
| **High impact** | Existing authoritative data overwritten or deleted; bulk operation, schema migration, cutover, trust/access boundary, cross-domain references or irreversible side effects; recovery has material cost | Fencing/leases, before-image or scoped/full snapshot as justified, parity and multi-stage acceptance, rollback/restore rehearsals and explicit owner gates appropriate to the changed authority and blast radius. |

A **Cloudflare R2 REST API PUT without conditional semantics** is not made atomic by a preceding GET. For an approved single-key additive correction: verify the exact target returns `404` immediately before writing; confirm no application, Task, Worker or other operator writes that key; perform exactly one PUT of approved bytes, with no blind retry; read back and compare byte length and SHA-256; stop on an unexpected object, ambiguity or mismatch. The 404 check only narrows the race: if exclusive ownership cannot be demonstrated, prefer a conditional-capable writer or elevate the operation. After an ambiguous timeout, **read the target before deciding whether any retry is needed**. An unexpected result is not permission to overwrite.

A downstream projector that is already authoritative, repeatable and recoverable may be verified through its single scoped status/readback. Do not automatically require both a full planner rerun and a separate equivalent parity test when one directly proves the acceptance criterion. A full system restore can itself destroy unrelated newer writes; treat it as an exceptional recovery path, not a routine prerequisite for a tiny correction. Record exactly what was checked and what remains unverified.

**Policy-based authorization:** verified L0 observations and approved L1 delivery need no repetitive owner prompt. L2 production remediation requires an operation-specific approved standing contract with enforceable limits; this policy enables **no new L2 production writer**. L3 requires an owner-approved plan and its holdpoints, not a conversation for every safe substep. Existing stronger ADRs/runbooks/Access guards remain in force until specifically changed. Some GET routes write. See [authorization](authorization-and-escalation.md).

The [cross-system safeguard audit](risk-proportional-safeguard-audit.md) records concrete keep/simplify decisions and limits across runbooks. A documentation change does not silently change CLI contracts or accepted ADRs.

## Newly discovered risks: automatic disposition and exception-based escalation

Do not expand acceptance or block unrelated work because a local risk was found. Classify against approved scope and actual invariants:
- **Fix now within scope:** give actionable findings to the Executor; retest/re-review, or use an *existing* authorized L1/L2 recovery.
- **Defer nonblocking:** record evidence, linked atomic follow-up and clear revisit trigger without false prerequisites.
- **Stop affected action:** unknown or conflicting generation, lease, authority, identity or write receipt; do not blind-retry or silently waive acceptance.
- **Escalate owner decision:** changed product intent, material architecture/security trade-off, high-impact action outside a plan or unresolvable integrity issue. Present options, consequences and recommendation.

An isolated problematic record may be deferred only when explicit acceptance/invariants allow partial success. Continue other approved safe work. See [authorization](authorization-and-escalation.md).

## Delivery pipeline for already approved work

### Executor opens the PR and subscribes without asking

When an Executor finishes the work of an approved card, opening the pull request and subscribing to its events are part of that delivery and are done **by default, in the same turn**, without a confirmation question to the owner: push the working branch, open a PR against `main` (never a direct `main` commit) with a body that satisfies [documentation governance](documentation-governance.md#automated-pull-request-checks), then subscribe to its activity where a subscription tool exists and follow it to a terminal status ([AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md)). Asking "shall I open a PR?" after finishing delivers nothing and delays review. This does not widen authority: merge, Task changes, production writes and flag changes keep their existing gates, and a tool or platform default that says "do not open a PR unless asked" is satisfied by this standing owner instruction. The rule binds every executor identically, including Claude Code, and is repeated in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) so no executor can miss it by not following the link; an executor that stops after the push and asks the owner for permission is following it wrongly.

### Shared CI incident coordination across subscribed PRs

Executors subscribe to their own PR and branch CI. A single broken `main` can therefore notify many agents independently, each of whom may try to repair the same failure. **Coordinate the common failure through GitHub PR comments, not through a presumed single shared agent or a background listener.**

1. **Distinguish shared from local.** The agent who first notices a failed check compares the failing job, test name, error signature and commit SHA with the latest `main` run and other affected PRs. A matching failure on `main` or multiple unrelated branches is a shared-incident signal; confirm the cause where possible. Do not declare all red checks shared merely because `main` is red, or dismiss an independent failure in the agent's own branch.
2. **First confirmed coordinator takes the communication lead.** Before posting, check existing GitHub incident and PR comments for an active coordinator or canonical tracking link. If none exists, post a short, timestamped canonical incident comment on the originating PR (or an already established tracking issue), with the failing `main` SHA/run, diagnostic signature, incident status, and a link to the designated fix PR or work item. If the fix owner is not yet known, say **investigating / unassigned**, not **being fixed**. The first coordinator to establish this record owns the *notification round*, not automatic exclusive rights to implement a fix; agree on a single fix scope without cancelling other agents' approved work.
3. **Notify affected open PRs immediately.** Enumerate the *live* open PRs against `main`, including draft PRs when affected. Post one brief **English GitHub Conversation comment per affected PR**, linking the canonical incident, the failing check/signature and the fix owner/PR if known. State explicitly: **do not duplicate the shared fix in this branch; continue only your own scope; do not merge while the common required checks are red.** For a repository-wide required test, treat all open PRs inheriting that test as affected; for a targeted failure, notify only the evidenced set. Check for an existing notice first to avoid notification loops or duplicates. If a PR is inaccessible or commenting fails, record which recipients were **not** notified in the canonical incident and alert the owner/coordinator; do not claim full coverage.
4. **Subscribers respond without racing.** On any red CI notification, an Executor first inspects the current `main` status and recent PR comments before editing shared code or global tests. If the failure is already coordinated, acknowledge the canonical link when useful and refrain from creating another copy of the fix. Keep normal branch work moving where safe, but leave its own CI failures unresolved until diagnosed and do not merge on the strength of another branch's fix. A genuine branch-specific failure remains the branch Executor's responsibility.
5. **Close the communication loop.** The coordinator checks the merged fix SHA and required *post-merge* `main` CI/deploy outcomes. After `main` is green, add a resolution comment to every PR notified in step 3 with the fix PR/SHA, passing run URL and instructions to refresh against current `main` and rerun its own exact-head checks. Do not announce resolution from green PR CI alone. If the fix fails or the incident changes, update the canonical record and affected PRs instead of inventing a new coordinated fix. Record the outcome in the originating Trello card.

**Short notification template (fill actual links and evidence; never invent a fix owner):**

> Shared CI incident: [canonical link]. Required check [name] fails on `main` [SHA/run] with [signature]. Status: [investigating or fix PR/owner]. Please do not duplicate the shared fix in this branch. Continue your own scope where safe; do not merge until the shared checks recover. A follow-up here will provide the verified fix SHA and green `main` run.

This is a **best-effort coordination protocol**, not a guarantee against races: subscription events may be delivered before a coordinator can comment. It does not install an agent, grant new monitoring/scheduling capabilities, create an external Scheduled Task, or authorize any production remediation. If no actor is connected and available to inspect/comment, leave an accurate handoff instead of claiming that notifications were sent.

**Preventive merge check:** for a PR touching shared operators, migrations, global fixtures or tests, compare its assumptions against the *current* `main` just before merge, not only the earlier green branch HEAD. In particular reject fixed schema versions or migration counts asserted over a dynamically discovered full chain; if a concurrent merge changed the relevant integration surface, rerun the relevant test on the combined state or defer merge until that verification is available.

### Test-suite performance quality gate

**Delivery is blocked by functional correctness and safety, not by GitHub-runner performance jitter.** A failed core test, a failed build, a per-file hang (the runner's 300000 ms timeout) and the job timeout still block merge and deploy. A wall-clock, percentile or throughput threshold measured on a GitHub-hosted runner does not.

**Core shard test-speed quality threshold: 180 seconds (owner decision, 15 Nov 2026).** Measure each required GitHub Actions shard's `Run tests` step, excluding setup, queue, build and deploy. A result above 180 seconds is a non-blocking quality signal: inspect the slow files and runner variance, document the finding, and address a confirmed regression separately. The threshold is **not** an assertion, required check, merge blocker or deploy gate. Functional failures, timeouts and failed builds retain their existing blocking behavior. See [current threshold evidence](../evidence/ci/core-shard-180s-quality-threshold-2026-11-15.md) and [suite classification](../evidence/ci/air-490-nonfunctional-suite-2026-11-15.md) for historical context.

Two suites, one registry (`scripts/test-classification.mjs`, checked by `tests/test-classification.mjs`):

- **Core** (`npm test`, the default; the required jobs pass `--suite core`): result, contract, integrity, CAS/fence/replay, boundedness, call or SQL counts, timeout semantics, and a resource hard cap a safe deploy needs. A file name that contains budget, memory or mutation is not the classification. A bound that multiplies a count by a fixed latency model stays in core. Milliseconds that are only logged stay in core.
- **Non-functional** (`npm test -- --suite non-functional`, also `npm run test:nonfunctional`): real wall-clock, latency, p95, throughput, CPU or heap profiles, benchmarks, large stress experiments, and mutation testing (whether the functional tests kill a one-rule mutant). `npm test -- --suite all` and `npm run test:all` run core and then this suite.

A file that holds both is **split**. `nonFunctional(id, fn)` in `tests/suite-gate.mjs` runs `fn` only when `IPTV_TEST_SUITE=non-functional`, so the core path does not pay a pure speed workload and cannot fail on the runner clock. The nightly run repeats the functional assertions of a split file and also runs the gated checks. Wholly core files and wholly non-functional files are disjoint. Split files are the only overlap. Support libraries and the historical fixture files are registered and are not executed. A new `tests/*.mjs` file fails the registry test until it is named.

### Local pre-PR testing and Git hooks (AIR-514)

Before creating any code PR affecting `src/`, `scripts/`, `tests/`, `migrations/`, `public/`, `data/`, build/package configuration or workflows, the executor runs `npm ci`, `npm run test:all` and `npm run build` on the final checkout. Report the exact tested SHA and actual results in the PR body. Rerun the full suite after edits before review. Documentation-only PRs are exempt from the expensive non-functional suite.

Local `npm ci` configures tracked `.githooks/` through the `prepare` script. In `--ignore-scripts` environments run `npm run hooks:install` separately. The pre-commit hook checks exact-text mutation anchors; pre-push runs all tests for pushed executable changes and skips docs-only pushes. In the hook the non-functional measurement files (every non-functional file that is not a mutant-kill `*-mutation*.mjs` file) run one at a time after the parallel phase (`--isolate-measurements`): beside the mutation files on a busy laptop their wall-clock and latency bounds fail although each passes alone. CI and the nightly workflow do not pass the option. A `test:all` run that passes in full on an unchanged working tree records the tested Git tree locally, and the hook does not run the suite again for pushed commits with that content, or content that differs from it only in documentation-only paths (AIR-668, `scripts/test-pass-record.mjs`); `IPTV_PREPUSH_RERUN=1` forces a run. Without a covering record the hook runs the suite only when the working tree is the content of every pushed commit, and refuses the push otherwise (another branch, uncommitted executable edits). The pre-PR `test:all` therefore also serves the push instead of running twice. Never overwrite unrelated `core.hooksPath` settings. Do not use `--no-verify` to bypass a red hook merely to open a PR.

Hooks are not an enforcement boundary: Git `--no-verify` and GitHub API commits bypass them. Independent reviewers inspect actual pre-PR evidence. The required `Test and build` job adds a cheap mutation target preflight for producer driver/store harnesses without running any mutant workload. Existing stale targets from AIR-490 must be fixed in that workstream before accepting this PR; do not suppress the signal. A red nightly still does not block deploy directly.

The required PR and post-merge jobs stay `Test and build` (core shard 1 of 3, plus the build and the Discovery bundle check), `Test shard 2 of 3` and `Test shard 3 of 3`, and on `main` `Deploy to Cloudflare`. Deploy needs those three jobs (owner decision on [AIR-418](https://trello.com/c/5HEzNlvh), 10 Nov 2026: one runner's speed varied by a factor of 1.65 on the same code; three shards instead of two on 12 Nov 2026, [AIR-374 P6](../evidence/ci/test-suite-air-374-ci-rebalance-p6-2026-11-12.md)). It does not need the nightly workflow. Wall time of `Run tests` is a health metric in the job summary; compare it against 180-second quality threshold. Do not add a wall-clock, p95 or throughput assertion to those jobs, and do not drop a functional assertion to make a shard faster.

`.github/workflows/nonfunctional-tests.yml` runs on a daily schedule at 04:17 UTC (05:17 Europe/Madrid while the offset is UTC+1, 06:17 while it is UTC+2; neither is a fixed local hour) and on `workflow_dispatch`. It has no pull-request trigger, no secrets, no Cloudflare environment and no deploy. The nightly runs the suite with `--jobs 3` on Node.js 22 (required core CI stays on Node.js 20) ([AIR-490 evidence](../evidence/ci/air-490-nightly-jobs-3-2026-11-18.md)). A red nightly is a regression signal. It is not a pass, and it is not a branch-protection required check. `workflow_dispatch` of that file can run only after the file is on the default branch.

A new test is classified the same way: functional and safety checks stay in core; a runner-clock or mutant-kill check goes in the non-functional suite or inside `nonFunctional`. The registry test fails when the file is unclassified, when a known wall-clock assertion sits on a core line, or when a gated marker is also asserted on the core path.

## Feature flag lifecycle: plan, test, switch, remove

Every temporary feature flag must have an explicit end-to-end lifecycle **at the time it is first planned**:

1. **Create and plan.** Specify its owner, exact permitted values and defaults, consumers, data effects, rollback boundaries and the reason the flag is temporary. Create a separate, atomic Trello **flag-removal card immediately**, before implementation, link it from the introduction/rollout card, and include the specific cutover, stabilization and acceptance criteria that will unblock deletion. Do not defer creation of the removal card until after rollout.
2. **Implement and test.** Ship the gated change with tests for old/new modes, compatibility and failure paths; register the flag in [the maintained feature-flag contract](../reference/feature-flags.md). No assumption that a deployed flag is enabled.
3. **Switch and stabilize.** Execute shadow/rollout/cutover under an owner-approved L3 plan with its explicit holdpoints; bounded preflight, steps and readback need not generate redundant per-call approval prompts. Verify the actual production binding from authoritative Cloudflare settings or an MCP diagnostic that **explicitly exposes that exact effective setting**, then verify the relevant production behavior and complete the defined stabilization period. A code default, previous Trello comment, generic MCP probe, or successful deployment alone does not establish a live binding.
4. **Close the rollout with an explicit flag disposition.** Before marking the rollout card Done, record in its Trello comments one owner-approved disposition for **each introduced temporary flag** (reusing a decision recorded in the approved rollout plan): `retire now`, `retire after stabilization`, or `retain as permanent operational gate`. State the evidence, reason, rollback/operational consequences, and exact successor card. For either retirement outcome, the pre-created removal card **must contain the actual rollout/acceptance/stabilization card as a native Trello prerequisite** (one bare canonical Trello URL per item), not merely a descriptive sentence or a chat note. Verify live card IDs and status, reuse the existing prerequisite checklist, and mark the item checked only after that prerequisite's defined acceptance is truly Done. For `retire after stabilization`, link an existing stabilization acceptance card **only if one genuinely exists and its outcome is required**. Stabilization can instead be a bounded, evidenced check inside the removal card's readiness/acceptance, using already available routine production observations; do not invent a separate card, waiting period, or rollback exercise merely to populate a checklist. If a distinct unsafe operation or material independent delivery is actually required, obtain its owner gate and create a separate atomic task. Do not confuse a readiness card (which may only prepare activation) with proof that activation occurred. For `retire now`, the completed rollout card remains a linked, checked prerequisite and the removal card can proceed only after readiness preflight. A permanent gate requires an explicit safety/operational rationale, owner-approved disposition, and a clear follow-up/review trigger; do not silently omit an existing disposal ticket. **The cleanup card's own scope and prerequisites are live Trello data, never chat-only knowledge.**
5. **Remove and deconfigure.** When the new mode is accepted and the old mode is no longer needed, use the removal card to delete the selector, legacy branch and obsolete tests and update references/runbooks. After approved merge, both CI/CD jobs and safe runtime verification, the **final required acceptance step** for a retired Cloudflare Worker environment variable is to remove that exact production binding under the approved L3 cleanup plan and any named holdpoint, then independently read back its absence (authorized settings or an MCP diagnostic reporting the exact flag). Never silently remove bindings or assume deployment clears them (`keep_vars: true`). Record both the code retirement and verified binding removal; only then mark the cleanup card Done and Trello `mark_done`. If permission or access is unavailable, leave the cleanup card open with the precise remaining step. Retaining a permanent operational gate requires a separate explicit disposition, not deletion. Restore the old binding according to the documented rollback procedure if reverting to code that reads it. Preserve data, security gates and supported operational capabilities.

Distinguish a **transitional switch** from a **permanent owner-only safety gate**: an endpoint permitting sensitive one-off writes may need a durable gate rather than automatic removal. In that case the initial card must state the rationale and the removal/disposition ticket must explicitly decide whether to retire the endpoint, retain its gate, or replace it with a supported operational control. Never remove protections merely to satisfy a cleanup metric.

Before creating any cleanup card, search live Trello for an existing removal/disposition card using the flag's literal identifier and relevant migration terms; avoid duplicate tickets and duplicate prerequisite checklists. If the required rollback or migration remains incomplete, do not prematurely remove the flag. Review the register after each rollout and verify the cleanup card's existence and prerequisites.

## Read routes must not write

A new or changed GET, HEAD, page or MCP read tool must pass `tests/get-write-audit.mjs` ([read side effects](../reference/api/read-side-effects.md), [ADR 0107](../adr/0107-get-head-read-side-effect-boundary.md)): register a new prefix route or MCP tool in `tests/get-write-baseline.mjs`, with no effect. A reviewer rejects an entry added to the baseline for new work; a PR that fixes a baseline writer deletes the entry in the same PR and moves the write to its owner writer. The audit runs the production bundle against an instrumented R2 and D1; it never calls production, and a production read of a route the baseline lists is still L2/L3.

## Documentation acceptance

Before implementation, record the documentation impact in the Trello card and implementation handoff using the [documentation governance matrix](documentation-governance.md). At independent review, compare the actual diff with that matrix; require the matching source, contract, evidence, ADR and diagram changes in the same delivery. A claimed `Documentation impact: none` needs a reason that survives review. Missing required documentation is a blocking finding even when tests are green. Do not defer it to a future cleanup card.

Every material architectural change requires a new ADR that records alternatives, rationale, trade-offs, and supersession where applicable, plus corresponding Mermaid diagrams and technical contracts. Pure refactoring that preserves architectural decisions does not require a new ADR. Mermaid modifications retain the existing owner visual review gate.

## Visual QA

A substantial UI change requires visual QA in a local browser at a desktop width around 1280 px and a mobile width around 390 px. Check representative full and partial states, visual hierarchy, spacing, overflow, whether the controls can be reached, and the main interactions. The PR needs a short written report of what was checked and which problems were found or fixed. Creating, saving, downloading, or attaching screenshots is not required. The owner does the final visual acceptance on real production data after a successful merge and CI/CD. Until that acceptance, the UI card stays open. Green tests or CI are not visual QA. Do not commit screenshots anywhere in the repository.

## Safety and production boundaries

- Side-effect-free verified observation is L0 and needs no repeated owner approval; unknown GET/status endpoints are not presumed read-only.
- Manual production sync, ratings, predictions, discovery/TMDB, lease resets, migration, cutover, cleanup, feature flags, Scheduled Tasks and security operations are **not** authorized merely by code deployment or this docs-only policy. Use existing L2 contract or owner-approved L3 plan.
- This policy grants no new autonomous L2 production writes and no new Access permissions. Preserve accepted ADRs, CAS, leases, identity and source-of-truth guards until specifically changed.
- A code rollback does not roll back R2/D1 data, authority markers or external schedules. Unknown receipt does not prove no side effect. Protect confidential data and credentials.
