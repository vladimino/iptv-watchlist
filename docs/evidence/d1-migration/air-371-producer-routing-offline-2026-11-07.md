# AIR-371: offline evidence of the canonical Film/Person producer routing

**Captured:** 2026-11-07 (UTC). **Method:** offline tests on a D1 stand-in over the sqlite3 CLI (`tests/canonical-producers.mjs`), the full `npm test` and `npm run build`. No production read or write, no schema apply, no flag, no R2 access. Nothing here measures production behavior, latency, row counts or parity.

## What was verified

- **Marker matrix.** `writerRoute` and `dispatch` over legacy (schema 12), `r2`, `prepared`, `open`, `held` and unreadable markers (missing row, read error): legacy, `r2` and `prepared` give the projector as the only writer and `dispatch` opens no batch and writes no row; `open` gives the adapters as the only writers and the projector compare-only; `held` throws `film_person_held` and every unreadable state throws `film_person_unavailable`, with no fallback to R2.
- **No overlap, no gap, no writer without a generation match.** A projector batch carrying `projectorFence` passes at `r2` and `prepared`; a batch built at `prepared` generation 1 and overtaken by a move to `open` before it runs fails whole and writes nothing; a stale gate fails after the move; a generation change inside `prepared` (back to `r2` and forward) also kills a batch built on the old generation. The canonical adapters refuse before the move and write after it. At no step do both writers succeed (asserted per state). A fresh `open` gate cannot build a projector fence.
- **EPG identity.** Credits are upserts only (no removal, no renumbering): a D1-only TMDB credit and its ordinal survive and the roster credit is appended; `tmdb` and `tmdb_tv` with one number coexist; a stale revision is a `conflict`; an invalid plan is refused; plans from different EPG generations are not batched.
- **Discovery admission.** Only a verified `tmdb_<n>` key; `tmdb_tv_*`, a short code, an unverified entry, a held typed claim and a redirect are refused; two concurrent admissions apply once and the other is a `conflict` (one Film); a different Film claiming the same trusted movie id is refused, no phantom or duplicate Film.
- **Rated admission and retention.** A candidate, a short-code key, a `watchlist` field, a missing verification fact and a bad digest are never Films; replay is a no-op; watched then removed keeps the Film and writes no membership; a monotonic observation time decides.
- **TMDB retirement.** Retire, a second retire refused, and an explicit restore appended (history complete and append-only); a decision without the owner's confirmation is refused before any SQL.
- **Localization.** A late automatic source never displaces an owner value (the effective value stays the owner's); reviewed, dictionary-sourced and empty values never become source writes.
- **Unknown outcomes (review fix).** A batch that committed but whose answer was lost, and one that never reached D1, are reported as `unknown` and nothing after them runs. `readback` never concludes "applied" from the revision alone: with an unchanged revision the call is `not_applied` (the atomic batch left nothing, the replay applies once); with a moved revision it checks that D1 holds the call's content (`content_present`) or says `reread_and_replan` (a competing write on the same Film is not proof of the lost batch; tested with a concurrent unrelated revision bump and with two distinct EPG payloads on one Film). Calls that dedupe by request id report `settle_by_request_id` and `mutateFilm`/`mutatePerson`, which carry no request id, never claim request-id replay safety. Every advertised call kind reconciles (localization, an `admitRatedFilm` without `filmId`) or is `unsupported`. A lost fence or revision is a `conflict`.
- **Exact content check (second review fix).** `content_present` requires exact equivalence of every write effect of the call, not row existence: the Film or Person row fields it sets (contract version, role id, canonical identity and original title when given), each external id with its trust, conflict flag and provenance, each removal (the row must be absent), each alias, each upserted credit (present) and each credit removal (absent). Tested after an unrelated same-entity revision bump: a differing `originalTitle`, canonical identity, contract version or Person role id, an untrusted or conflicting row where a trusted one was wanted (each flag alone), different provenance, a removal not performed, a credit removal not performed and an upserted credit that is absent are all `reread_and_replan`; the exact states are `content_present`. Mutation checks on each branch fail the test. A replay after `reread_and_replan` goes through the revision check and is not claimed to be neutral: an external id row is re-written with the call's trust and provenance, so a conflicting trust decision found by the re-read is the caller's to resolve, not overwritten to make the replay pass.
- **Dependency-safe EPG plan (review fix).** The plan creates the Persons first, then the Film with its credits; a credit whose Person is neither in the plan nor declared known is refused (`credit_person_unresolved`). A previously unseen Person referenced by a new Film's credit applies; a later-step failure is reported as `partial` (the plan is not one atomic batch, `atomic:false`); after `readRevisions` and a re-plan the retry converges with no duplicate credit.
- **Shadow and readiness.** The shadow is read-only, bounded at 200 Films per call and 200 listed rows, treats a D1-only credit as no difference and reports missing, differing and truncated input. `handoffReadiness` is read-only and stays not ready: with a prepared marker, a verified baseline and schema 16 the only blockers left are the uncovered producer writes (`GAPS`); `r2` and `held` markers, a live projector lease and an unreadable marker add their own blockers.
- **Dormant.** No source file imports the module, the build and package files do not name it, it has no R2 or network reference and the projector, repository, projection and Worker files are unchanged.

Mutation checks (each removal makes the test fail): the routing of `r2`/`prepared`, the canonical gate in `dispatch`, the generation term of the projector fence, the verified-only and held checks of the planners, the stop after an unknown outcome, the shadow input cap, the readiness gaps and the mixed-generation check. One equivalent mutant survives: adding `d1` to the modes the projector fence accepts changes nothing, because the generation always differs once the marker is `d1` (defense in depth, documented).

## Not covered, and why this is a blocker for AIR-374

No fenced mutation exists for `film_metadata`, `film_dimension`, `film_sync_state`, `watchlist_film` membership, `person_dimension`, `localization_conflict` outside a merge, the overwrite of `canonical_identity` and `original_title`, and the projector does not move `canonical_revision`. Making the adapters the only writers at `open` while these writes stay with the projector would leave them unwritten, so `handoffReadiness` reports not ready. They need their own fenced writers and an owner decision on the revision semantics before AIR-374; this card does not invent a new authority.
